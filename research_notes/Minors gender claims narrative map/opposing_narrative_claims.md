## 1. Which of these claims have no support, which overstate a real basis, and which are substantially accurate?

### Takeaway
Of 18 claims traced, none is pure invention: every one rests on a documented kernel, but most of the strongest wordings outrun it. The absolute denials ("no school hides anything", "no minors get surgery") are contradicted by records; the predictive harm claims (notification "will result in" homelessness, violence, suicide; care is "life-saving"; blockers "fully reversible"; regret "1%") are real-basis-but-overstated or unverified; the narrow factual claims (schools do not medicate or operate; surgery on minors is rare; Texas did open child-abuse investigations of parents; threats followed viral posts) are substantially accurate.

#### C1. "No school is hiding anything from parents" / "this never happens" / "manufactured panic"

Gavin Newsom on his podcast, 2025-03-06, answering Charlie Kirk's claim that AB 1955 stops districts telling parents: "No, they can. They just can't get fired for not doing that." — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/nbc-out/out-politics-and-policy/california-gov-gavin-newsom-breaks-democrats-trans-sports-participatio-rcna195165) [msm]; (d) California's 2026 suit against the U.S. Education Department "says the federal department presented no evidence that any parental request to view their child's gender support plan has been denied" — [Education Week, 2026-09](https://www.edweek.org/policy-politics/trump-admin-turns-to-student-privacy-laws-in-gender-identity-fights-with-schools/2026/09) [msm].
- **A careful version from this side.** Ari Drennen (trans-rights writer; a Media Matters affiliation is recalled but was not verified in this run), 2023-11-24: "it is not possible for transgender minors to access hormone therapy without the consent of at least one of their parents ... But there are schools where teachers will not automatically out a student to their parents, and there's a really good reason for that." — [Ask Ari, Substack](https://aridrennen.substack.com/p/are-teachers-really-transitioning) [adv-L]. This concedes nondisclosure and denies only the medical claim.

The Skaneateles (N.Y.) Gender Support Plan directed the legal name with the mother (catalog SOC-US-006, corroborated account) — [N.D.N.Y. order](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-nynd-5_24-cv-00155/pdf/USCOURTS-nynd-5_24-cv-00155-0.pdf) [court]. The U.S. Education Department's FERPA finding of 2026-01-28 cites district forms, training statements and reports of "at least 300" gender support plans (catalog POL-012) — [SPPO letter](https://californiapolicycenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/DOE.FERPAFindings.1.28.26.pdf) [gov]; [The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/ed-dept-says-california-violated-law-by-concealing-students-gender-identity/) [msm]. FERPA scholars quoted say no specific denial of records was identified (same catalog entry).
- **The record against the absolute denial (incidents).** In Escondido a counselor emailed a teacher a list of seven transgender-identifying students, "for six of whom their parents were unaware" (catalog SOC-CA-007; sworn but contested, teacher's account via plaintiffs' brief) — [Answering Brief, pp. 7–8](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/63d954d4e4ad424df7819d46/6a905f0d4fa78dfe6c216b9b_260826-Mirabelli.pdf) [court filing]. The Poe family's declarations describe staff saying "no" when asked and charter teachers continuing a male name over written instruction (SOC-CA-006; sworn but contested; neither school is a party) — [MSJ order, pp. 18–20, 33–34](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/63d954d4e4ad424df7819d46/6949e11ea6bae817c8eaf637_Dkt%20%20307_MSJ%20Order.pdf) [court]. Kettle Moraine (Wis.) said it "will follow the guidance of A.F. in what pronouns to use, even over parental objections" (SOC-US-003; established by records; the parents knew, so it is a continuation-over-objection case, not a concealment case) — [Decision and Order](https://assets.adflegal.org/archive/adfm/TF-Decision.pdf) [court]. Delaware Valley (N.J.): the board's counsel wrote that the district "would continue" after the father refused consent (SOC-US-007; established by records) — [D.N.J. opinion](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-njd-3_24-cv-00107/pdf/USCOURTS-njd-3_24-cv-00107-2.pdf) [court].

No case located shows a school arranging medication or surgery (catalog section 0); when CNN asked the White House in October 2026 for cases behind the school-surgery claim it provided none — [CNN via KEYT, 2026-10-20](https://keyt.com/politics/cnn-us-politics/2026/10/20/fact-check-trump-brings-back-his-2024-lie-that-schools-are-secretly-sending-kids-to-hospitals-for-gender-surgery/) [fc].
- **Critiques and fact-checks.** Jesse Singal, 2023-01-23, on Katie Baker's New York Times report: the nondisclosure policy "has been enacted at schools across the country (Baker doesn't provide any stats about how common this practice is, and I don't think any exist)" and living one identity at school and another at home "just can't be psychologically healthy" — [Singal-Minded](https://jessesingal.substack.com/p/on-teachers-letting-kids-transition) [het]. #### C2. "Parental notification = forced outing" and the harm/suicide claims

- **Strongest wording.** California Legislative LGBTQ Caucus, 2026-03-03: "For LGBTQ+ youth, forced outing is not a theoretical concern – it will result in discrimination, rejection, homelessness, and even violence." — [Caucus release](https://lgbtqcaucus.legislature.ca.gov/node/219). Attorney General Rob Bonta, 2023-08-28: "The forced outing policy wrongfully endangers the physical, mental, and emotional well-being of non-conforming students who lack an accepting environment in the classroom and at home"; the release says students "face the risk of emotional, physical, and psychological harm" — [California OAG](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-announces-lawsuit-challenging-chino-valley-unified-school) [gov]. NCLR legal director Shannon Minter, 2026-03-02: the Court "has effectively greenlit the forced outing of transgender students to parents — even where the evidentiary record shows that disclosure threatens significant psychological, emotional, and physical harm" — [NCLR](https://www.nclrights.org/about-us/press-release/supreme-court-blocks-california-law-protecting-transgender-students/) [adv-L]. Asm. Chris Ward's AB 1955 signing release, 2024-07-15, twice calls it "life-saving legislation" and says "Despite homes not always being safe for transgender youth, schools should be a sanctuary" — [Asm. Ward release](https://a78.asmdc.org/press-releases/20240715-new-safety-act-signed-law-protect-lgbtq-students-california) [gov].
- **The statistics used.** Bonta's release: "In the 2015 U.S. Transgender Survey, 10% of respondents said that an immediate family member had been violent toward them because they were transgender, and 15% ran away from home or were kicked out of their home because they were transgender. And fewer than one-in-three transgender and gender nonbinary youth found their home to be gender-affirming." — [California OAG](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-announces-lawsuit-challenging-chino-valley-unified-school). The third figure is unsourced in the release; its wording matches The Trevor Project's 2022 survey ("Fewer than 1 in 3 transgender and nonbinary youth found their home to be gender-affirming") — [Trevor Project 2022 survey](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/survey-2022/) [adv-L]. The Ward release cites "less than 40% of transgender and nonbinary youth find their homes to be LGBTQ-affirming" (2024 Trevor survey). Drennen cites "29% of LGBTQ people report experiencing abuse from family members, including 43% of trans people" — [Ask Ari](https://aridrennen.substack.com/p/are-teachers-really-transitioning).
- **What the underlying surveys measure.** Trevor Project 2024: "final analytic sample of 18,663 LGBTQ+ young people ages 13 to 24", "recruited via targeted ads on social media", "A quantitative cross-sectional design", data collected "between September 13 and December 16, 2023"; 40% of LGBTQ+ respondents and 38% of transgender and nonbinary respondents listed home as an affirming space; past-year suicide attempts were 16% among trans and nonbinary youth in homes that were not gender-affirming versus 12% in gender-affirming homes; the page does not describe the sample as non-probability and lists no limitations — [Trevor Project 2024 survey](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/survey-2024/). "Not affirming" is a respondent's rating of a space; it is not a measure of abuse or danger, and the survey does not ask about school-initiated disclosure.
- **"One accepting adult" statistic.** Trevor Project research brief (2019): "LGBTQ youth who report having at least one accepting adult were 40% less likely to report a suicide attempt in the past year" (OR = .60); cross-sectional online survey, February–September 2018, analytic subsample 20,202; the adults asked about were "parent, family member other than a parent or sibling, teacher or guidance counselor, and doctor or other healthcare provider" — [Trevor Project brief](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/research-briefs/accepting-adults-reduce-suicide-attempts-among-lgbtq-youth/). The accepting adult can be a parent; the statistic does not compare disclosure with nondisclosure.
- **The one study on being outed.** University of Connecticut researchers (McCauley et al., 2024) used a 2017 survey of LGBTQ youth ages 13–17 collected with the Human Rights Campaign; about 30% said they had been outed to family; those outed reported more depressive symptoms and lower family support; 69% called the experience extremely stressful — [The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/report-higher-rates-of-depression-anxiety-for-lgbtq-teens-forcibly-outed/) [education news nonprofit]. As reported, the study concerns outing to parents by anyone, uses 2017 cross-sectional data, and does not evaluate school notification policies. The 74 also reports that "44% of LGBTQ youth say they have not reported harassment to an adult at school out of fear their parents would learn their identity."
- **Evidence after notification policies took effect.** None found. The Trevor Project's own August 2024 brief says of forced-outing and facility policies: "Many of these policies were only recently implemented and their impact cannot yet be measured" — [Trevor Project, Anti-LGBTQ+ School Policies brief](https://thetrevorproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Anti-LGBTQ-School-Policies-and-LGBTQ-Young-People.pdf). The Williams Institute counts eight states with a law "requiring parental notification when students request to be addressed with a different pronoun," covering an estimated 112,100 transgender youth aged 13–17 — [Williams Institute, Jan. 2026](https://williamsinstitute.law.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025-Anti-Trans-Legislation-Jan-2026.pdf) [academic, LGBTQ-policy think tank]. No outcome comparison for those states was located.
- **Homelessness data and limits.** Chapin Hall's Voices of Youth Count found LGBTQ youth "at more than double the risk of homelessness compared to non-LGBTQ peers" and also that "Homelessness stems from multiple factors beyond 'coming out' among LGBTQ youth" — [Chapin Hall](https://www.chapinhall.org/research/lgbtq-young-adults-experience-homelessness-at-more-than-twice-the-rate-of-peers/) [academic].
- **Suicide-messaging caution.** England's national suicide-prevention adviser wrote in 2024 that "The evidence on suicide risk in children and young people with gender dysphoria is generally poor. Most studies are methodologically weak, being based on online surveys and self-selected samples and coming from biased sources," while "there are good reasons to believe that their risk is high compared to other young people"; guidance is to "avoid the impression that suicide is the expected or likely outcome in certain situations" — [Appleby review, GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/review-of-suicides-and-gender-dysphoria-at-the-tavistock-and-portman-nhs-foundation-trust/review-of-suicides-and-gender-dysphoria-at-the-tavistock-and-portman-nhs-foundation-trust-independent-report) [gov, UK].
- **What "forced outing" covers.** The Chino Valley policy required notice within three days of a name, pronoun or facilities request (catalog POL-009), which fits the term — [2024 ruling](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/attachments/press-docs/Chino.Injunction.Decision.9.9.24.pdf) [court]. The *Mirabelli* injunction, by its text, bars misleading parents and overriding a communicated objection and "does not in terms impose an affirmative duty on every school to notify" (catalog POL-010) — [Supreme Court opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25a810_b97d.pdf) [court]. The Advocate itself wrote that "Critics have labeled it 'forced outing'" — [The Advocate, 2026-03-02](https://www.advocate.com/news/education/supreme-court-forced-outing-transgender) [adv-L].
- **Kernel.** Some LGBTQ youth report family violence or being put out; being outed correlates with distress in one survey. **Distortion.** Survey correlation becomes causation; "home not rated affirming" becomes "home unsafe"; a possible worst case becomes "will result in"; one label covers both mandatory-notice rules and rules against lying when a parent asks. **Labels.** Family-rejection data: corroborated (survey-based, non-probability samples). Viral wording: real basis, materially overstated; the outcome prediction is unverified.

#### C3. "Anti-trans laws increase suicide attempts by up to 72%" (added)

- **Wording and origin.** Lee et al., *Nature Human Behaviour* (2024), by Trevor Project researchers: "Enacting state-level anti-transgender laws increased incidents of past-year suicide attempts among TGNB young people by 7–72%"; difference-in-differences on Trevor survey respondents (n = 61,240 aged 13–24; 35,196 aged 13–17) — [Nature Human Behaviour](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01979-5) [med]. The Trevor Project's page on the study carries the statement: "It is without question that anti-transgender policies, and the dangerous rhetoric surrounding them, take a measurable toll" — [Trevor Project brief](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/research-briefs/state-level-anti-transgender-laws-increase-past-year-suicide-attempts-among-transgender-and-non-binary-young-people-in-the-usa/). A co-author told CNN "this is the first time any study has shown this causal relationship" (as quoted by City Journal).
- **Critique.** The journal page now carries the notice "Matters Arising to this article was published on 28 May 2026," with a reply by the authors titled "Reply to: Methodological considerations for evaluating policy impacts on transgender and non-binary youth suicidality" — [Nature Human Behaviour](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01979-5); [Matters Arising DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-026-02467-8). City Journal (2026-06-16) says the critique shows "the observed elevation in suicide attempts came from a small sample (roughly 100 youth) in a single state (Idaho), at a time when that state's 'anti-transgender' laws were not even in effect," and that the authors replied that the criticisms "do not alter the interpretation of our findings" — [City Journal](https://www.city-journal.org/article/nature-human-behavior-transgender-suicide-study) [adv-R]. The Matters Arising text and the reply were not read directly (paywalled); the Idaho point rests on City Journal's account.
- **Label.** Real basis (a peer-reviewed study exists), materially overstated when the upper bound is quoted as the effect; status contested.

#### C4. Nex Benedict (Owasso, Oklahoma, February 2024)

## 2. What recurring distortion moves appear on this side, and how do they mirror the moves on the other side?

### Takeaway
Six moves recur: survey correlation presented as causation; "rare" hardened into "never"; privacy framing or policy silence presented as "no concealment"; worst-case readings of a law or order presented as its settled effect; institutional endorsement presented as evidentiary certainty; and population counts presented as people harmed. Each has a counterpart on the other side, usually the same move run in the opposite direction.

### Cited Findings
- **Move 1: correlation → causation, and non-probability survey → population fact.** Example: "more than half of transgender and nonbinary young people reported that their schools are gender-affirming, which correlates with lower suicide attempt rates" is used to call AB 1955 "life-saving legislation" — [Asm. Ward release](https://a78.asmdc.org/press-releases/20240715-new-safety-act-signed-law-protect-lgbtq-students-california). The survey is cross-sectional and recruited through social-media ads — [Trevor Project 2024](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/survey-2024/). Lee et al. made an explicit causal claim that drew a published methodological critique — [Nature Human Behaviour](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01979-5). Mirror on the other side: individual lawsuits and a parent's belief are generalized into "schools are transitioning children" (catalog: 33 of 51 entries are reported allegations).
- **Move 2: "rare" → "never."** Hedged primary wording ("typically do not," "rare exceptions," "almost never") — [HRC](https://www.hrc.org/resources/get-the-facts-on-gender-affirming-care) — coexists with claims data showing hundreds of chest surgeries on minors — [FactCheck.org](https://www.factcheck.org/2023/05/scicheck-young-children-do-not-receive-medical-gender-transition-treatment/). The movement's own guide warns against this move: "citing specific ages or including words like 'always' and 'never' in your messages makes them easy for anti-LGBTQ opponents to attack" — [MAP guide](https://mapresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/talking-about-transgender-youth-health-care-1.pdf). Mirror: "it happened in some places" → "children leave for school and come home after surgery" (the claim PolitiFact and CNN rated false; catalog section 0).
- **Move 3: policy silence or privacy framing → "no concealment."** "Privacy rights" recast "as government secrecy" — [LGBTQ Caucus](https://lgbtqcaucus.legislature.ca.gov/node/219) — against a state FAQ that told schools to respect limits on disclosure "including not sharing that information with the student's parents" — [Education Week](https://www.edweek.org/policy-politics/supreme-court-backs-parents-in-school-gender-disclosure-fight/2026/03). Mirror: a policy that permits nondisclosure → "the school lied to me," which discovery negated in *Willey* (SOC-US-008).
- **Move 4: worst-case reading → settled effect.** "Okays forced outing"; "could mandate forced outing of trans youth across America" — [The Advocate](https://www.advocate.com/news/education/supreme-court-forced-outing-transgender); [Erin in the Morning](https://www.erininthemorning.com/p/the-supreme-court-just-handed-down) — against an order limited to objecting parents — [opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25a810_b97d.pdf). "Florida will ... kidnap trans kids" against a custody-jurisdiction rule — [PolitiFact](https://www.politifact.com/article/2023/may/04/no-florida-cant-kidnap-trans-kids-under-proposed-l/). Mirror: House Republicans said California's AB 665 would let counselors "strip custody from parents" and AB 957 would charge non-affirming parents with child abuse; Third Way's memo says "The bill makes no changes to custody laws" and "Parents would not be charged with child abuse" — [Third Way](https://www.thirdway.org/memo/correcting-gop-lies-about-blue-state-bills-on-transgender-youth) [center-left think tank].
- **Move 5: endorsement → evidence ("every major medical association" → "settled").** [HRC](https://www.hrc.org/resources/get-the-facts-on-gender-affirming-care) and [MAP guide](https://mapresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/talking-about-transgender-youth-health-care-1.pdf) versus low-certainty findings — [ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/documents/health-policy/positions/2026-gender-surgery-children-adolescents.pdf). Mirror: one body's caution → "the science is settled against it"; Assigned Media quotes a Washington Examiner editor writing that Cass "proves ... 'gender-affirming care' is an evidence-free, failed approach that yields overwhelmingly harmful outcomes," which goes beyond Cass's "weak evidence" finding — [Assigned Media](https://assignedmedia.org/breaking-news/cass-report-misinformation-right-wing) [adv-L]. STAT notes conservatives read the AMA's surgery comment as a reversal though it "does not appear to have changed any of its policies" — [STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2026/02/10/medical-societies-trans-care-minors-ama-asps/).
- **Move 6: population or bill counts → people harmed or laws passed.** "146,300 ... have lost or are at risk of losing access" — [Williams Institute](https://williamsinstitute.law.ucla.edu/publications/bans-trans-youth-health-care/); "1,014 anti-transgender bills" — [Williams Institute, Jan. 2026](https://williamsinstitute.law.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025-Anti-Trans-Legislation-Jan-2026.pdf). Mirror: counts of districts with nondisclosure policies presented as counts of children "secretly transitioned" (catalog SOC-CA-002, the disputed "23 students").
- **Move 7: one label for unlike things.** "Forced outing" covers a three-day mandatory-notice rule and an injunction against lying to a parent who asks (C2, C13). Mirror: "secret transition" covers non-notification, exclusion from a meeting, a false answer, and continuation over objection, which the catalog's conclusion separates.
- **Move 8: tragedy before facts.** "Beaten to death" circulated before the examiner ruled (C4). Mirror: the catalog's medical-question candidates in which a parent "believes" treatment occurred (MED-006).
- **Move 9: villain attribution in place of engagement.** The TLC/ASO guide instructs: "Name the villains ... expose their motivation of getting back into or holding onto power" and advises against repeating opposition claims even to refute them — [guide](https://www.nea.org/sites/default/files/2025-03/advancing-lgbtq-inclusion-and-belonging-handout-2-rcgn-messaging-guide-trans-youth-the-freedom-to-be-ourselves-handout-1.pdf). Mirror: "groomer" and "mutilation" language; the RNC told PolitiFact Democrats are "defending child gender mutilation" — [PolitiFact, 2026-08-28](https://www.politifact.com/article/2026/aug/28/why-republicans-are-focusing-on-transgender-issues-in-competitive-2026-campaigns/) [fc].

### Inferences
- The mirror is close to exact on Moves 2, 3 and 4: each side takes the other's documented kernel (policies exist; surgery is rare) and converts it into its own absolute. A report that states each kernel with its number and its limit removes most of the disagreement about facts and leaves the real dispute, which is about values: who decides, and at what age.
- Move 9 helps explain why this side's absolute denials are hard to find in quotable form: the published guidance is to avoid restating or rebutting the opposing claim at all. That link between the guidance and the observed pattern is inference.

### Gaps
- No systematic content analysis was done; examples are illustrative and were chosen because they could be quoted and sourced.
- The other side's examples are drawn from the catalog and a few fact-checks, not from the companion narrative track, which the writer should use for the full mirror.

## 4. What do published messaging guides reveal about strategy, in their own words, and what documented drivers sit behind this side's messaging?

### Takeaway
The guides are candid: they tell advocates to lead with shared values and family, to keep out of medical detail, to avoid "always" and "never," not to repeat or rebut opposition claims, to name "certain politicians" as villains motivated by power, and (in the Transgender Law Center guide) not to lead with harm at all because it lowers trust in trans youth. Much of the public messaging traced in section 1 does the opposite of that last advice. Documented drivers are revenue that rose with the legislative fight and fell after 2024, a small set of disclosed funders, legal-compliance incentives on schools, an electoral strategy that shifted in 2026, and real grievances (investigations, threats, clinic closures).

### Cited Findings

#### Guide 1: Movement Advancement Project, "Talking About Transgender Youth Health Care" (2023; 2025 edition, published Aug.–Sept. 2025)
- Partners named on the landing page: Advocates for Trans Equality, Equality Federation Institute, GLAD Law, GLAAD, National Center for LGBTQ Rights, PFLAG National, The Trevor Project — [MAP landing page](https://www.mapresearch.org/talking-about-transgender-youth).
- Audience: "conversations that can help conflicted audiences."
- Core frame: "Decisions about this care ... should rest with families, doctors, and the patient—not politicians." "Those pushing these bans are trying to overrule families ... which is why emphasizing the role of families is so critical."
- On harms: "Don't just focus on the negative; always pair discussions about the damage caused by these bans with an emphasis on how this care improves the lives of transgender youth. Narrowly focusing on the harms of banning the care can inadvertently suggest that the lives of transgender youth are defined by those harms."
- On medical detail: "Leave in-depth medical conversations to physicians and providers ... If this starts to happen, return to a focus on how health care bans hurt transgender youth and their families; for example: 'I'm not a doctor, but what I know is that the government should not be overruling parents.'"
- What to avoid: "Avoid sweeping or categorical statements about complex aspects of transgender youth medical care. Care for transgender people is individualized, and different stages of care might happen at different ages, so citing specific ages or including words like 'always' and 'never' in your messages makes them easy for anti-LGBTQ opponents to attack."
- Stock answers when questioned: "All the leading medical associations in the U.S. ... support this care. Decades of peer-reviewed research show that it is safe, effective, and essential"; "The decision to seek care is not something a transgender young person and their family are able to rush into."
- On penalties: "check with legal experts to ensure that the sanctions are accurate based on the specific ban."
- All quotations: [MAP guide PDF](https://mapresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/talking-about-transgender-youth-health-care-1.pdf).

#### Guide 2: MAP, "Talking About Family Acceptance & Transgender Youth" (2019; 2024 edition)
- "Research shows that when transgender youth are loved and supported by their families, they are able to thrive. But when parents reject a transgender child or try to force them to reject the gender their child knows themselves to be, it can contribute to depression, suicidal behavior and other negative health outcomes."
- First approach: "Acknowledge and address feelings of unfamiliarity"; parents "will be better able to open up if they feel understood and that they can have an honest conversation with someone who won't attack or judge them."
- The guide reproduces a video script from "Advancing Acceptance," a 2019 campaign for which "MAP teamed with the Biden Foundation." 
- All from: [MAP family-acceptance guide](https://www.mapresearch.org/file/talking-about-family-acceptance-transgender-youth.pdf).
- Note for the writer: this guide treats parents as the persuadable audience and the family as the protective setting, which sits uneasily with "forced outing" messaging that presents parents as the risk.

#### Guide 3: ASO Communications, Transgender Law Center and Lake Research Partners, "Transgender Youth and the Freedom to Be Ourselves: Building Our Choir with a Race Class Gender Narrative" (2021; updated April 2022)
- Research base: online survey of 1,000 adults with oversamples (July–August 2021), ten focus groups, and a February 2022 voter survey.
- Do not rebut: "advocates have an unfortunate habit of repeating (and retweeting) their offensive claims with the simple addition of a 'not' ... these refutations reinforce the opposition's narrative ... With a slogan like 'supporting trans youth is not child abuse,' for example, people are more likely to recall a vague association between 'trans youth' and 'child abuse.'"
- Architecture: "Name the villains who violate our values, expose their motivation of getting back into or holding onto power"; "Call out how the villains: a. exploit divisions across races, and b. exploit the lack of familiarity with transgender people."
- Say "Certain politicians..." instead of "Our opposition is racist/transphobic," because "Our target audiences know that transphobia is widespread — and often themselves feel unsure about transgender people — so they do not buy statements attributing fear and division to certain politicians."
- Do not lead with harm: say "Respect each other for who we are, transgender or not" instead of "Protect vulnerable transgender kids"; avoid "Transgender kids face crushing bullying and discrimination, which threatens their physical and mental health," because "Focusing on the unique harms that transgender people face ... further distances our audiences ... at best eliciting pity. It also decreases our base and even activists' trust in transgender young people to know what is best for their own health and well-being."
- Language engineering: "pluralizing and personalizing genders — making statements about 'genders' or 'our genders' ... subtly helps orient people towards our worldview."
- Candid weaknesses: on sports, "Our base and persuadables want to support transgender student-athletes, but are extremely susceptible to our opposition's argument"; "white people were the only racial group to rate the opposition message we tested above all of our side's messages."
- Parental-rights framing is named as an attack to withstand: "our opposition fearmongers around transgender youth, 'parental rights,' and Critical Race Theory."
- All from: [guide, NEA-hosted copy](https://www.nea.org/sites/default/files/2025-03/advancing-lgbtq-inclusion-and-belonging-handout-2-rcgn-messaging-guide-trans-youth-the-freedom-to-be-ourselves-handout-1.pdf). The Transgender Law Center's own download page was not confirmed.

#### Guide 4: ACLU, "Your Guide to Talking About Attacks on Trans Youth" (2022-02-17)
- "Misinformation is fueling an attack on trans youth"; "When a young person's name and pronouns are respected, they do better in school, have more confidence, and have lower rates of suicide"; "Trans youth know who they are"; "These bills are ultimately about stopping trans youth from experiencing joy" — [ACLU](https://www.aclu.org/news/lgbtq-rights/your-guide-to-talking-about-attacks-on-trans-youth). ### Inferences
- The guides show a deliberate choice to argue from values and authority ("leading medical associations") and to stay out of the evidence, which is where the systematic reviews have gone against the strongest claims. That reading is inference; the guides give persuasion effectiveness, not evidentiary weakness, as their reason.
