## Key question 1: What is the typical lifecycle and time lag from a local incident or bill to national circulation?

### Takeaway
The documented path has four recurring steps: (1) a local artifact (a teacher's own video, a school-board comment clip, a records-request document, a bill's passage, an advocacy article), (2) repackaging by an aggregator or advocacy outlet with a compressed headline, (3) pickup by national partisan outlets and by an elected official or candidate, and (4) institutional response (threats, discipline, policy change, lawsuit, legislation), which becomes the next story. Measured lags are short: hours to three days from aggregator post to official amplification or threats, three days to about three weeks from a bill or event to national circulation, and fact-checks trail the first viral post by roughly two to eight days; the fully false "litter box" claim took about one month to go national and ten months to peak.

### Inferences
- Observed lags, compiled from the cases above: aggregator post to official amplification, about 1 day (Union, Aug. 21 to 22); aggregator post to threats, hours to "several days" (NBC average); bill passage to national viral claim, 0–3 days (SB 5599, AB 957); event to national outlet through a Substack scoop, about 3 weeks, then under 1 day from Substack to Fox; advocacy article to national partisan site, 3 days (Elk Grove); claim to first fact-check, about 2–8 days (SB 5599, Trump, AB 957); local hoax to national circulation, about 1 month, with a peak about 10 months later in campaign season.
- Each step shortens the text. The Elk Grove article's own qualifiers and the SB 5599 bill's reunification duty are present at the source and absent from the headline that travels; in the Trump case supporters themselves described the gap between the literal claim and the notification grievance behind it.
- The "strong" version (surgery, removal of children) appears at the national-politician and national-site stage, not at the local stage: local artifacts in every case traced here concern names, pronouns, clubs, records or shelter-notification rules.
- Elections act as a second amplifier: the litter-box claim peaked in September–October 2022 and the Trump line recurred in October 2024 and October 2026.

### Gaps
- No academic study measuring these lags systematically was found; the figures above are case observations and NBC's qualitative "several days."
- Original upload dates of the teacher videos are not given in the sources, so the lag from a teacher's post to the aggregator's repost is unmeasured.
- USA Today's Nov. 2, 2023 investigation and the Washington Post's April 19, 2022 article could not be opened (HTTP 402 and paywall); they are represented only through Wikipedia and NBC.
- The full NBC litter-box article body, CNN's Oct. 26, 2024 page (HTTP 451) and the CFC Elk Grove article (403) were not viewable directly.
- Whether the Elk Grove story moved beyond Gateway Pundit and Free Republic after Nov. 17, 2026, and what the board did, are unknown as of Nov. 19.
- Wisconsin rally wording of Sept. 7, 2024 was not transcribed in any page read.

## Key question 2: Which channel features are documented as accelerating the sensational version over the accurate one?

### Takeaway
The best-documented accelerants are structural: reposting of identifiable individuals' own content to a much larger hostile audience; engagement-ranked feeds that favor high-engagement and conservative-activist accounts on X; the removal of moderation rules and of third-party fact-check labels; press-release and single-source journalism syndicated across local station sites; tip lines and "incident" lists that publish submissions with little or no stated verification; and stump speeches, ads and hearings that restate claims after fact-checks. Evidence that monetization specifically drives this content is thin.

### Inferences
- The feature with the strongest evidence is identification plus reach: reposts that name a teacher or school to millions, followed by an official's quote-post, precede threats within days in dozens of counted cases. Causation for any single threat is unproven, as NBC states.
- Tip lines function more as announcements than as evidence pipelines: in every case with released data (Virginia, Arizona, Missouri) usable complaints were a small fraction, and where submissions were published (Indiana) the named districts disputed them. - Syndication multiplies single-source items: one advocacy poll or one aggregator post becomes dozens of local-station web pages.
- Monetization as a driver is asserted more than measured: the only dollar figures concern platform ad revenue projections and total creator payouts.

## Key question 4: Where does the accurate version circulate, and does it reach the same audience?

### Takeaway
Corrections travel through fact-checkers, wire services, mainstream and nonprofit education outlets, district statements and advocacy organizations, typically days after the claim, and the available research suggests they largely reach different audiences: fact-checks rarely reach the people who saw the false item, crowd-sourced notes work when shown but usually arrive late or are not shown, and the main platform program that attached fact-checks to viral posts ended in 2025. Direct evidence for this specific narrative is absent; the conclusion rests on general studies.

### Cited Findings
- Fact-check channels used in the traced cases: AP "Fact Focus" (syndicated on local station sites), PolitiFact, FactCheck.org, Lead Stories, Snopes, CNN; in each case the correction quoted primary text and officials. Sources under key question 1.
- Corrections also come from the named institutions: every district named in the litter-box claims denied it — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/misinformation/urban-myth-litter-boxes-schools-became-gop-talking-point-rcna51439); Newport-Mesa said "We do not refer students for gender-affirming care" (catalog, POL-002); the Minnesota Department of Education called the 2026 claim "unequivocally false" — [CNN via KEYT](https://keyt.com/politics/cnn-us-politics/2026/10/20/fact-check-trump-brings-back-his-2024-lie-that-schools-are-secretly-sending-kids-to-hospitals-for-gender-surgery/).
- Retractions exist but are individual: the Nebraska senator within hours; Rogan about three weeks later; Tony Dungy deleted a post and apologized (January 2023). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litter_boxes_in_schools_hoax). The claim continued afterwards (Fox anchor, August 2023; bills in 2024 and 2025).
- Claims persisted after correction in the Trump case: at least three further 2024 repetitions and four 2026 rallies after two fact-checks. — sources under key question 1.
- Audience overlap: in web-traffic data from the 2016 campaign, visits to untrustworthy sites were concentrated among "a subset of Americans with strong preferences for pro-attitudinal information" (Guess, Nyhan and Reifler, Nature Human Behaviour, 2020) [abstract fetched] — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32123342/); a Princeton summary says fact-checks of those articles "almost never" reached their readers [summary] — [Princeton SPIA](https://spia.princeton.edu/news/exposure-fake-news-during-2016-us-election-has-been-overstated). A 2020 replication (1,151 adults, 7.5 million visits) found 26.2% exposed to untrustworthy sites, down from 44.3%, with older adults and conservatives most exposed — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37055575/).
- Community Notes: an early study found "no evidence that the introduction of Community Notes significantly reduced engagement with misleading tweets" and suggested notes are too slow (Chuai et al., CSCW 2024) — [arXiv 2307.07960](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07960). A later study of 237,180 noted cascades (431 million reposts) found a displayed note reduces subsequent spread by 61.2% and raises deletion odds by 94.3%, but because notes "often appear too late" the system-wide reduction in engagement is 14.9%, and the effect is "significantly weaker for posts from influential accounts and political content" (Nature Communications, May 2026) — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42086582/). In an experiment with 1,810 Americans, notes with explanations were trusted more than plain flags across party lines (PNAS Nexus, 2024) — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38948016/).
- CCDH (Oct. 30, 2024): for 209 of 283 misleading election posts (74%), accurate notes were not shown to all users; those posts had 2.2 billion views. Selection method and period are not stated, and no post was about gender. — [CCDH](https://www.counterharm.com/research/rated-not-helpful-x-community-notes/)
- Platform change: Meta announced on Jan. 7, 2025 that it was ending its third-party fact-check program, the route by which the SB 5599 post was flagged, in favor of Community Notes "over the coming months." — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/07/meta-drops-fact-checking-and-loosens-its-content-moderation-rules)
- Audience migration: Bluesky added about 1 million users in the week after the 2024 election, reaching about 15 million; Threads reported 275 million monthly users [summary] — [AP via ABC News](https://abcnews.go.com/US/wireStory/bluesky-added-1-million-users-us-election-people-115833823). Libs of TikTok joined Bluesky in late November 2024 and was suspended Dec. 2, 2024 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libs_of_TikTok).
- Advocacy and union channels: HRC, GLAAD and Media Matters publish the counts cited here; Equality California issues statements and endorsements (catalog, section 4); NEA issued "Guidance for Educators Experiencing Harassment and Doxxing" (July 2022) and CTA an advisory on online harassment (September 2025) telling members to lock accounts and not to speak to media without union staff [summary] — [NEA guidance](https://www.nea.org/sites/default/files/2022-07/Guidance%20for%20Educators%20Experiencing%20Harassment%20and%20Doxxing%20-%202022.07.05.pdf); [CTA advisory](https://www.cta.org/document/online-harassment-and-doxxing-of-educators-september-2025). The National Education Association's lawsuit shut the federal portal — [Alabama Daily News](https://aldailynews.com/end-dei-school-complaint-portal-launch-in-february-now-offline/).
- Counter-mobilization inside the same channels: opponents flooded the Virginia, Missouri, Arizona and FBI tip lines (sources under key question 2); an X user's $1,000 challenge spread the Trump clip among critics — [Metro Weekly](https://metroweekly.com/?p=240904).
- Channels used by the other side to carry its own narrative: Democratic ads (about $11 million in 2026, AP); organized ballot-measure and recall campaigns (Orange Unified recall; Washington initiative opposition by the state teachers' and nurses' associations, catalog section 4); amicus briefs by ACLU, Lambda Legal and teachers' unions in *Mirabelli* (catalog section 4).

### Inferences
- The accurate version lives in formats with lower shareability: long fact-check articles, court records, district statements and nonprofit education news, published days later and mostly on sites the original audience does not visit.
- Corrections do reach the speaker in some cases, since retractions occurred, but they do not stop reuse by others.
- Two responses common on the correcting side may reduce its reach further: union advice to lock accounts and avoid media removes first-person rebuttals, and migration to Bluesky and Threads moves correcting voices away from where the claims circulate. This is an inference; no study of either effect was found.
- Flooding tip lines suppresses their output but also generates a second news cycle about the tip line.

### Gaps
- No study measuring whether fact-checks of these particular claims reached the audiences that saw them.
- No analysis of Community Notes on posts about schools and gender.
- No data on reach of LGBTQ organizations' or teachers' unions' own channels, or of TikTok creators rebutting these claims; these were covered by one combined search that returned only guidance documents.
- The 2020 paper's specific fact-check statistic comes from a press summary, not the paper.
- Not searched for lack of time rather than budget: Threads-specific data; Telegram and Truth Social content; talk-radio audience data; individual podcast episode reach; model-legislation tracking; books; sermons beyond Calvary Chapel Chino Hills.
