# Viral claims inventory: schools, the state, and minors' gender transition (U.S., 2021 to 2026-11-19)

Status checked through 2026-11-19. Record side = the companion catalog (`/home/user/test/reports/Minors gender actions parental consent catalog.md`); its case IDs (MED-, SOC-CA-, SOC-US-, CRT-, POL-, LEG-) are cited instead of re-researching the cases. Access notes that affect every entry: (1) posts on X, Facebook, Instagram and TikTok were NOT seen directly; wording is taken from fact-checks or news reports that quote them, as stated per item. (2) Most pages were read through an automated fetch tool that returns extracted quotes; Snopes, the AP transcript on PBS, the AP fact-check on FOX 13, the Do No Harm "about" page and a saved Reuters PDF were read as raw text. (3) Labels: factual status = established by records / corroborated account / sworn but contested allegation / reported allegation / unresolved; viral-wording label = no underlying case found / real case, materially distorted / substantially accurate / unverified. A law permitting something is never treated as evidence that it happened.

## Which viral claims have NO documented underlying case, which have a real case that was distorted, and which are substantially accurate?

### Takeaway
Of 33 claims inventoried, the surgery and medication claims (schools sending children for an "operation", school nurses or school clinics giving hormones without parents knowing, states "taking" children to transition them) have no documented underlying case; the custody and "the state took my child" stories rest on real cases whose records show a different trigger (court order, suicide risk, eating disorder, abuse report) than the viral telling; and the claims that are substantially accurate are almost all about social transition, written policy or statute text (the Oakland "transition closet" video, the Eau Claire training slide, the Kiel Title IX investigation, Fairfax's handbook, Oregon's and Maine's minor-consent statutes, the existence of non-disclosure policies). The summary table at the end of this section sorts all 33.

## What are the recurring distortion moves between the record and the viral version?

### Takeaway
Ten moves recur, and most viral claims use two or three at once: category upgrade (social → medical; device → medication; counseling → custody), "policy permits" → "it happened", jurisdiction → custody, court-authorized → "secret" or "the school did it", single cause substituted for the court's stated ground, clinic or agency → "the school", one case → "all over", parent-known → "without parents", proposed or failed → enacted, and enrollment → affected children. A smaller set of moves runs the other way in debunks: absolute denials, attacking the misattributed bill while skipping the real statute, and answering the wildest version only.

### Cited Findings
- **Social transition → surgery or "operation".** Campaign and White House both answered requests for surgery cases with social-transition material (school notification policies in 2024; a list of social-transition cases in 2026). — [PolitiFact](https://www.politifact.com/factchecks/2024/sep/05/donald-trump/trumps-pants-on-fire-claim-that-schools-are-perfor); [CNN via KEYT](https://keyt.com/politics/cnn-us-politics/2026/10/20/fact-check-trump-brings-back-his-2024-lie-that-schools-are-secretly-sending-kids-to-hospitals-for-gender-surgery/). ### Inferences
- The distortion usually happens at one identifiable hand-off: from a records-based advocacy post to a headline (Seattle), from an originator to a larger aggregator (Elk Grove, AB 665), or from a complaint to political speech (Sage Blair). Tracing the first hand-off is more informative than cataloguing later repeats.
- The category upgrade is the move that matters most for a reader: a claim can be accurate about social transition and false one sentence later about medicine, in the same speech (the 2026 State of the Union passage does both).
- Debunk-side overstatement is smaller in scale but gives the original claimants a real error to point at (the Federalist's AB 665 rebuttal is built on one).

### Gaps
- No systematic sample of posts was drawn; the moves are derived from 33 claims chosen for prominence, so their relative frequency is not measured.
- Whether amplifiers saw the originators' qualifiers (e.g. Gateway Pundit and the CFC text) cannot be established from the pages.

### Inferences
- The order of appearance is social (2022) → custody statutes (late 2022–2024) → on-campus clinics and hormones (2023–2024) → surgery (Aug. 2024). Each later family borrows credibility from the earlier, better-documented one; the White House's 2026 "nexus" statement makes that borrowing explicit.
- Three triggers account for most spikes: a legislative calendar in California, Washington, Minnesota, Maine and Colorado; records releases by one advocacy organization (the Seattle emails, the district list); and election-season speeches. Court events (cert petitions in Cox and IPEC, the Mirabelli order) re-energize stories without adding medical facts.
- The 2026 pattern (State of the Union in February, rallies in October, Elk Grove in November) suggests the family is active going into the Dec. 7, 2026 Supreme Court argument on SB 5599; that is an observation about timing, not about intent.

### Gaps
- Spike sizes are not measured: no engagement series, search-trend data or broadcast counts were collected, so "spike" here means clustering of dated publications found.
- Dates for the first tellings of several personal stories (Keck 2019, Yaeli's first public account, Friday's first account) predate 2021 or were not pinned to a primary source in this research.
- The first appearance of the phrase "state-sanctioned kidnapping" for these laws was not traced beyond the March 29, 2023 Fox headline cited by Third Way.
