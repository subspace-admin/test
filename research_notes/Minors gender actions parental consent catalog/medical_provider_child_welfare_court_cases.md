# Medical-provider, child-welfare, agency and family/juvenile-court cases: gender-related medical treatment or custody/placement of a minor where a parent was unaware, did not consent, or objected

Researcher notes. Status checked through 2026-11-18 unless a narrower date is given per case. Method notes: primary documents were downloaded and read directly (court opinions, SCOTUS dockets and filings, RECAP documents). The shared web-search budget for this session ran out part-way through, so several lower-priority items (marked in Gaps) rest on a single secondary source or could not be re-verified. Nothing below should be classified as "secret school-arranged treatment": no case in this file satisfies all four test elements.

Evidence labels used: **[R]** established by records; **[C]** corroborated account; **[S]** sworn but contested allegation; **[A]** reported allegation (unsworn or single-source); **[U]** unresolved.

Four-element test: (1) school or school employee arranged/materially facilitated; (2) gender-related medication or surgery actually prescribed/dispensed/administered; (3) relevant parent/guardian unaware or had not consented; (4) no prior court order authorized it.

---

## Key Question 1: For each case, who had legal authority to consent; did a court order pre-date treatment and what did it authorize; did treatment occur; did a school play any role?

### Takeaway
In every case with a usable record, treatment either followed a court order or custody allocation (D.H., Hudacko, Yaeli/Martinez by the mother's own account, J.N.S.), rested on a state minor-consent statute (Calgaro), or never demonstrably occurred (Siefert, Kolstad, Indiana M.C./J.C., Paul E., Cooper, "Ahmed," Texas DFPS). The only case where a provider gave gender-related medication to a minor with no parental consent and no court order is Calgaro (Minnesota), and there the school's documented role was refusing the mother records, not arranging treatment.

#### A. In re D.H. (San Diego) — the lead is correctly cited; it is a juvenile dependency appeal
- **Identity [R]:** *In re D.H.* (San Diego County Health & Human Services Agency v. J.D.), Cal. Ct. App., 4th Dist., Div. 1, No. D078915, filed 11/10/2021, **unpublished** (rule 8.1115); San Diego Super. Ct. No. J519523; Judges Rohanee Zapanta and Yvonne Esperanza Campos; opinion by Aaron, J.; affirmed. Mother "J.D." is appellant; the Agency (county counsel) is respondent. — [Opinion PDF, p.1](https://www4.courts.ca.gov/opinions/nonpub/D078915.PDF)
- **Custody/authority [R]:** D.H. (born Jan. 2004, assigned female) was taken into protective custody May 2017 at 13 after mother's arrest; declared a dependent Sept. 2017 and removed from mother; mother's reunification services terminated Feb. 2019; permanent plan of long-term foster care at San Pasqual Academy (SPA); a no-contact order against mother issued July 2020 on D.H.'s own §388 petition. Father (T.H.), previously incarcerated, had adjudicated parentage. — [Opinion pp.2-7](https://www4.courts.ca.gov/opinions/nonpub/D078915.PDF)
- **Order pre-dating treatment [R]:** D.H. filed an application for an order for medical treatment on 2/23/2021 with a Rady Children's Hospital Center for Gender Affirming Care physician's statement (Dr. David Inwards-Breland), a letter from the Center's director (psychosocial assessments Nov. 2019, Jan. 2020, Jan. 2021), and a six-page informed-consent form signed by D.H. The application recorded that mother and her counsel were notified 2/18/2021 (phone/e-mail) and opposed; father, father's counsel and D.H.'s counsel agreed. The court signed an **ex parte order on 2/25/2021 "authorizing gender affirming therapy (i.e., testosterone treatment)"**. — [Opinion pp.7-9 & fn.3](https://www4.courts.ca.gov/opinions/nonpub/D078915.PDF)
- **Treatment [R]:** First hormone appointment 4/30/2021; injection teaching 5/5/2021; third testosterone injection 5/19/2021. D.H. was 17. So the order preceded treatment by about two months. — [Opinion p.10](https://www4.courts.ca.gov/opinions/nonpub/D078915.PDF)
- **Holdings [R]:** (i) Mother had actual notice and forfeited her due-process claim by not promptly seeking a hearing before therapy began and by abandoning the contested hearing set for 5/25/2021 in favour of a §388 petition; (ii) summary denial of the §388 petition was not an abuse of discretion; (iii) the juvenile court had jurisdiction under Welf. & Inst. Code §362(a) ("any and all reasonable orders … including medical treatment") and §369(c) (court may authorize care when no parent is "capable of authorizing or willing to authorize"); mother's objection "did not divest the court of its jurisdiction"; (iv) the court expressly did **not** decide the Agency's argument that father's consent alone sufficed (fn.6), and stated it did not imply that a juvenile "generally has the legal capacity or authority to consent to such medical treatment" (fn.11). — [Opinion pp.16-18, 29-31](https://www4.courts.ca.gov/opinions/nonpub/D078915.PDF)
- **School role [R]:** None in arranging treatment. SPA appears in the opinion as D.H.'s foster placement with dormitories and on-site therapy; the court directed the Agency to ask SPA about a male dormitory; SPA staff were listed as part of D.H.'s support system and reported concerns about self-isolation. The court-appointed special advocate held educational rights. — [Opinion pp.4, 6-8, 10](https://www4.courts.ca.gov/opinions/nonpub/D078915.PDF)
- **Parental-involvement category:** one parent (father) agreed, the other (mother) was notified and objected; a court authorized the action. Legally relevant consent: the juvenile court's, because D.H. was a dependent in out-of-home care.
- **Test:** (1) No. (2) Yes. (3) No — mother was notified and objected; father agreed. (4) No — prior court order. **Fails 3 of 4.**

#### B. Hudacko v. Regents of the University of California (N.D. Cal. No. 3:23-cv-05316-SI; 9th Cir. No. 24-7360; S. Ct. No. 26-180) and the Contra Costa custody case
- **Custody order [R]:** "Findings and Order After Hearing," *Christine Hudacko v. Edward Hudacko*, Contra Costa Super. Ct., hearing 8/26/2020 (Judge Joni Hiramoto). Children: S.H. (born 2004, "AGE 16") and W.H. Joint legal custody of W.H.; mother made "the sole temporary legal custodial parent" of S.H., able to "make decisions on her own for [S.H.'s] health, education and welfare without Father's consent"; father kept a right to information but no "right" to attend medical appointments. **§7a:** S.H. "shall be permitted to pursue the services provided by UCSF as to [S.H.]'s gender identity, and shall be permitted to commence hormone therapy, if recommended by UCSF." **§7b:** S.H. "will not be permitted to undergo any gender identity related surgery until they are 18 years of age, absent a written agreement by both parties … or an order of the court." — [Cert. petition appendix, App.146a-153a; district court order App.11a-12a, 38a](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26-180/418433/20260807153429146_Petition%20for%20Certiorari-Hudacko%20v%20Regents%20of%20Univ%20of%20Cal.pdf)
- **Earlier order [A/S]:** Temporary sole legal custody was first granted 6/24/2020; Hudacko's own Oct. 2020 e-mail (complaint exhibit) says the June order "disallowed Christine from having sole legal discretion regarding hormonal treatment" and that the 8/26/2020 order "extended" her authority to hormonal treatment "while continuing to limit decisions over surgical treatment." — [Petition App.247a-248a; City Journal exhibit App.176a-177a](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26-180/418433/20260807153429146_Petition%20for%20Certiorari-Hudacko%20v%20Regents%20of%20Univ%20of%20Cal.pdf)
- **Treatment [R as to occurrence]:** On 8/4/2021 at UCSF, with mother's consent, S.H. (about 17; turned 18 in summer 2022) received a subcutaneous Supprelin (histrelin) implant; mother's e-mail of 10/18/2021 told father S.H. "has begun hormone therapy - testosterone blocker (Supprelin) via an implant and estrogen via pills (Estradiol)" and "started HRT in Aug." The procedure was billed to father's insurer as "surgery" ($209,820.34 per the City Journal exhibit). — [Petition pp.9-10, App.12a, 160a, 243a-244a](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26-180/418433/20260807153429146_Petition%20for%20Certiorari-Hudacko%20v%20Regents%20of%20Univ%20of%20Cal.pdf); [Regents' brief in opposition, pp.4-5](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26-180/433112/20261118114719391_26-180%20Brief%20in%20Opposition.pdf)
- **What Hudacko alleges exceeded the order [S, complaint allegation]:** that the implant was "gender identity related surgery" under §7b (citing Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §3041, the manufacturer's label that insertion "is a surgical procedure," and UCSF's own billing), performed without his written agreement or a court order although UCSF had the custody order (Sept. 2020) and his Oct. 2020 e-mail refusing consent. His federal claim targets the implant; estrogen falls within §7a's "hormone therapy" and is pleaded as accompanying treatment. — [Petition pp.8-11](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26-180/418433/20260807153429146_Petition%20for%20Certiorari-Hudacko%20v%20Regents%20of%20Univ%20of%20Cal.pdf)
- **Characterization conflict:** the petition calls the device a "cross-sex hormone-dispensing surgical implant"; respondents describe histrelin as medication that "help[s] temporarily suspend or block the physical changes of puberty," offered also as injections or tablets, inserted "during an outpatient visit." — [Petition p.9](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26-180/418433/20260807153429146_Petition%20for%20Certiorari-Hudacko%20v%20Regents%20of%20Univ%20of%20Cal.pdf); [BIO pp.2, 4-5](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26-180/433112/20261118114719391_26-180%20Brief%20in%20Opposition.pdf)
- **Rulings [R]:** 8/20/2024 — Regents dismissed (Eleventh Amendment); mother, her attorney (Bigger) and minor's counsel (Harkins) dismissed with leave (not state actors); substantive-due-process claim against UCSF individuals survived, court saying whether the implant was "surgery" or "hormone therapy" "will be determined on the merits." 11/25/2024 — second amended complaint dismissed; UCSF individuals (Drs. Rosenthal, Lee, Ehrensaft; CAGC legal director Asaf Orr) given qualified immunity because "[r]easonable doctors or lawyers could have believed the Supprelin implant was part of or an adjunct to the type of hormone therapy specifically permitted by Section 7a." Judgment 11/26/2024. 9th Cir. memorandum 10/21/2025 (S.R. Thomas, Nguyen, Bress) affirmed: "it was not clear the implant procedure fell within Provision 7b's 'surgery' exception," and "Hudacko cites no clearly established law creating a parental liberty interest in precluding the procedure at issue here when the other parent and minor child consent to it"; the custody order "does not grant UCSF any powers." Rehearing en banc denied 3/11/2026 (168 F.4th 1245; a judge called for a vote). — [Petition App.1a-9a, 38a-39a, 92a; pp.10-13](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26-180/418433/20260807153429146_Petition%20for%20Certiorari-Hudacko%20v%20Regents%20of%20Univ%20of%20Cal.pdf)
- **No court has found the order was violated [R]:** the district court "did not decide that the implant procedure was in fact hormone therapy" (9th Cir. mem.). A state-court contempt application (Sept. 2023) was withdrawn after the superior court indicated it lacked jurisdiction; final dissolution judgment March 2023. — [BIO pp.5-6](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26-180/433112/20261118114719391_26-180%20Brief%20in%20Opposition.pdf)
- **Status [R], checked 2026-11-18:** cert. petition filed 8/7/2026 (questions limited to qualified-immunity doctrine); all respondents waived; distributed for 10/9/2026 conference; **response requested 9/17/2026**; Regents/UCSF brief in opposition, Harkins' opposition and Bigger's joinder filed **11/18/2026**; amici for petitioner include NC Values Institute, Independent Women's Law Center, American College of Pediatricians, Our Duty-USA, Do No Harm, Genspect-USA, Manhattan Institute scholars. Pending. — [S. Ct. docket 26-180](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/26-180.html)
- **Judge Hiramoto [S/A]:** the complaint and Abigail Shrier's City Journal article (attached as an exhibit; advocacy-leaning) say the judge has a transgender adult child and later co-presented a training with Orr; Shrier reports asking the judge for comment. No court has ruled on recusal; Hudacko withdrew bias arguments on appeal. — [Petition pp.6-7, App.2a fn.1, 177a](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26-180/418433/20260807153429146_Petition%20for%20Certiorari-Hudacko%20v%20Regents%20of%20Univ%20of%20Cal.pdf)
- **School role:** none in the record (the custody order mentions school records and tuition only).
- **Parental-involvement category:** one parent consented (with sole legal custody) and the other objected; conduct continued after explicit objection; a court had authorized hormone therapy; father says he was not notified of the procedure until Oct. 2021. Legally relevant consent: mother's, plus (if the implant was "surgery") father's written agreement or a court order — the unresolved question.
- **Test:** (1) No. (2) Yes. (3) Partly — custodial parent consented; father unaware and objecting. (4) Disputed — an order authorized "hormone therapy"; whether it covered the implant is undecided. **Does not satisfy the test.**

Parents of patients disputed her accounts to local media, and the university's own response reportedly conceded it had not required written consent or custody agreements while denying other claims (search-summary level). — [Media Matters roundup](https://www.mediamatters.org/local-news/st-louis-based-news-outlets-investigate-anti-trans-whistleblower-report-after-right-wing); [Just the News](https://justthenews.com/politics-policy/health/gender-clinic-kids-confirms-some-whistleblower-claims-pledges-vague-changes)

Not gender-related care. — [NBC Los Angeles, 7/28/2020](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/local/lawsuit-alleges-teenage-boy-in-la-county-juvenile-hall-was-given-female-hormones/2403936/)
- **Excluded — Wailes v. Jefferson County Public Schools (Colo.):** overnight-trip room assignments; no medical element; dismissed 8/7/2025; 10th Cir. argued May 2026, undecided. — [ADF case page](https://adflegal.org/case/wailes-v-jefferson-county-public-schools/)

### Inferences

- In Hudacko the practical dispute is a drafting ambiguity ("hormone therapy" vs "surgery") in a custody order, not absence of consent: the parent with sole legal custody consented.

## Key Question 3: What has a court actually found vs. what is alleged? What did the provider/agency say? (Plus 2025-2026 federal context)

### Takeaway
Judicial findings are narrow: courts have upheld a dependency court's power to authorize testosterone (D.H.), granted qualified immunity without deciding whether a custody order was breached (Hudacko), and dismissed parents' federal claims on state-action, Monell, mootness or preclusion grounds (Calgaro, Galdamez, Kolstad). No court in these cases has found that a school arranged medication, and no federal investigation has published findings on parental consent.

### Cited Findings
- **Found (final):** juvenile court may authorize hormone therapy for a dependent child over a parent's objection; mother forfeited her hearing claim. — [In re D.H. pp.16-18, 29-30](https://www4.courts.ca.gov/opinions/nonpub/D078915.PDF)
- **Found (final below, cert. pending):** UCSF clinicians and counsel have qualified immunity; mother and family lawyers are not state actors; fraudulent-concealment and IIED claims fail. **Not found:** that the implant was or was not "surgery." Provider position: the order "expressly authorized 'hormone therapy'" and the implant was one delivery method chosen by the custodial parent and patient. — [9th Cir. mem., App.3a-8a](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26-180/418433/20260807153429146_Petition%20for%20Certiorari-Hudacko%20v%20Regents%20of%20Univ%20of%20Cal.pdf); [BIO p.2](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26-180/433112/20261118114719391_26-180%20Brief%20in%20Opposition.pdf)
- **Found:** private providers honouring a minor's consent are not state actors; no decision on the consent's validity. — [Calgaro pp.4-5](https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/19/03/172279P.pdf)
- **Found:** Martinez's federal claims fail as pleaded; nothing decided about hormones or the school. — [Galdamez order](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cacd.826608/gov.uscourts.cacd.826608.52.0_2.pdf)
- **Found (pleading stage only):** a hospital working "in tandem" with a county agency to withhold a child for four weeks without a hearing states a procedural due-process claim. — [Siefert pp.2, 6](https://www.opn.ca6.uscourts.gov/opinions.pdf/20a0066p-06.pdf)
- **Found:** Kolstad's federal claims are precluded by the unappealed state adjudication. — [9th Cir. mem.](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/memoranda/2025/11/12/25-2106.pdf)
- **Jury finding:** malpractice liability where a parent *did* consent (Varian). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varian_v_Einhorn)
- **Related Supreme Court context:** the Hudacko petition cites *Mirabelli v. Bonta*, 607 U.S. 492 (2026) (per curiam), as holding at the preliminary-injunction stage that California school nondisclosure policies "likely violate parents' rights"; that concerns school secrecy and social transition, not medication. — [Petition pp.4-5](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26-180/418433/20260807153429146_Petition%20for%20Certiorari-Hudacko%20v%20Regents%20of%20Univ%20of%20Cal.pdf)

**Federal actions 2025-2026 (status context only)**
- EO 14187 (1/28/2025) defines "child" as under 19; §8(e) directs the Attorney General to prioritize action against "so-called sanctuary States" that "facilitate stripping custody from parents." — [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/01/protecting-children-from-chemical-and-surgical-mutilation/)

### Inferences
- Parents' federal suits have mostly failed on threshold doctrines, so the underlying factual disputes (was the order breached; was the minor's consent valid; what did the school do) remain unadjudicated.
- Federal enforcement to date has produced litigation over records, not public findings about consent practices.
