### Inferences
- The quoted pronoun sentence is worded as an example of conduct that "may constitute" gender-based harassment. It is not, on the quoted text, a stand-alone offense with its own penalty. The discipline sentence quoted by CFC is permissive ("may include") and conditioned on "the entire circumstances of the incident(s)."
- The quoted text contains no "persistent" or "intentional" qualifier attached to the pronoun example itself. The intent and severity limits come from elsewhere: Education Code 48900.4 ("intentionally engaged," "severe or pervasive"), 48900.5 (other means of correction), and the district's own poster language ("counseling and appropriate discipline").
- The staff carve-out for "inadvertent slips or honest mistakes" is, on CFC's quotation, written for district personnel. Whether the AR has an equivalent sentence for students is unknown.
- Simbli now redirects the BP link used by Gateway Pundit on Nov 16 (revid `SplusO9IEGwnQYOmKdN9DsOyw==`) to a different revision ID (`qWApBlaKDzZX5sBs0y7vUA==`), and the district's published AR link to `AslshWels3TU2lrghj9LJQa9Q==`. ## A5. Is the language derived from CSBA / CDE? How common is it?

### Takeaway
Yes as to origin: the sentence CFC quotes from EGUSD is word-for-word the first example in CSBA's sample AR 5145.3 as adopted elsewhere, and CSBA's 2023 legal guidance ties the concept to CDE and U.S. Department of Education examples. I confirmed the same or near-identical sentence in two other districts' documents. CFC's count of "at least 10 districts and one county office" is its own review and is unverified.

### Cited Findings
- ESTABLISHED BY RECORDS — El Rancho USD AR 5145.3 (regulation "approved: August 18, 2020"), section "Transgender and Gender-Nonconforming Students": "Examples of the types of conduct which are prohibited in the district and which may constitute gender-based harassment include, but are not limited to: 1. Refusing to address a student by a name and the pronouns consistent with the student's gender identity," followed by six further examples (restroom blocking, taunting, revealing transgender status "to individuals who do not have a legitimate need for the information, without the student's consent," slurs, assault). The document lists CSBA publications as references. — [El Rancho USD BP/AR 5145.3](https://www.erusd.org/pdf/board_policies/5145_3.pdf)
- ESTABLISHED BY RECORDS — The same CSBA-based policy states the discipline rule with the statutory threshold: students who engage in unlawful discrimination "shall be subject to appropriate consequence or discipline, which may include suspension or expulsion when the behavior is severe or pervasive as defined in Education Code 48900.4." (BP 5145.3, "adopted: August 7, 2018"). — [El Rancho USD BP/AR 5145.3](https://www.erusd.org/pdf/board_policies/5145_3.pdf)
- ESTABLISHED BY RECORDS — The same regulation has the staff carve-out CFC quotes for Elk Grove: "Inadvertent slips or honest mistakes by district personnel in the use of the student's name and/or consistent pronouns will, in general, not constitute a violation of this administrative regulation." — [El Rancho USD BP/AR 5145.3](https://www.erusd.org/pdf/board_policies/5145_3.pdf)
- ESTABLISHED BY RECORDS — San Dieguito Union HSD, September 11, 2025 board item on revised AR 5145.3: the draft's "Prohibition of Certain Acts" includes "Refusing to address or refer to a student in a manner by a name and the pronouns consistent with the student's gender identity," and a staff provision that "Inadvertent slips or honest mistakes by district personnel... may not constitute a violation." A trustee (Michael Allman) proposed edits. — [SDUHSD trustee memo, Sept 11, 2025](https://d16k74nzx9emoe.cloudfront.net/c14c8547-fc39-4053-a51b-1aed21a1ea83/Allman%20AR%205145.3%20Proposed%20Revisions.pdf)

## A6. Legal limits on disciplining students for pronoun use, and how *Mirabelli* bears on it

### Takeaway
State statutes set the discipline floor: harassment-based suspension requires intentional, severe or pervasive conduct in grades 4–12, suspension is a last resort, and California separately protects student speech. *Mirabelli v. Bonta* (U.S. March 2, 2026) concerns how a school treats a parent's own child and what it tells that parent; it does not decide whether one student may be disciplined for how he or she addresses another. Those are separate questions and should be filed separately.

### Cited Findings
- ESTABLISHED BY RECORDS — Ed. Code § 220: "No person shall be subjected to discrimination on the basis of disability, gender, gender identity, gender expression... in any program or activity conducted by an educational institution that receives, or benefits from, state financial assistance." — [Ed. Code § 220](https://california.public.law/codes/education_code_section_220)
- ESTABLISHED BY RECORDS — Ed. Code § 48900.4: a pupil "in any of grades 4 to 12, inclusive, may be suspended from school or recommended for expulsion if the superintendent or the principal... determines that the pupil has intentionally engaged in harassment, threats, or intimidation... sufficiently severe or pervasive to have the actual and reasonably expected effect of materially disrupting classwork, creating substantial disorder, and invading the rights of either school personnel or pupils by creating an intimidating or hostile educational environment." — [Ed. Code § 48900.4](https://california.public.law/codes/education_code_section_48900.4)
- ESTABLISHED BY RECORDS — Ed. Code § 48900.5: "Suspension... shall be imposed only when other means of correction fail to bring about proper conduct," with listed alternatives (parent conference, counseling referral, intervention teams, restorative and positive-behavior programs). First-offense suspension is limited to § 48900(a)–(e) violations or danger to persons. — [Ed. Code § 48900.5](https://california.public.law/codes/education_code_section_48900.5)
- ESTABLISHED BY RECORDS — Ed. Code § 48907: public-school pupils "shall have the right to exercise freedom of speech and of the press," except expression that is "obscene, libelous, or slanderous" or that incites "the violation of lawful school regulations, or the substantial disruption of the orderly operation of the school." — [Ed. Code § 48907](https://california.public.law/codes/education_code_section_48907)
- ESTABLISHED BY RECORDS — Ed. Code § 48950: a district operating high schools "shall not make or enforce a rule subjecting a high school pupil to disciplinary sanctions solely on the basis of conduct that is speech or other communication that, when engaged in outside of the campus, is protected" by the First Amendment or the California Constitution; "This section does not prohibit the imposition of discipline for harassment, threats, or intimidation, unless constitutionally protected." A pupil may sue for injunctive and declaratory relief. — [Ed. Code § 48950](https://california.public.law/codes/education_code_section_48950)
- ESTABLISHED BY RECORDS — *Mirabelli v. Bonta*, No. 25A810 (U.S. Mar. 2, 2026) (per curiam): the application to vacate the Ninth Circuit's stay "is granted as to the parents but is otherwise denied." Justices Thomas and Alito would grant in full; Justice Sotomayor would deny in full; Justice Barrett concurred, joined by the Chief Justice and Justice Kavanaugh. — [Supreme Court docket 25A810](https://www.supremecourt.gov/search.aspx?filename=/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25a810.html); opinion text at [Justia](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/607/25A810/) and [Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/25A810)
- ESTABLISHED BY RECORDS — What the Court said the case is about: parents object that state policies "prevent schools from telling them about their children's efforts to engage in gender transitioning at school unless the children consent," and "take issue with California's requirement that schools use children's preferred names and pronouns regardless of their parents' wishes." The district-court injunction "prevents the schools from 'misleading' parents about their children's gender presentation at school" and "requires the schools to follow parents' directions regarding their children's names and pronouns." (slip op. at 1, 3). — [Cornell LII, 25A810](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/25A810)
- ESTABLISHED BY RECORDS — Ninth Circuit follow-up: the stay "is vacated as to the parents' claims and remains in effect as to the remaining claims" (order of March 3, 2026, described in order of March 17, 2026); the state's motion to carve out disclosure "to parents who would engage in abuse" was denied without prejudice and sent to the district court. — [Ninth Circuit docket No. 25-8056 (CourtListener)](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72227486/mirabelli-et-al-v-bonta-et-al/)
- ESTABLISHED BY RECORDS — Appeal status: consolidated appeals Nos. 25-8056 and 26-2337 are fully briefed (state reply brief filed October 16, 2026; plaintiffs' Rule 28(j) letter filed November 6, 2026). No merits decision appears on the docket as of 2026-11-18. — [Ninth Circuit docket No. 25-8056 (CourtListener)](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72227486/mirabelli-et-al-v-bonta-et-al/)
- ESTABLISHED BY RECORDS — CDE posted the injunction-compelled statement: "Parents and guardians have a federal constitutional right to be informed if their public school student child expresses gender incongruence...," noting that the Supreme Court "allowed a stay of that injunction with respect to public school employee plaintiffs to remain in effect." — CDE "Protections for LGBTQ+ Students: AB 1955," Last Reviewed March 3, 2026 (cached copy; section home [cde.ca.gov/pd](https://www.cde.ca.gov/pd/))
- CORROBORATED ACCOUNT — A state court has read *Mirabelli* narrowly: on September 15, 2026, San Bernardino Superior Court Judge Michael Sachs declined to dissolve his injunction against Chino Valley's notification policy, distinguishing a policy that "essentially mandated that staff lie to the parent" from the question "whether school employees can be required to disclose." — [LAist, Sept 17, 2026](https://laist.com/news/education/judge-says-chino-valley-unified-can-disclose-students-gender-identity-to-parents)
- CORROBORATED ACCOUNT — The Ninth Circuit applied *Mirabelli* on June 18, 2026 in *City of Huntington Beach v. Newsom*, No. 25-3826, granting an injunction pending appeal that "pauses the SAFETY Act only for seven specific parent plaintiffs." — [Equality California / ACLU SoCal statement, June 30, 2026](https://www.eqca.org/aclu-ninth-circuit-safety-act/); consistent with [ACLU NorCal, updated Sept 10, 2026](https://www.aclunc.org/no-forced-outing)

### Inferences
- On the statutes, an isolated or mistaken pronoun use cannot by itself support suspension or expulsion: § 48900.4 requires intent and severity or pervasiveness, § 48900.5 requires other means of correction first, and K–3 pupils are excluded. Prior finding 2 is consistent with the statutory text.
- *Mirabelli* bears on Elk Grove in one specific way: where a parent directs the school on his or her own child's name and pronouns, the injunction as described by the Supreme Court requires schools to follow that direction. CFC says Elk Grove's AR has no parental-objection provision; if so, that is a gap between the AR and the injunction as to the school's own conduct. It is not a ruling on peer speech.
- A second-order question is unresolved: if a parent has directed use of a birth name and pronouns for a child, can a classmate's use of the same be "gender-based harassment" under the AR? No authority found addresses it.
- Whether the *Mirabelli* injunction binds a non-party district such as EGUSD directly, or only through the state defendants, is a legal question I did not resolve.

### Gaps
- *Defending Education v. Olentangy Local School District* (6th Cir. en banc 2025): not retrieved; Justia returned 403. CFC describes it as a ruling "against an Ohio school district's policy punishing students for misgendering classmates." It is out-of-circuit and not binding in California. Holding and date need primary verification.
- Ninth Circuit authority on student-to-student pronoun speech: none identified. CFC relies on a 2025 adoption-licensing case (captioned by CFC "*Bates v. Wendt*"), which is not a student-discipline case; not retrieved.
- Any 2026 Supreme Court action on student pronoun speech (for example a certiorari petition in *Olentangy*): not checked.
- The UC order attributed to Judge Slaughter (September 2026) and the California Supreme Court's November 2025 long-term-care decision: not retrieved.
