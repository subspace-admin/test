# Legislation and minor-consent laws: gender-related treatment, mental-health care, shelter, and school parental notification (policy appendix — separate from incident records)

**Date checked for every item below: 2026-11-18.** Status labels used: **ENACTED**, **PENDING**, **FAILED / VETOED**, **ENJOINED**. Care types are kept separate: **medication** (puberty blockers / hormones), **surgery**, **mental-health counseling**, **shelter / notification**.

**Standing caveat (applies to every row):** a statute that permits a minor to consent, or a bill that would, is a statement of what the law allows. It is not evidence that any particular minor received any treatment, and nothing in this appendix documents a treatment incident.

**Method note:** statutory quotes were pulled from the official legislature / code sites with a direct download unless marked "mirror" or "secondary". California's official site (leginfo.legislature.ca.gov) refused automated requests (HTTP 403), so California code text is quoted from the california.public.law mirror, which reproduces leginfo and shows a "verified" date of Nov. 11–16, 2026; bill histories are from CalMatters Digital Democracy (which republishes leginfo histories and committee analyses). congress.gov also refused requests, so federal bill status is from govinfo.gov bulk BILLSTATUS files. ## Key Question 1: In which U.S. jurisdictions, as of 2026-11-18, can a minor lawfully obtain gender-related MEDICATION without a parent's consent, under what statutory conditions, and at what age? Surgery?

### Takeaway
Maine is the only state found with a statute that expressly authorizes gender-affirming hormone therapy (including puberty suppression, excluding surgery) for a minor without parental consent — age 16–17, with a gender dysphoria diagnosis, a provider harm finding, the minor's report that a parent refused to support treatment, and written informed consent (22 M.R.S. §1508, in force since Oct. 25, 2023; a 2025 repeal bill failed). Oregon reaches the same care through a general, not gender-specific, statute (age 15+, medical "or surgical" treatment); Washington, Vermont (since July 1, 2026), Colorado, Minnesota, Massachusetts, Rhode Island and others have general minor-consent rules limited mostly to homeless, living-apart, or older minors that do not name gender care; California has no statute letting an ordinary minor consent to hormones or blockers, and its age-12 mental-health and shelter consent laws expressly exclude psychotropic drugs.

#### Maine — ENACTED (medication only; surgery excluded)
- LD 535 (H.P. 340), "An Act Regarding Consent for Gender-affirming Hormone Therapy for Certain Minors," 131st Legislature, First Special Session; final disposition "Enacted, Jul 11, 2023"; chaptered as Public Law 2023, chapter 413. — [Maine Legislature, LD 535 status](https://legislature.maine.gov/legis/bills/display_ps.asp?LD=535&snum=131)
- Sponsor: Rep. Erin Sheehan. House passed to be enacted June 27, 2023 (Roll Call 347, 71–65); Senate July 6, 2023 (Roll Call 471, 19–12); signed by the Governor. — [Maine LawMakerWeb summary, HP 340](https://legislature.maine.gov/LawMakerWeb/summary.asp?paper=HP0340&SessionID=15)
- Effective date: the session-law volume gives the general effective date for First Special Session non-emergency laws as October 25, 2023. — [Laws of Maine 2023, PL c. 413 (title page)](https://lldc.mainelegislature.org/Open/Laws/2023/2023_PL_c413.pdf)
- Operative text, §1508(2): "A health care professional may provide gender-affirming hormone therapy and follow-up care to a minor without obtaining the consent of the parent or guardian of the minor only if:" (A) "The minor is at least 16 years of age"; (B) "The minor has been diagnosed with gender dysphoria by a health care professional"; (C) "In the judgment of the health care professional, the minor is experiencing harm from or is expected to experience harm from not receiving gender-affirming hormone therapy"; (D) "The minor informs the health care professional that the minor has discussed the minor's gender dysphoria with a parent or guardian of the minor and that parent or guardian refused to support treatment of the minor's gender dysphoria"; and (E) the minor gives informed written consent and "under all the surrounding circumstances, is mentally and physically competent to give consent." — [22 M.R.S. §1508](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/22/title22sec1508.html)
- Type of care: "'Gender-affirming hormone therapy' means nonsurgical, medically necessary health care that respects the gender identity of the patient ... that includes, but is not limited to ... (1) Interventions to suppress the development of endogenous secondary sex characteristics". Surgery is therefore outside the section; puberty suppression is inside it. — [22 M.R.S. §1508(1)(A)](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/22/title22sec1508.html)
- Counseling / informed-consent duties, §1508(3): the professional must, among other things, "Clearly and fully explore with the minor the alternative choices available for managing and treating gender dysphoria" and "Discuss the possibility of involving the minor's parents or guardians in the minor's decision making"; if the minor chooses not to involve them, "the reasons for making that choice must be stated in writing on the form." — [22 M.R.S. §1508(3)](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/22/title22sec1508.html)
- Who may prescribe: a prescriber licensed by the Board of Licensure in Medicine, the Board of Osteopathic Licensure, or an advanced practice registered nurse; a 2025 amendment (PL 2025, c. 738, Pt. C, §6) rewrites that definition effective Jan. 1, 2027 to refer to the "Maine Board of Medicine." The consent conditions were not changed. — [22 M.R.S. §1508(1)(C)](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/22/title22sec1508.html)
- Liability: a compliant written consent "bars an action by a parent or guardian of the minor on the grounds of battery, malpractice or any other claim for providing gender-affirming hormone therapy without consent from a parent or guardian"; and §1508(7): "This section does not affect the legal authority of a parent or guardian to consent." — [22 M.R.S. §1508(4), (7)](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/22/title22sec1508.html)
- Litigation: none affecting §1508 was found; the statute page shows no repeal. — [22 M.R.S. §1508 section history](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/22/title22sec1508.html)
- Separate general rule (secondary source): a Maine minor living separately from parents and independent of parental support may consent to health services under 22 M.R.S. §1503. — [SchoolHouse Connection compilation, July 2026 (secondary)](https://schoolhouseconnection.org/article/state-laws-on-minor-consent-for-routine-medical-care)

### Inferences
- "Expressly authorized" and "reachable through a general statute" are different things and should be reported separately. Only Maine's statute names gender-affirming hormone therapy. Oregon's wording is broad enough to cover it, and state and press sources describe it that way, but the statute itself never mentions gender care.
- Surgery: Maine's statute excludes it by definition. Oregon's and Rhode Island's general statutes use the word "surgical," and Vermont's and Colorado's cover surgical care for the narrow groups they reach; none of these is a gender-specific authorization, and whether a provider would operate on a minor's consent alone is a clinical and payer question these sources do not answer.
- California: an ordinary minor living at home cannot consent to hormones or blockers under any statute found. The only statutory route is the self-sufficient-minor rule (15+, living apart, managing own finances), which is a general medical-care rule. The age-12 statutes cover counseling and shelter only and exclude psychotropic drugs.
- Vermont's 2026 act is new enough that the Vermont Medical Society's 2023 statement ("parental consent is currently required") is now incomplete for certified homeless 16–17-year-olds, though still accurate for other minors.
- Federal payment limits now sit on top of state consent rules: a minor's legal ability to consent does not mean the care is covered (see Key Question 2, federal Medicaid rule effective Oct. 13, 2026).

- Washington mature-minor doctrine (Smith v. Seibly, 72 Wn.2d 16 (1967)) was not retrieved from an official source in this session; the citation is from background knowledge and should be verified before use.
- Oregon Health Plan coverage criteria and any age limits on gender-related surgery for minors were not verified. A search result pointed to OAR 413-020-0150 (child-welfare rule listing "non-reversible gender affirming medical services") but the rule text was not fetched.
- Massachusetts' common-law mature-minor rule, and the minor-consent rules of Connecticut, New Jersey, Michigan, Delaware, Hawaii, New Mexico, Nevada, Illinois, New York and D.C., were not checked against official text; the entries above for those states rest on one secondary compilation.

## Key Question 2: Which pending bills (state or federal) could change minors' access to such treatment without parental permission — in either direction — and what is each bill's latest action? (Includes failed / vetoed measures, Nov. 3, 2026 ballot results, and the two context tables.)

### Takeaway
No pending state bill was found that would newly let minors consent to gender medication; the movement is in the restricting direction. Efforts to roll back the Maine and Oregon consent rules failed in 2025, Washington voters rejected the 2026 parental-rights initiative, and no gender-related measure qualified for California's 2026 ballot. Federally, a Medicaid/CHIP funding ban for minors took effect Oct. 13, 2026, a hospital-participation ban remains only proposed, and three House-passed bills (criminal ban, Medicaid ban, school parental-consent bill) are sitting in Senate committees.

### Inferences
- The three state consent rules most often cited (Maine 16+, Oregon 15+, Washington shelter notification) each survived a direct rollback attempt in 2025–2026: Maine LD 380 and Oregon HB 2429 died in committee process, and Washington voters declined to restore the 2024 notification rights.
- For practical access, the federal Medicaid/CHIP rule (effective Oct. 13, 2026) is the most consequential enacted change of 2026: it leaves state consent statutes intact but removes federal payment for minors, subject to pending multistate litigation.
- The three House-passed federal bills need 60 Senate votes and have had no Senate committee action; they are properly described as pending, not enacted.
- After Mirabelli and the June 2026 Huntington Beach ruling, California's AB 1955 cannot be described as fully in force: its core "shall not be required to disclose" provisions are preliminarily enjoined, and objecting parents have court-ordered protection while the appeal proceeds. Neither ruling is a final merits judgment.
- Colorado is the one protective-leaning state where voters appear to have newly restricted minors' gender care (surgery only) in 2026.
