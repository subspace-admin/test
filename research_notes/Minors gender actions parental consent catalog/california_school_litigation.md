# California school social-transition / parental-notification litigation and underlying incidents (excluding Orange County, Pasadena-specific coverage, Santa Clarita, Elk Grove)

**Status checked through 2026-11-18** (each item gives the date of the source relied on). Evidence labels used: **[REC]** established by records · **[CORR]** corroborated account · **[SWORN-C]** sworn but contested (or untested) allegation · **[ALLEG]** reported/complaint allegation · **[UNRES]** unresolved. "Four-element test" = (1) school/employee arranged or materially facilitated; (2) gender-related medication or surgery actually prescribed/dispensed/administered; (3) parent unaware or non-consenting; (4) no prior court order.

**Headline negative finding:** In none of the school-litigation incidents below (Mirabelli/Poe, Mirabelli/Doe, Regino, Konen, Escondido, Chino Valley) do the records I reviewed allege or establish puberty blockers, cross-sex hormones or surgery. They are social-transition / information-withholding cases. The only California incident located in which gender-related medication is described (Abigail Martinez / Arcadia USD / L.A. County DCFS) is, on the mother's own account, one where a juvenile court authorized hormones over her objection — so it fails element 4 and its school-facilitation element is a contested, unsworn allegation.

## 1. Mirabelli v. Olson / Mirabelli v. Bonta (S.D. Cal. No. 3:23-cv-00768-BEN-VET; 9th Cir. No. 25-8056; U.S. No. 25A810): plaintiffs, each family, rulings, Supreme Court order of March 2, 2026, and everything after

### Takeaway
Mirabelli began as two Escondido Union School District (EUSD) teachers' suit and became a statewide Rule 23(b)(2) class action against the Attorney General, State Superintendent and State Board; the two parent-plaintiff families are **not** Escondido families — the Poes are tied to **Clovis Unified** and then **Yosemite Valley Charter School**, the Does to **Pasadena Unified** — and those LEAs are named in filings/declarations but are not defendants. On March 2, 2026 the Supreme Court (per curiam) vacated the Ninth Circuit's stay of the Dec. 22, 2025 class-wide permanent injunction **only as to the parent subclasses**, leaving the teacher portions stayed; as of 2026-11-18 the merits appeal is fully or nearly fully briefed and still pending in the Ninth Circuit with no merits decision found.

**Ninth Circuit stay and Supreme Court order**
- Jan. 5, 2026: Ninth Circuit stayed the injunction pending appeal, describing it as "sweeping, ambiguous," questioning class certification rigor and standing of uninjured class members, and finding the State likely to defeat the parents' claims (treating Mahmoud v. Taylor as narrow). **[REC]** — [9th Cir. order, Dkt. 13.1, 1/5/2026](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/63d954d4e4ad424df7819d46/695d46b36a0be201a62e2ab3_26-01-05%20-%20Mirabelli%2C%20et%20al.%20v.%20Bonta%2C%20et%20al.%20-%20ORDER%20FILED.pdf); summarized in [per curiam slip op. 3–4](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25a810_b97d.pdf)
- March 2, 2026 disposition (verbatim): "The application to vacate the Ninth Circuit's stay presented to JUSTICE KAGAN and by her referred to the Court is granted as to the parents but is otherwise denied. JUSTICE THOMAS and JUSTICE ALITO would grant the application in full. JUSTICE SOTOMAYOR would deny the application in full." Barrett, J., concurred (joined by Roberts, C.J., and Kavanaugh, J.); Kagan, J., dissented (joined by Jackson, J.). **[REC]** — [slip op. 7](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25a810_b97d.pdf); [docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25A810.html)
- Reasoning: (i) parents seeking religious exemptions "are likely to succeed on the merits of their Free Exercise Clause claim" under Mahmoud/Yoder — "the intrusion on parents' free exercise rights here—unconsented facilitation of a child's gender transition—is greater than the introduction of LGBTQ storybooks"; policies likely fail strict scrutiny because they "cut out the primary protectors of children's best interests: their parents" and "The State's interest in safety could be served by a policy that allows religious exemptions while precluding gender-identity disclosure to parents who would engage in abuse"; (ii) the due-process subclass is likely to succeed because parental rights include "the right not to be shut out of participation in decisions regarding their children's mental health" (Parham); "when a child exhibits symptoms of gender dysphoria at school, California's policies conceal that information from parents and facilitate a degree of gender transitioning during school hours"; (iii) irreparable harm and equities favor parents; (iv) parents "very likely have standing because they are objects of the challenged exclusion policies" and "the injunction does not provide relief for all the parents… but only for those parents who object… or seek religious exemptions"; class certification "was likely proper." The opinion gives no reasoning for denying relief to the teachers. **[REC — interim "likelihood" ruling, not a merits judgment]** — [slip op. 4–7](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25a810_b97d.pdf)
- Barrett concurrence: "The word 'likely' is important, because it reflects that our assessment is preliminary… The parents must continue to litigate in the Ninth Circuit"; notes the record shows nondisclosure "applies even if parents expressly ask" and that "One set of parents learned of their child's transition at school only after the child attempted suicide… even after this tragic event, school administrators continued to withhold information." — [Barrett, J., concurring 2–3](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25a810_b97d.pdf)
- Kagan dissent: objects to deciding on the emergency docket while a Ninth Circuit en banc request and the Foote v. Ludlow cert petition (No. 25-77) were pending; counts "almost 40 cases raising due process and/or free exercise objections to similar school policies"; says no one will read the per curiam "as anything less than a conclusive merits judgment." — [Kagan, J., dissenting 1–4](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25a810_b97d.pdf)
- Recital of facts by the Court (useful as the most authoritative summary, though derived from plaintiffs' declarations): Poes "were not told by their daughter's school when she began to present as a boy… during her seventh-grade year"; "At a new school in ninth grade… Contrary to the Poes' instructions, teachers and school officials continued to use a male name and pronouns… citing their obligations under California state law. The Poes have placed their daughter in therapy and obtained psychiatric care for her." — [slip op. 2](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25a810_b97d.pdf)

## 6. Applicable California rules (CDE guidance and its replacement; AB 1266; Educ. Code §§220/221.5; AB 1955; minor-consent statutes; can a minor consent to gender-related medication without a parent?)

### Takeaway
California's school rules during 2016–2024 came from statute (Educ. Code §§220, 221.5(f)) plus CDE's 2016 Legal Advisory/FAQ, which said schools must, "with rare exceptions," respect a student's limits on disclosure "including not sharing that information with the student's parents"; AB 1955 (eff. Jan. 1, 2025) codified a ban on *mandatory*-disclosure policies and CDE replaced the FAQ with non-binding AB 1955 guidance. Minor-consent law lets a mature 12-year-old consent to **outpatient counseling** but expressly **not** psychotropic drugs; general medical care (which is where blockers/hormones fall) requires parental consent unless the minor is emancipated, a self-sufficient 15+ living apart, or a court authorizes it.

### Cited Findings
- Educ. Code §221.5(f) (AB 1266, eff. Jan. 1, 2014): "A pupil shall be permitted to participate in sex-segregated school programs and activities, including athletic teams and competitions, and use facilities consistent with his or her gender identity, irrespective of the gender listed on the pupil's records." Says nothing about parental notice. — [Educ. Code §221.5](https://california.public.law/codes/education_code_section_221.5)
- CDE FAQ (published Jan. 29, 2016 with a Legal Advisory), as quoted from the record: districts "should accept and respect a student's assertion of their gender identity"; "schools must consult with a transgender student to determine who can or will be informed of the student's transgender status, if anyone, including the student's family"; "[w]ith rare exceptions, schools are required to respect the limitations that a student places on the disclosure of their transgender status, including not sharing that information with the student's parents." Plaintiffs' brief (citing record exhibits) says NCLR supplied the draft advisory in March 2013 and that CSBA's model BP/AR 5145.3 repeated these directives. **[REC for quoted text via court record; drafting history is plaintiffs' characterization of exhibits]** — [Mirabelli Answering Brief pp.15–18](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/63d954d4e4ad424df7819d46/6a905f0d4fa78dfe6c216b9b_260826-Mirabelli.pdf)
- Withdrawal/replacement: CDE's current page states "This guidance replaces (1) Frequently Asked Questions: School Success and Opportunity Act (Assembly Bill 1266) and (2) Legal Advisory… The guidance itself is non-binding. Local educational agencies must comply with statutes and regulations." The Supreme Court notes that "In 2025, defendants asserted that the guidance documents… were no longer operative," but "the State had promulgated a new required training curriculum [PRISM] that similarly directed teachers not to tell parents… without the children's consent," after which defendants withdrew their mootness argument. — [CDE AB 1955 page](https://www.cde.ca.gov/ci/pl/ab-1955-sum-of-prov.asp); [Mirabelli slip op. 3](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25a810_b97d.pdf)
- AB 1955 (SAFETY Act; signed July 15, 2024; eff. Jan. 1, 2025): Educ. Code §220.3(a): a school employee/contractor "shall not be required to disclose any information related to a pupil's sexual orientation, gender identity, or gender expression to any other person without the pupil's consent unless otherwise required by state or federal law"; §220.5(a): LEAs "shall not enact or enforce any policy, rule, or administrative regulation that would require an employee or a contractor to disclose" such information without the pupil's consent; §220.5(c) voids inconsistent policies; §220.1 bars retaliation against employees who support pupils' rights. Both §220.3(b) and §220.5(b) say the rule "does not constitute a change in, but is declaratory of, existing law." — [§220.3](https://california.public.law/codes/education_code_section_220.3); [§220.5](https://california.public.law/codes/education_code_section_220.5); [§220.1](https://california.public.law/codes/education_code_section_220.1); [CDE AB 1955 page](https://www.cde.ca.gov/ci/pl/ab-1955-sum-of-prov.asp)
- Reading of AB 1955 by the district court: it "prohibits forced disclosure by teachers" but does not itself forbid a willing employee from disclosing; the State nonetheless relied on "existing law" (privacy/anti-discrimination provisions and guidance). — [Mirabelli MSJ order pp.8–9](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/63d954d4e4ad424df7819d46/6949e11ea6bae817c8eaf637_Dkt%20%20307_MSJ%20Order.pdf)
- Current status of those rules vis-à-vis objecting parents: enjoined in part (Mirabelli injunction as to parent subclasses; Huntington Beach injunction as to seven plaintiff parents for §§220.3(a), 220.5(a)); mandatory-outing district policies still held unlawful by the San Bernardino court (Sept. 2026). — sources in §§1 and 5 above
- Family Code §6924(b) (as amended by AB 665, operative July 1, 2024): "A minor who is 12 years of age or older may consent to mental health treatment or counseling on an outpatient basis, or to residential shelter services, if the minor, in the opinion of the attending professional person, is mature enough to participate intelligently"; (d): treatment "shall include involvement of the minor's parent or guardian unless the professional person… after consulting with the minor, determines that the involvement would be inappropriate," with the contact attempt or reason recorded; (f): "This section does not authorize a minor to receive convulsive therapy or psychosurgery… or psychotropic drugs without the consent of the minor's parent or guardian"; (g): "operative on July 1, 2024." — [Fam. Code §6924](https://california.public.law/codes/family_code_section_6924)
- Health & Safety Code §124260(b)(1): "a minor who is 12 years of age or older may consent to mental health treatment or counseling services if, in the opinion of the attending professional person, the minor is mature enough to participate intelligently"; "professional person" includes "A credentialed school psychologist, as described in Section 49424 of the Education Code"; (c) parental involvement unless inappropriate; (e) "does not authorize a minor to receive convulsive treatment or psychosurgery… or psychotropic drugs without the consent of the minor's parent or guardian." — [H&S Code §124260](https://california.public.law/codes/health_and_safety_code_section_124260)
- Family Code §6920: minors may consent only "to the matters provided in this chapter." §6922(a): a minor may consent to "medical care, vision care, or dental care" only if "15 years of age or older," "living separate and apart from the minor's parents or guardian," and "managing the minor's own financial affairs." §7050(e)(1): an emancipated minor may "Consent to medical, dental, or psychiatric care, without parental consent, knowledge, or liability." §6911: a court "may summarily grant consent for medical care" for a minor 16+ who "has no parent or guardian available to give the consent." — [§6920](https://california.public.law/codes/family_code_section_6920); [§6922](https://california.public.law/codes/family_code_section_6922); [§7050](https://california.public.law/codes/family_code_section_7050); [§6911](https://california.public.law/codes/family_code_section_6911)
- Dependency context (relevant to Martinez): Welf. & Inst. Code §369(b)–(c): where "there is no parent, guardian… capable of authorizing or willing to authorize" care for a child under juvenile-court petition or a dependent child, the court "upon the written recommendation of a licensed physician… and after due notice to the parent… may make an order authorizing the performance of the necessary medical, surgical, dental, or other remedial care," or order that the social worker may authorize it. — [WIC §369](https://california.public.law/codes/welfare_and_institutions_code_section_369)

### Inferences
- **Can a California minor consent alone to puberty blockers or hormones?** On the statutes reviewed, no general authority exists: the enumerated minor-consent provisions in Family Code ch. 3 (§§6920–6930) do not include gender-related medication; the counseling provisions (§6924; H&S §124260) stop at outpatient counseling and exclude psychotropic drugs; independent consent to "medical care" requires §6922 self-sufficiency (15+), emancipation (§7050), or a court order (§6911; WIC §369 for dependents). This is an inference from statutory text — I found no statute or case stating the rule in terms for gender medication.
- A school counselor or credentialed school psychologist counseling a 12+ student about gender identity without first obtaining parental consent is, in principle, within §6924/§124260 (subject to the parental-involvement default and documentation duty); that statutory authorization covers *counseling*, not name/pronoun changes in school records or any medical intervention. This matters for categorizing Regino (A.S. was 11 — below the statutory age) and Konen (A.G. was ~11–12 at the relevant times): for the under-12 periods "applicable law allowed the minor to consent independently" does **not** apply even to counseling.
- The Mirabelli defense expert's testimony that gender-clinic access requires a caregiver is consistent with this legal structure.

### Gaps
- Official leginfo.legislature.ca.gov pages returned 403; statutory text was taken from the california.public.law mirror (which reports verification against leginfo in Nov. 2026). AB 1955's chaptered bill text and AB 665's chapter citation were not pulled from the official site.
- The exact date CDE took down the 2016 FAQ/Legal Advisory, and the text of the PRISM training, were not obtained.
- Not researched: SB 107 (2022), AB 957 (2023, vetoed), Medi-Cal/EPSDT minor-consent regulations, or Health & Safety/insurance confidentiality rules (e.g., AB 1184) that affect whether parents learn of sensitive services.
