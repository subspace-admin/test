# Non-California school incidents: social transition of a minor at school with information withheld from parents, or continued over a parent's objection

Research date: 2026-11-18. "Status checked through" dates are given per case with the date of the source that supports them. Scope: U.S. cases outside California. Medical treatment is kept separate from social transition throughout; each case entry states whether any medication, surgery, device or medical referral is alleged.

Method notes for the report writer:
- Primary documents were downloaded and read directly where possible (court opinions from govinfo.gov, court websites, supremecourt.gov dockets and briefs, CourtListener dockets). Where a fact comes only from a party's page or a news summary, the entry says so.
- Evidence labels used: **established by records** / **corroborated account** / **sworn but contested allegation** / **reported allegation** / **unresolved**. Most federal appellate opinions below were decided on motions to dismiss, so their fact sections are the complaint's allegations assumed true; they are not findings. This is flagged in each entry.
- The four-element test applied to every incident: (1) school or school employee arranged or materially facilitated; (2) gender-related medication or surgery actually prescribed, dispensed or administered; (3) relevant parent or guardian unaware or had not consented; (4) no prior court order authorized it.
- The session's web-search budget ran out before every item could be re-searched. Items affected are listed under Gaps. Direct document fetches and docket lookups continued after that point.

## 1. For each case: what did school staff do, when, at whose request; what were parents told; did the parent object and did the conduct continue; what did the district say; what has a court actually found?

### Takeaway
In every case examined, the school conduct at issue is social (name, pronouns, counseling, support plans, bathroom access); no case alleges that a school arranged medication or surgery, and only one (Lavigne, Maine) alleges a device (a chest binder), which no court has found to have been given. Only two non-California cases have record-based judicial rulings on the facts, and they point in opposite directions: Kettle Moraine (Wisconsin, 2023, judgment for parents on undisputed affidavits) and Willey (Wyoming, 2025, summary judgment for the district, with the court finding no evidence of concealment or lying).

#### A. Cases with a concrete individual child

**A1. Foote v. Ludlow School Committee (Massachusetts)**
- Location and institution: Baird Middle School, Ludlow Public Schools, Ludlow, MA. Child "B.F.", age 11, sixth grade, 2020–21 school year. — [First Circuit opinion, 128 F.4th 336, Feb. 18, 2025, Part I.A](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca1-23-01069/pdf/USCOURTS-ca1-23-01069-0.pdf)
- Timeline as alleged: December 2020, the student told teacher Bonnie Manchester about depression and self-image problems; Manchester called the mother. The mother then emailed the principal (Stacy Monette), superintendent (Todd Gazda), School Committee members and all the student's teachers: "we request that you do not have any private conversations with [the Student] in regards to this matter. Please allow us to address this as a family and with the proper professionals." — [First Circuit opinion, Part I.A](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca1-23-01069/pdf/USCOURTS-ca1-23-01069-0.pdf)
- School action as alleged: on Feb. 28, 2021 the student emailed teachers, counselor Marie-Claire Foley and the superintendent: "I am genderqueer", asking to use a new first name and "any pronouns (other than it/its)". Foley met the student privately, then "directed Baird staff to use the name 'B***' and she/her pronouns when communicating with the Student's parents, but during school times, to address the Student as 'R***'." Teachers changed nametags; librarian Jordan Funke spoke one-on-one about gender identity; Foley told the student they could choose the boys', girls' or gender-neutral bathroom. — [First Circuit opinion, Part I.A](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca1-23-01069/pdf/USCOURTS-ca1-23-01069-0.pdf)
- Discovery and objection: in early March 2021 the parents learned of the school name from teacher Manchester and met the principal and superintendent that month. Staff continued: a teacher mailed a card addressed to the new name in April 2021, and Foley corresponded with the student by text and chat through April–May 2021 and encouraged weekly meetings. — [First Circuit opinion, Part I.C](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca1-23-01069/pdf/USCOURTS-ca1-23-01069-0.pdf)
- District response: Superintendent Gazda defended staff, said they complied with Massachusetts law and the state education department (DESE) guidance, and at a May 2021 School Committee meeting said the district would "continue to help . . . children 'express who they are' despite parents' wishes to the contrary." — [First Circuit opinion, Part I.C](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca1-23-01069/pdf/USCOURTS-ca1-23-01069-0.pdf). In the Supreme Court the school board and officials said the alleged unwritten "Protocol" does not exist and that staff were implementing state guidance in response to the student's requests. — [CBS News, Apr. 20, 2026](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/supreme-court-parental-rights-child-gender-transition-massaschusetts/)
- Applicable rule at the time: DESE's non-binding 2012 guidance said staff "should speak with [a] student first before discussing [that] student's gender nonconformity or transgender status with the student's parent or guardian." No Massachusetts statute required notification. — [First Circuit opinion, Part I.B](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca1-23-01069/pdf/USCOURTS-ca1-23-01069-0.pdf)
- Rulings and scope: district court dismissed under Rule 12(b)(6) (2022 WL 18356421). First Circuit affirmed. It held the challenged conduct was a policy (legislative, not executive), that the parents identified a fundamental right with enough specificity, but that the "Protocol of nondisclosure as to a student's at-school gender expression without the student's consent does not restrict parental rights in a way courts have recognized as a violation of the guarantees of substantive due process." It also rejected the theory that using a name and pronouns is medical treatment. No court made findings of fact. — [First Circuit opinion, conclusion, pp. 45–46](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca1-23-01069/pdf/USCOURTS-ca1-23-01069-0.pdf)
- Supreme Court: No. 25-77. Distributed for conference repeatedly from Dec. 2025; petitioners filed a letter on Mirabelli on Mar. 19, 2026; "Petition DENIED" Apr. 20, 2026, with no noted dissent and no GVR. — [Supreme Court docket 25-77](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-77.html); [Volokh Conspiracy, Apr. 20, 2026](https://reason.com/volokh/2026/04/20/after-mirabelli-the-other-foote-did-not-drop/)
- Parental involvement category: parents not notified of the name/pronoun change; staff allegedly used the birth name with parents (affirmative concealment alleged); conduct continued after parental objection. The December 2020 email objected to private conversations about mental health, not to a name change that had not yet been requested.
- Medical: no medication, surgery, device or medical referral alleged. The parents' theory that social transition is itself "mental health treatment" was rejected by the court.
- Element test: (1) yes, as to social transition only; (2) no; (3) yes as alleged; (4) no court order. Fails element 2.
- Evidence label: **corroborated account**. The source is an unsworn complaint assumed true, but the respondents' own filing accepts that staff acted on the student's requests under state guidance. The existence of an unwritten district "Protocol" is disputed. Never tested in discovery.
- Organizations (documented relationship): Child & Parental Rights Campaign (Vernadette Broyles, Mary McAlister) and Massachusetts Family Institute as counsel on appeal; Alliance Defending Freedom as amicus on appeal and counsel at the cert stage; amici for the district side included ACLU, GLBTQ Legal Advocates & Defenders (for the Massachusetts Association of School Superintendents), Lambda Legal (for PFLAG and others) and Massachusetts plus 15 other jurisdictions; Montana plus 18 states for the parents. — [First Circuit opinion, counsel listing](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca1-23-01069/pdf/USCOURTS-ca1-23-01069-0.pdf); [ADF case page](https://adflegal.org/case/foote-v-ludlow-school-committee/)
- Status checked through: 2026-04-20 (docket).

**A2. Littlejohn v. School Board of Leon County (Florida)**
- Location and institution: Deerlake Middle School, Leon County Schools, Tallahassee, FL. Child age 13, 2020–21 school year. — [Eleventh Circuit opinion, No. 23-10385, Mar. 12, 2025, pp. 3–6](https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/files/202310385.pdf)
- School action as alleged: before the school year the child asked to use they/them pronouns and a "male" name, J. The opinion recites that the parents "did not allow their child to use a different name or pronouns, though they permitted the child to use 'J.' as a 'nickname' at school", and that the mother asked the teacher not to use a different name or pronouns. The child told counselor Rachel Thomas they wanted the name and pronouns. Thomas and other staff met with the child and developed a "Transgender/Gender Nonconforming Student Support Plan" without notifying the parents, because the child did not request their presence. The plan recorded that the parents were "aware, but not supportive". — [Eleventh Circuit opinion, pp. 3–5](https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/files/202310385.pdf)
- What parents were told: Thomas and Assistant Principal Robin Oliveri told the mother she was not invited because "by law" the child had to request parental attendance; Assistant Superintendent Kathleen Rodgers emailed, "We currently do not have any Florida specific law that obligates us to inform the parents or says we cannot listen to the student without their parent present." — [Eleventh Circuit opinion, pp. 5–6](https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/files/202310385.pdf)
- Applicable rule at the time: the district's 2018 LGBTQ+ Support Guide told staff not to notify parents when a student's behavior suggested the student was LGBTQ+ ("Outing a student, especially to parents, can be very dangerous"). Florida's Parents' Bill of Rights was enacted in 2021, after the events; the board adopted a revised Guide in June 2022 stating staff "must not intentionally withhold information from parents" absent a reasonable belief that disclosure would result in abuse, abandonment or neglect. — [Eleventh Circuit opinion, pp. 4–6 and n.2](https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/files/202310385.pdf)
- Rulings and scope: N.D. Fla. dismissed in December 2022. Eleventh Circuit affirmed 2–1 on Mar. 12, 2025 (132 F.4th 1232): the officials' actions were executive, so the "shocks the conscience" test applied, and the conduct did not meet it as a matter of law. Facts were recounted "in the light most favorable to the Littlejohns"; there are no findings of fact. Rehearing en banc was denied July 17, 2025. — [Eleventh Circuit opinion, p. 3 and n.1](https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/files/202310385.pdf); [WFSU, July 21, 2025](https://news.wfsu.org/wfsu-local-news/2025-07-21/a-federal-appeals-court-wont-reconsider-a-leon-school-gender-case)
- Supreme Court: No. 25-259. Both sides filed supplemental briefs on Mirabelli on Mar. 9, 2026; petitioners asked for a GVR. The board answered that petitioners brought no First Amendment claim, that Mirabelli did not address the executive-conduct question, and that the board had already replaced the old policy. "Petition DENIED" Apr. 27, 2026; no statement or dissent. — [Supreme Court docket 25-259](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-259.html); [Respondents' supplemental brief, Mar. 9, 2026](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/25/25-259/400385/20260309141316956_25-259%20LCSD%20Supplemental%20Brief.pdf)
- Contradicting record: see Question 3 below (the mother's August 2020 emails).
- Parental involvement category: disputed. Parents were aware of the child's requested name and pronouns before the school acted; they were not notified of, or invited to, the support-plan meeting; whether the mother objected to or acquiesced in the name use is contested between the complaint and her own emails.
- Medical: none alleged.
- Element test: (1) yes, social only; (2) no; (3) disputed (aware of the request, not of the plan meeting); (4) no court order. Fails element 2.
- Evidence label: **corroborated account** for the support-plan meeting held without the parents (the plan was later given to the family and the district's explanations are quoted); **disputed** for the claim that the parents were unaware or had forbidden the name.
- Organizations: Child & Parental Rights Campaign as counsel; Florida and 19 other states as amici supporting en banc review. — [WFSU, July 21, 2025](https://news.wfsu.org/wfsu-local-news/2025-07-21/a-federal-appeals-court-wont-reconsider-a-leon-school-gender-case)
- Status checked through: 2026-04-27 (docket); [WFSU, Apr. 28, 2026](https://news.wfsu.org/wfsu-local-news/2026-04-28/the-u-s-supreme-court-wont-consider-a-leon-school-gender-case).

**A3. Lavigne v. Great Salt Bay Community School Board (Maine)**
- Location and institution: Great Salt Bay Community School (K–8), Damariscotta, ME. Child "A.B.", age 13, December 2022. — [First Circuit opinion, No. 24-1509, July 28, 2025](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca1-24-01509/pdf/USCOURTS-ca1-24-01509-0.pdf)
- School action as alleged: school social worker Samuel Roy gave A.B. a chest binder ("a device used to flatten a female's chest so as to appear male") and a second binder the same day; some staff used a different name and pronouns at A.B.'s request. The allegation that Roy said he would not tell the parents is pleaded "on information and belief". — [First Circuit opinion, pp. 3–5](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca1-24-01509/pdf/USCOURTS-ca1-24-01509-0.pdf); [Brief in opposition, Feb. 23, 2026, pp. 4–5](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/25/25-759/397047/20260223142651745_25-759BriefInOpposition.pdf)
- Discovery, objection, intervention: the mother found the binder while cleaning A.B.'s room in December 2022; met the principal and superintendent on Dec. 5, 2022, who she says expressed sympathy; the superintendent later told her no policy was violated; she withdrew A.B. on Dec. 12, 2022 and addressed the board on Dec. 14, 2022. — [Brief in opposition, pp. 5–6](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/25/25-759/397047/20260223142651745_25-759BriefInOpposition.pdf)
- District response: board statements of Dec. 19, 2022 and Jan. 14, 2023. The second cited Maine law giving minors the right to mental-health services without parental consent (22 MRSA § 1502) and a confidential counseling relationship with school-based providers (20-A MRSA § 4008), and said "neither the Board nor school administration are aware of any violation of policy or law". The district's written Transgender Student Guidelines require a parent or guardian at the planning meeting; the district denies any unwritten withholding policy. The district notes the mother does not allege she ever asked the school for the information, nor how long the school had known. — [Brief in opposition, pp. 3–7](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/25/25-759/397047/20260223142651745_25-759BriefInOpposition.pdf)
- Rulings and scope: D. Me. dismissed May 3, 2024; First Circuit affirmed July 28, 2025, solely because the complaint did not plausibly allege a board custom or policy of withholding (Monell). Neither court decided whether a constitutional right was violated, and neither found whether a binder was given. The individual defendants were dismissed without objection because they were sued in official capacity only. — [First Circuit opinion, pp. 3, 25–26](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca1-24-01509/pdf/USCOURTS-ca1-24-01509-0.pdf); [D. Me. order, May 3, 2024](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-med-2_23-cv-00158/pdf/USCOURTS-med-2_23-cv-00158-0.pdf)
- Supreme Court: No. 25-759; response requested Jan. 22, 2026; "Petition DENIED" Mar. 30, 2026; no statement or dissent. — [Supreme Court docket 25-759](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-759.html)
- Parental involvement category: parent not notified (alleged). The board invoked statutes under which a Maine minor may consent independently to mental-health services; those statutes concern counseling, not provision of a garment.
- Medical: a device (chest binder) is alleged; no medication, surgery or medical referral is alleged.
- Element test: (1) yes as alleged; (2) no (a binder is neither medication nor surgery); (3) yes as alleged; (4) no court order. Fails element 2.
- Evidence label: **reported allegation**, unresolved. Unsworn complaint; the district has neither confirmed nor specifically denied the binder allegation in the documents reviewed.
- Organizations: Goldwater Institute as counsel; ADF and Child & Parental Rights Campaign as amici; South Carolina and 11 other states as amici. — [First Circuit opinion, counsel listing](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca1-24-01509/pdf/USCOURTS-ca1-24-01509-0.pdf)
- Status checked through: 2026-03-30.

**A4. T.F. and B.F. v. Kettle Moraine School District (Wisconsin)**
- Location and institution: Kettle Moraine Middle School, Waukesha County, WI. Child "A.F.", described by the parents' counsel as then 12. — [ADF case page](https://adfmedia.org/case/tf-and-bf-v-kettle-moraine-school-district/)
- Court's findings of fact (summary judgment, on affidavits and the district's interrogatory answers): A.F. began questioning her gender identity; her parents placed her in a mental health center that "focused on 'affirming'" the identity; she returned to school "with some parental support for the identity", but the parents "ultimately changed course and went to the school informing them they wished to refer to A.F. by her legal name and female pronouns"; "The District replied by saying they will follow the guidance of A.F. in what pronouns to use, even over parental objections"; the parents withdrew A.F., who shortly afterwards "concluded that she did not want to transition genders." — [Decision and Order, Oct. 3, 2023, Findings 1–5](https://assets.adflegal.org/archive/adfm/TF-Decision.pdf)
- Actors per the findings: principal Mike Comiskey, school psychologist Allison Beyerl, director of student services Charles Wiza, superintendent Pat Deklotz and board president Gary Vose gave input; Deklotz "was the individual ultimately responsible for deciding to affirm A.F.'s preferred gender identity over the objection of her parents." The district "acknowledges that they affirmed A.F.'s social transition because of advice from A.F.'s therapist and worry over a potential Title IX violation." — [Decision and Order, Findings 21–23](https://assets.adflegal.org/archive/adfm/TF-Decision.pdf)
- Ruling and scope: Waukesha County Circuit Judge Michael Maxwell granted the parents summary judgment and denied the district's. Declared that the district's practice "to enable and affirm a minor student's transition to a different gender identity at school without parental consent violates parents' constitutional right to determine the appropriate medical and healthcare for their children", that it violated T.F. and B.F.'s rights, and enjoined the district "from allowing or requiring staff to refer to students using a name or pronouns at odds with the student's biological sex, while at school, without express parental consent." The court treated social transition as a medical or healthcare decision, relying on the parents' expert affidavits (Dr. Erica Anderson, Dr. Stephen Levine), which the district did not rebut with opposing evidence. A second couple (P.W. and S.W.) with no affected child was also held to have standing. — [Decision and Order, pp. 18–19](https://assets.adflegal.org/archive/adfm/TF-Decision.pdf)
- District position: no justiciable controversy because the family had left the district; handled such matters case by case. — [Decision and Order, p. 1](https://assets.adflegal.org/archive/adfm/TF-Decision.pdf); [WPR, Oct. 2023](https://www.wpr.org/wisconsin-judge-blocks-school-districts-policy-allowing-students-choose-pronouns)
- Parental involvement category: parents were aware; **conduct (the district's decision to use the student's chosen name) over explicit parental objection**. This is not a concealment case. The record indicates the parents withdrew the child in response to the district's stated position, so the period of actual use over objection appears short or nil.
- Medical: none by the school. The court characterized social transition as healthcare; no medication, surgery, device or referral by the school is found or alleged.
- Element test: (1) yes, social only; (2) no; (3) parents aware, had withdrawn consent; (4) no court order. Fails element 2.
- Evidence label: **established by records** (judicial findings on an undisputed summary-judgment record, including the district's own admissions). It is a trial-court judgment, not appellate precedent.
- Organizations: Wisconsin Institute for Law & Liberty and ADF as counsel. — [WPR](https://www.wpr.org/wisconsin-judge-blocks-school-districts-policy-allowing-students-choose-pronouns)
- Status checked through: 2023-10-03 for the judgment; ADF's page lists a March 2024 "Resolution" document without describing it.

**A5. Blair v. Appomattox County School Board (Virginia; "Sage Blair", S.B.)**
- Location and institution: Appomattox County High School, VA. S.B., age 14, ninth grade; events Aug. 10–25, 2021. Plaintiff is the paternal grandmother and adoptive mother. — [Fourth Circuit opinion, No. 24-1682, Aug. 7, 2025, pp. 3–8](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca4-24-01682/pdf/USCOURTS-ca4-24-01682-0.pdf)
- School action as alleged: on Aug. 11, 2021 counselor Dena Olsen, after a teacher overheard S.B. tell a friend she wanted a male name, asked S.B. in the hallway whether she identified as a boy or girl and told her that "if she identified as a boy, she could use the male restroom at school." On Aug. 12 S.B. told Olsen and contract counselor Avery Via she wanted the name "D" and he/him pronouns. Staff used the male name "consistently". Olsen called S.B. to the counseling office about gender identity "eight times during the 12 days school had been in session". "A few days later" Olsen told S.B. to use only the boys' bathroom because girls were uncomfortable; on Aug. 24 she was moved to the nurse's restroom after reports from other parents. — [Fourth Circuit opinion, pp. 5–7](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca4-24-01682/pdf/USCOURTS-ca4-24-01682-0.pdf)
- What the guardian was told: on Aug. 12 Olsen told Blair only that there had been an "incident" on the bus. S.B. "asked Olsen not to use that name with Blair because 'it might upset her.' Accordingly, Olsen referred to S.B. as 'S' throughout her interaction with Blair." The counselors "claimed that S.B. told them that her parents were not supportive of her gender identity." — [Fourth Circuit opinion, p. 6](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca4-24-01682/pdf/USCOURTS-ca4-24-01682-0.pdf)
- School conduct versus later events: the school-conduct allegations cover two weeks and include bus threats, hallway and bathroom harassment and assaults, and an Aug. 25 meeting in which Olsen and a school resource officer warned S.B. about "defamation of character". The complaint then alleges S.B. ran away, "was abducted by sex traffickers", was in the custody of the Maryland Department of Juvenile Services for at least two months, ran away again and was trafficked again. The trafficking was not done by school staff. — [Fourth Circuit opinion, pp. 7–9](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca4-24-01682/pdf/USCOURTS-ca4-24-01682-0.pdf)
- Rulings and scope: W.D. Va. dismissed. Fourth Circuit (Gregory, joined by Benjamin; Wilkinson dissenting in part) affirmed dismissal of the three substantive due process (parental-rights) claims and the Monell claim, and reversed only on the Title IX deliberate-indifference claim against the school board. All facts are complaint allegations. — [Fourth Circuit opinion, pp. 2–3](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca4-24-01682/pdf/USCOURTS-ca4-24-01682-0.pdf)
- Current posture: on remand, discovery closed Nov. 2, 2026; summary-judgment and exclusion motions were filed Nov. 16, 2026; the case was referred for mediation Nov. 18, 2026; jury trial is set for Mar. 1–5, 2027 before Senior Judge Norman K. Moon. — [CourtListener docket, W.D. Va.](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/67718224/blair-v-appomattox-county-school-board/)
- Parental involvement category: guardians not notified (alleged); staff allegedly used the birth name with the guardian at the student's request; no opportunity to object during the two weeks.
- Medical: none alleged against the school (a private psychiatric evaluation diagnosing gender dysphoria was arranged by the family).
- Element test: (1) yes as alleged, social and facility access; (2) no; (3) yes as alleged; (4) no court order. Fails element 2.
- Evidence label: **reported allegation**, unresolved; a fact record now exists in discovery but no ruling on it was found.
- Organizations: Child & Parental Rights Campaign as counsel. — [CPRC press page, Sept. 8, 2025](https://cprc-site.pages.dev/happening/cprcs-historic-title-ix-victory-holding-schools-accountable-for-student-safety)
- Status checked through: 2026-11-18 (docket).

**A6. Mead v. Rockford Public School District (Michigan)**
- Location and institution: Rockford middle school, Rockford Public Schools, MI. Child "G.M.", seventh into eighth grade, 2022. — [Order granting reconsideration, W.D. Mich. No. 1:23-cv-1313, Aug. 7, 2026, pp. 1–3](https://adfmedia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/meadmtrgrant.pdf)
- School action as alleged: "In the final months of seventh grade ... the Meads' child messaged a guidance counselor, requesting that teachers no longer use the name G.M. and instead use a male name, F.M. School staff complied". "The school did not tell the Meads about their child's request." Legal paperwork sent home had to use the legal name under a separate policy. The parents learned when they "received notes from a teacher that referred to their child using a masculine name", then withdrew the child. The court states these facts are "a summary of the Meads' allegations". — [Order, pp. 2–3 and n.1](https://adfmedia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/meadmtrgrant.pdf)
- Parents' fuller account (party source): staff began in May or August 2022; in October 2022 the parents found the masculine name in a document an employee "had only partially altered" before sharing it. — [ADF case page](https://adflegal.org/case/mead-v-rockford-public-school-district/)
- Applicable rule: district policy modeled on Michigan State Board of Education guidance, which says disclosure to parents of a student who is not out at home "should be made on a case-by-case basis". — [Order, pp. 2–3](https://adfmedia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/meadmtrgrant.pdf)
- Rulings and scope: motion to dismiss granted in part in 2025; the due process claim proceeded and the free exercise claim was dismissed. On Aug. 7, 2026 Judge Paul Maloney granted reconsideration and reinstated the free exercise claim, reasoning that Mirabelli "arose from a challenge to a substantially similar school district policy" and that the district's discretionary-notification policy "cut" parents out. This is a pleading-stage ruling. — [Order, pp. 7–8](https://adfmedia.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/meadmtrgrant.pdf)
- Outcome: the Meads voluntarily dismissed in September 2026. The district said "This was not a settlement", that it "did not pay the plaintiffs any money", and that staff never lied to the parents or changed any records, adding it was "confident that the evidence would have proven this." — [ADF case page](https://adflegal.org/case/mead-v-rockford-public-school-district/); [FOX 17](https://www.fox17online.com/news/local-news/rockford-cedar-springs-sparta/lawsuit-against-rockford-public-schools-over-students-gender-identity-dismissed)
- Parental involvement category: parents not notified (alleged). Whether records were altered to conceal is disputed.
- Medical: none alleged.
- Element test: (1) yes, social only; (2) no; (3) yes as alleged; (4) no court order. Fails element 2.
- Evidence label: **reported allegation**; concealment and record-alteration contested by the district; never adjudicated.
- Organizations: ADF as counsel.
- Status checked through: September 2026 (ADF page; FOX 17 article undated in the fetched text).

**A7. Vitsaxaki v. Skaneateles Central School District (New York)**
- Location and institution: Skaneateles middle school, NY. Child "Doe"/"Jane", age 12, seventh grade, 2020–21. — [N.D.N.Y. Memorandum-Decision and Order, Mar. 20, 2025, pp. 5–11](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-nynd-5_24-cv-00155/pdf/USCOURTS-nynd-5_24-cv-00155-0.pdf)

**A17. Doe v. Manchester School District (New Hampshire)**

#### B. Policy and standing cases with no individual incident

- **Doe v. Madison Metropolitan School District (Wisconsin).** Parents filed Feb. 18, 2020 in Dane County against district guidance. The Wisconsin Supreme Court's 2022 decision (2022 WI 65) concerned the parents' request to litigate anonymously and did not reach the merits. On remand the circuit court dismissed for lack of standing on Nov. 23, 2022, finding the remaining plaintiff had no evidence the policy harmed her. The appeal was voluntarily dismissed June 30, 2023 after she withdrew her child. No court ruled the policy lawful or unlawful. Record as policy/standing; no incident. — [Civil Rights Litigation Clearinghouse](https://clearinghouse.net/case/43978/); [ADF case page](https://adflegal.org/case/doe-v-madison-metropolitan-school-district); [WILL case page](https://will-law.org/doe-v-mmsd/)
- **Parents Protecting Our Children v. Eau Claire Area School District (Wisconsin).** Seventh Circuit affirmed dismissal for lack of standing on Mar. 7, 2024: a pre-enforcement facial attack "without identifying any instance of the School District applying the policy". Cert denied Dec. 9, 2024 (No. 23-1280); Justice Kavanaugh would have granted; Justice Alito, joined by Justice Thomas, dissented. — [Seventh Circuit opinion](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca7-23-01534/pdf/USCOURTS-ca7-23-01534-0.pdf); [Supreme Court docket 23-1280](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/23-1280.html)
- **John and Jane Parents 1 v. Montgomery County Board of Education (Maryland).** Fourth Circuit, Aug. 14, 2023 (Quattlebaum, joined by Rushing; Niemeyer dissenting): vacated and remanded for lack of standing; the parents did not allege their own children had gender support plans. Cert denied May 20, 2024 (No. 23-601). — [Fourth Circuit opinion, No. 22-2034](https://www.ca4.uscourts.gov/opinions/222034.P.pdf); [Supreme Court docket 23-601](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/23-601.html)
- **Parents Defending Education v. Linn-Mar Community School District (Iowa).** Eighth Circuit opinion filed Sept. 29, 2023 on an association's challenge to district policy after denial of a preliminary injunction. Policy case; no individual child. — [Eighth Circuit opinion, No. 22-2927](https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/23/09/222927P.pdf)
- **New Jersey Attorney General v. Hanover Township, Middletown, Marlboro and Manalapan-Englishtown boards.** These run the other way: the state sued in 2023 to block board policies that required notifying parents (Hanover) or rescinded the state-model policy (the three Monmouth County districts). Trial judges enjoined the policies pending Division on Civil Rights proceedings. The Appellate Division affirmed in two rulings the week of Feb. 10, 2025, stating that no statutes "impose affirmative requirements of parental notification concerning a student's gender identity or expression" and that case law "has not extended this right to require schools to affirmatively provide parents with information". Middletown then repealed its policy. No individual child. — [InsiderNJ, Feb. 12, 2025](https://www.insidernj.com/the-bottom-line-in-the-state-appellate-division-ruling); [New Jersey Monitor, Feb. 14, 2025](https://newjerseymonitor.com/2025/02/14/court-battle-over-policies-on-transgender-students-leads-to-ruling-and-repeal/); [New Jersey Monitor, Oct. 2, 2023](https://newjerseymonitor.com/2023/10/02/hanover-schools-parental-notification-policy-enjoined-by-judge/)
- **Kaltenbach (other parents)** — dismissed for lack of standing, as above.
- **School District of the Chathams (New Jersey).** Thomas More Society reportedly sued on Aug. 24, 2026 for Moms for Liberty and two anonymous parents after Mirabelli. Single low-quality source; not verified against a docket. — [Washington Digest](https://washingtondigest.com/new-jersey-school-district-sued-for-hiding-student-gender-transitions-from-parents-after-supreme-court-rulings/)

#### C. Teacher cases (no parent plaintiff; policy evidence only)

- **Ricard v. USD 475 Geary County (Kansas).** After an evidentiary hearing on May 6, 2022, the court made "factual findings based on the record" and on May 9, 2022 preliminarily enjoined the district's "Communication with Parents Policy", which barred a teacher from using a student's preferred name and pronouns with parents unless the student asked. The court denied relief on the name-and-pronoun policy. The case settled Aug. 31, 2022 for $95,000 in damages and fees, with a statement that the teacher retired in good standing; the board revoked the parent-communication policy. This is judicial confirmation that a withholding policy existed, not a finding about any particular child. — [D. Kan. order, May 9, 2022](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ksd-5_22-cv-04015/pdf/USCOURTS-ksd-5_22-cv-04015-0.pdf); [Kansas Public Radio](https://kansaspublicradio.org/kpr-news/teacher-receives-95000-after-refusing-use-students-preferred-name-and-pronouns); [ADF](https://adflegal.org/article/kansas-school-board-scraps-policy-after-adf-lawsuit/)
- **Figliola v. School Board of the City of Harrisonburg (Virginia).** Three teachers sued in June 2022 under Virginia's constitution and religious-freedom statute. Settled and dismissed December 2024 with no merits ruling. ADF says the board agreed the teachers need not ask for or use pronouns inconsistent with sex; the board said it never required that, does not support withholding information from parents, and that "nothing really changes". — [The Harrisonburg Citizen, Dec. 12, 2024](https://hburgcitizen.com/2024/12/12/lawsuit-against-city-schools-over-teacher-training-ends-after-2-years/); [ADF case page](https://adflegal.org/case/figliola-v-school-board-city-harrisonburg/)
- **Polk v. Montgomery County Public Schools (Maryland).** Substitute teacher's free exercise and compelled-speech challenge to a policy requiring preferred pronouns and withholding from parents. Cert petition No. 26-8 is pending (see Question 5).

### Inferences
- No case in this collection satisfies all four elements of the test. Every one fails element 2: no source alleges that a school or school employee arranged gender-related medication or surgery that was actually prescribed, dispensed or administered. The nearest allegations are a chest binder (Lavigne, a device, unproven) and club literature listing a gender clinic (Vitsaxaki, not a referral).
- The pattern most often documented in a school's own paperwork is not "secret transition" in general but one specific practice: staff instructed to use the birth name with parents and the chosen name at school (Foote's counselor directive; Vitsaxaki's Gender Support Plan; Blair; Landerer's principal). Of these, only Vitsaxaki's is supported by a district document in the court record.
- Documented continuation over an explicit parental objection is rarer than non-notification. It is record-established in Kettle Moraine and Delaware Valley, sworn in Vitsaxaki, and alleged in Foote, Landerer and Mesa.
- Federal appellate rulings against parents before March 2026 mostly avoided the constitutional question (Monell in Lavigne and Lee; standing in Eau Claire and Montgomery; "shocks the conscience" in Littlejohn). Only Foote reached and rejected the parental-rights claim on the merits.

### Gaps
- Kettle Moraine: whether the district appealed, any fee award, and the content of the March 2024 "Resolution" were not verified. The exact dates of the school meeting (reported by counsel as early 2021) were not confirmed from a fetched source.
- Willey: the April 28, 2025 summary-judgment order itself was not read (only the district's brief and press); whether the Tenth Circuit has ruled since March 2026 is unknown.
- Kaltenbach: the summary-judgment opinion was not read; the limitations ground rests on one local report.
- Landerer: settlement terms and any admission of liability not found.
- Vitsaxaki: the Second Circuit summary order's text was not read (paywalled); its content is taken from ADF and one blog.
- Doe v. Manchester and Doe v. Madison: the opinions were not re-read; individual facts in Manchester and the precise 2022 Wisconsin Supreme Court holding should be checked against the opinions.
- Mesa: status after remand unknown.
- "Thomas v. Loudoun County" could not be identified. A separate America First Legal suit by 11 parents against Loudoun's Regulation 8040 was reportedly dismissed for lack of standing ([AFL](https://aflegal.org/?p=6129)), not verified.
- No individual-incident cases with court records were located for Texas, Washington, Oregon or Minnesota; the search budget ran out before state-specific searches could be run.
- No document reviewed ties any of these districts' policies to a GLSEN or Gender Spectrum model policy. The documented policy sources are state agencies (Massachusetts DESE, New Jersey DOE, Michigan State Board of Education) and district committees.

## 3. Which cases involved disputed or contradicted parent accounts?

### Takeaway
Three cases have parent accounts that documents or courts contradicted in part (Littlejohn, Willey, and Delaware Valley on one point), and three more have flat denials by the district that were never adjudicated (Perez, Mead, Lavigne's "unwritten policy"). Contemporaneous reporting contradicting the Perez parents' account, as distinct from the district's denial, could not be located.

### Cited Findings
- **Littlejohn.** Emails obtained by CNN (reported Apr. 6, 2022) and the Tallahassee Democrat show that on Aug. 27, 2020 the mother told a teacher her child was "currently identifying as non-binary", "would like to go by the new name [redacted] and prefers the pronouns they/them", and "If she wants to go by the name [redacted] with her teachers, I won't stop her." Asked whether the teacher could share this with other teachers, she replied "Whatever you think is best or [redacted] can handle it herself" and "I'm going to let her take the lead on this." — [PolitiFact, Mar. 5, 2025](https://politifact.com/article/2025/mar/05/january-littlejohn-story-about-secret-gender-trans/)
- The emails do not contradict the separate allegation that staff held a support-plan meeting in September 2020 without notifying the parents; the district gave the family the plan in November 2020. — [PolitiFact, Mar. 5, 2025](https://politifact.com/article/2025/mar/05/january-littlejohn-story-about-secret-gender-trans/)
- A fact-checking site concluded the school "did not conceal" use of they/them pronouns from the mother and that the emails show consultation. — [Lead Stories, Mar. 2025](https://leadstories.com/hoax-alert/2025/03/fact-check-florida-school-did-not-conceal-use-of-student-they-them-pronouns-from-january-littlejohn-emails-show-consultation.html); see also [Reason, Mar. 5, 2025](https://reason.com/2025/03/05/trumps-trans-kid-story-doesnt-add-up/)
- The Eleventh Circuit's own recitation partly reflects this: the parents "permitted the child to use 'J.' as a 'nickname' at school", and the support plan recorded them as "aware, but not supportive". — [Eleventh Circuit opinion, pp. 3–5](https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/files/202310385.pdf)
- **Willey.** Per the district's brief citing the summary-judgment order: no evidence anyone lied or concealed; teachers volunteered the name when the mother first asked; the court noted the mother's response to the district's statement of facts "misrepresented the record" on one point, being "directly contradicted by her own deposition testimony". — [Appellees' answer brief, pp. 3, 8–9](https://clearinghouse-umich-production.s3.amazonaws.com/media/doc/168271.pdf)
- **Delaware Valley.** The complaint says the counselor "asked Jane if she would like to change her name and pronouns"; the counselor attests under oath that Jane made the request and that she "did not initiate" it. The counselor also denies promising to keep the change from two teachers. — [D.N.J. opinion, pp. 2–3](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-njd-3_24-cv-00107/pdf/USCOURTS-njd-3_24-cv-00107-2.pdf)
- **Perez.** The district called the allegations "completely false, fabricated"; no court has resolved the dispute. — [News4JAX, Jan. 27, 2022](https://www.news4jax.com/news/local/2022/01/27/clay-parents-sue-district-claim-counselor-discussed-elementary-students-gender-identity-without-their-knowledge)
- **Mead.** The district says staff "never lied to the parents or changed any records". — [FOX 17](https://www.fox17online.com/news/local-news/rockford-cedar-springs-sparta/lawsuit-against-rockford-public-schools-over-students-gender-identity-dismissed)
- **Lavigne.** The district says the alleged unwritten withholding policy "does not exist" and points to written guidelines requiring parental involvement; the First Circuit held the complaint did not plausibly allege such a policy. — [Brief in opposition, p. 3](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/25/25-759/397047/20260223142651745_25-759BriefInOpposition.pdf)
- **Foote.** The board says the alleged "Protocol" does not exist as a policy. — [CBS News, Apr. 20, 2026](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/supreme-court-parental-rights-child-gender-transition-massaschusetts/)

- Coverage of the Littlejohn emails traces to two original sources (CNN and the Tallahassee Democrat); the later fact-checks repeat them and are not independent corroboration.

## 5. Effect of Mahmoud v. Taylor, United States v. Skrmetti and the March 2, 2026 Mirabelli order; any Supreme Court grant on school nondisclosure policies

### Takeaway
As of 2026-11-18 no case on school gender-identity nondisclosure policies has been granted for argument in either Supreme Court term, and no GVR in light of Mirabelli was found: Lee, Lavigne, Foote and Littlejohn were all denied outright. Mirabelli's effect has instead been felt in the lower courts (Second Circuit remand in Vitsaxaki, reinstated claim in Mead, supplemental briefing in Willey) and in new filings, including a Justice Department suit against a Maryland district.

### Cited Findings
- **Mirabelli v. Bonta, No. 25A810 (Mar. 2, 2026, per curiam).** On an emergency application, the Court vacated the Ninth Circuit's stay of a class-wide permanent injunction "with respect to the parents" and otherwise denied the application. It held parents seeking religious exemptions "are likely to succeed on the merits of their Free Exercise Clause claim" because the policies "substantially interfere" with religious upbringing under Mahmoud, that California's policies "will likely not survive the strict scrutiny that Mahmoud demands", and that the due process subclass is also likely to succeed because parental rights include "the right not to be shut out of participation in decisions regarding their children's mental health." Justices Thomas and Alito would have granted in full; Justice Sotomayor would have denied in full; Justice Barrett concurred, joined by the Chief Justice and Justice Kavanaugh; Justice Kagan dissented, joined by Justice Jackson. It is an interim ruling, not a merits decision. — [Mirabelli v. Bonta, per curiam and separate opinions](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/25A810)
- The underlying injunction bars schools from "misleading" parents and requires them to follow parents' directions on names and pronouns; it does not in terms impose a duty of proactive disclosure. — [Mirabelli v. Bonta, per curiam at 3](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/25A810)
- **Mahmoud v. ### Inferences
- After Mirabelli, a parent's free exercise claim is the stronger vehicle in the lower courts (Mead, Vitsaxaki); due-process-only cases decided earlier (Foote, Littlejohn) were left in place. Parents without religious objections remain in a less certain position, as the Foote petitioners argued.
- Because Mirabelli is an interim order on a California class injunction, its holdings are "likely to succeed" predictions. Lower courts are treating it as highly persuasive but not binding, and no court has yet entered final judgment for a parent outside California on its authority.
- The repeated denials without GVR, after Justice Kagan's public invitation to take Foote, suggest the Court is not currently seeking a merits vehicle among the pre-2026 cases; the next candidate is likelier to come from the Ninth Circuit's merits ruling in Mirabelli or from a post-Mirabelli circuit decision.
