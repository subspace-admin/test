# Searches find no school-arranged gender medication

No case located in this research establishes that a U.S. school or school employee arranged or materially facilitated gender-related medication or surgery for a minor without the relevant parent's knowledge or consent and without prior court authorization. Every candidate traced to a record fails at least one of the four elements: the nearest on the school side, *Lavigne* (Maine), alleges a chest binder, which is a device and was never proven; the nearest on the medication side, Yaeli Galdamez (Arcadia, California), involved hormones that a juvenile-court judge authorized, by the mother's own account; and *Calgaro* (Minnesota) meets three elements as pleaded but the school's only role was refusing the mother records. This finding describes the searches performed through 2026-11-18 and does not establish that no such case exists. What the records do show is a consistent two-step pattern: schools are documented or alleged to have used a new name and pronouns, counseled a student, or withheld information, and any medication was then prescribed by clinicians under a parent's consent, a minor-consent statute, or a court order. This first maintained version of the catalog holds 51 incident entries (8 medical-question candidates, 10 California school incidents, 19 non-California school incidents, 14 provider, agency and court cases); only 6 are established by records, 33 are reported allegations, and the figures count this collection, not the country. The legal ground is still moving: the Supreme Court's March 2, 2026 order in *Mirabelli v. Bonta* is an interim ruling for objecting California parents, with the Ninth Circuit merits appeal fully briefed and undecided. Orange County, the first geographic priority, yielded one pseudonymous family incident and one disputed aggregate claim, neither with any medical element.

## 0. Research summary: no case meets all four elements, and each near-miss fails on an identifiable one

### The direct answer to the first-priority question

The test applied to every incident has four elements: (1) a school or school employee arranged or materially facilitated; (2) gender-related medication or surgery that was actually prescribed, dispensed or administered; (3) the relevant parent or guardian was unaware or had not consented; and (4) no prior court order authorized it. **No case found satisfies all four.** In every candidate traced to records, either no medication or surgery occurred (most are social transition, counseling, brochures or a chest binder), or the treatment was authorized by a parent, by a juvenile court or custody order, by a minor-consent statute applied by clinicians, or it began after age 18. This is a description of what the searches found. It is not proof that no qualifying case exists, and the decisive records in the closest cases (juvenile files, medical charts, claims data) are sealed or private.

The finding survived three adversarial checks. First, when CNN asked the White House in October 2026 for cases behind the claim that children leave for school and return days later after surgery, the White House provided none and sent a list of social-transition cases; the one cited case that mentioned surgery (the Colorado *Doe v. Weiser* complaint) did not result in surgery ([CNN via KEYT, 2026-10-20](https://keyt.com/politics/cnn-us-politics/2026/10/20/fact-check-trump-brings-back-his-2024-lie-that-schools-are-secretly-sending-kids-to-hospitals-for-gender-surgery/)). PolitiFact reached the same result in 2024, when the campaign cited school notification policies instead of a case ([PolitiFact, 2024-09-05](https://www.politifact.com/factchecks/2024/sep/05/donald-trump/trumps-pants-on-fire-claim-that-schools-are-perfor)). Second, a researcher downloaded all 63 substantive filings on the Supreme Court docket in *International Partners for Ethical Care v. Ferguson* (No. 25-840), including about 55 amicus briefs from groups with every incentive to cite such a case, and text-searched for "school" near testosterone, estrogen, cross-sex hormone, puberty blocker and mastectomy; the terms co-occurred in five documents, and none alleges that a school arranged medication or surgery without parental knowledge and without a court order. That was a keyword-proximity search, not a full reading of every brief ([docket No. 25-840](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-840.html)). Third, web searches for any criminal charge, licensure action, termination, judicial finding, settlement, agency finding or sworn testimony that a school employee obtained, supplied or arranged blockers, hormones or surgery returned none, and the 2025–2026 federal investigations of school districts allege social transition, records and notification, not medical treatment ([Education Week, 2026-09-17](https://www.edweek.org/policy-politics/trump-admin-turns-to-student-privacy-laws-in-gender-identity-fights-with-schools/2026/09)).

### Why verified examples are scarce: evidence-backed observations

The records point to structural consent gates, not to proof of absence. **Parental consent is the stated default.** Clinicians quoted by CNN and PolitiFact say blockers and hormones for minors require parental consent even where legal, and that the AAP, WPATH and Endocrine Society standards require parental involvement; the National Association of School Nurses' leaders say even giving a student Tylenol typically requires written parental permission and that gender surgery at school happens "Not ever" ([PolitiFact](https://www.politifact.com/factchecks/2024/sep/05/donald-trump/trumps-pants-on-fire-claim-that-schools-are-perfor); [CNN via KEYT](https://keyt.com/politics/cnn-us-politics/2026/10/20/fact-check-trump-brings-back-his-2024-lie-that-schools-are-secretly-sending-kids-to-hospitals-for-gender-surgery/)). Surgery on minors is itself rare: an analysis of 2019 claims covering 22.8 million insured minors found 85 gender-affirming surgeries, 82 of them chest procedures ([PolitiFact](https://www.politifact.com/factchecks/2024/sep/05/donald-trump/trumps-pants-on-fire-claim-that-schools-are-perfor)).

**School staff have no lawful route to medication.** California's minor-consent statutes let a mature 12-year-old consent to outpatient counseling but state that they do "not authorize a minor to receive ... psychotropic drugs without the consent of the minor's parent or guardian," and independent consent to medical care requires a minor who is 15 or older, living apart and self-supporting, emancipation, or a court order ([Fam. Code §6924](https://california.public.law/codes/family_code_section_6924); [Fam. Code §6922](https://california.public.law/codes/family_code_section_6922)). That reading is an inference from statutory text; no statute or case found states the rule in terms for gender medication. Washington told the Supreme Court that its age-13 behavioral-health statute "does not permit minors to consent to medical care like puberty blockers or hormone replacement therapy" ([Brief for Respondents, 2026-10-30](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/25/25-840/431251/20261030103650623_BrRespts_Corrected.pdf)). A state-side expert in *Mirabelli* testified that at her clinic "in order to get to gender clinic, you have to be out to at least one caregiver" ([MSJ order, p. 25](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/63d954d4e4ad424df7819d46/6949e11ea6bae817c8eaf637_Dkt%20%20307_MSJ%20Order.pdf)).

**On-campus clinics state a parental-consent rule in writing.** The operator of a Seattle school-based pilot wrote that hormonal treatment "can only be consented to by a parent or legal guardian," and a second operator said it was "seeking and receiving the consent of parents or legal guardians"; the district said it does not provide or supervise the care ([Defending Education FOIA summary](https://defendinged.org/incidents/seattle-public-schools-school-based-health-center-provider-offers-students-gender-affirming-care-services-including-hormonal-treatment-emails-reveal/); [KOMO](https://komonews.com/news/local/gender-affirming-care-seattle-students-lgbt-trans-transgender-health-center-washington-on-campus-services-sps-gay-academic-transition-non-profit-parents-law-regulation-doctor-community)). Whether practice matched the written rule is untested.

**Child-welfare custody runs through courts.** Massachusetts bars its agency from consenting to "extraordinary medical treatment" and requires "prior judicial approval" absent parental consent ([110 CMR 11.17](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/110-CMR-11-17)); California's dependency statute lets a juvenile court authorize care "after due notice to the parent" ([WIC §369](https://california.public.law/codes/welfare_and_institutions_code_section_369)), which is the mechanism documented in *In re D.H.* and described by the mother in Yaeli's case. The dependency-court route is the main documented path by which a minor received hormones over a living parent's explicit objection.

**The statutory exceptions run through licensed clinicians, not schools.** Oregon lets a minor 15 or older consent to medical treatment, and Maine lets a clinician give hormone therapy to a 16- or 17-year-old without parental consent under five conditions ([ORS ch. 109](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors109.html); [22 M.R.S. §1508](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/22/title22sec1508.html)). Where such a statute applies, a parent's lack of consent does not make the treatment unauthorized, which also reduces litigation and public records. Scarcity of documented cases is therefore weaker evidence of non-occurrence in Oregon and Maine than in parental-consent states such as California and Washington. No utilization data were found showing how often clinicians in those states start hormones on a minor's own consent, at a school site or elsewhere.

**What schools can lawfully do without parents is counseling, and that is where the disputes sit.** The Great Salt Bay board cited Maine statutes giving minors access to mental-health services and confidential school counseling ([Brief in Opposition, pp. 5–6](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/25/25-759/397047/20260223142651745_25-759BriefInOpposition.pdf)). The distribution of allegations matches: counseling, information, abuse reports, and one alleged hand-over of a non-prescription item. One further explanation, that insurance statements and pharmacy records make sustained undisclosed treatment on a parent's policy hard to conceal, is plausible but no source in this research documents it. Finally, the failure of motivated parties (the White House, federal agencies, dozens of amici) to produce a qualifying case when asked directly is meaningful evidence of rarity, not conclusive evidence of absence.

A statute that permits a minor to consent is a statement of what the law allows. It is not evidence that any treatment incident occurred. The statutes, bills and policies are set out in section 3, separately from the incidents.

## 1. Incident catalog: 51 entries in four separate classes, none with school-arranged medication

### How the IDs work

This is the first maintained version of the catalog; no earlier catalog file exists, so every ID below is new and is to be kept stable in later versions. **MED-###** marks candidates on the first-priority medical question: an incident gets a MED ID when the allegation that matters for that question is that a school employee supplied a device, linked a student to a clinician or clinic, or advocated for medication that was then actually given or sought. **SOC-CA-###** marks California school social-transition and notification incidents, Orange County first. **SOC-US-###** marks school incidents elsewhere. **CRT-###** marks medical-provider, child-welfare, agency and court cases, including cases where one parent consented and the other objected. The three kinds (social transition, medical treatment, court or agency action) are never merged: a school incident in which staff allegedly talked about binding or surgery stays in SOC with that fact flagged, and a court-authorized treatment stays in CRT even where a school appears in the background.

One incident has one ID even where it appears in several notes files or lawsuits. The Pasadena "Doe" family (SOC-CA-004) and the Clovis/Yosemite Valley "Poe" family (SOC-CA-006) are two separate families in one class action, *Mirabelli v. Bonta* (POL-010); the seven Escondido students (SOC-CA-007) are a third, separate matter in the same case. Yaeli Galdamez is MED-002 only, although her story is told in three amicus briefs and one civil suit. *Lavigne* is MED-001 only. Orange County "Family 7" (SOC-CA-001) is not merged with the Newport-Mesa aggregate claim (SOC-CA-002), because the pleading gives no basis to assign that family to any named district. Ohio's *In re J.N.S.* (CRT-005) and *Siefert* (CRT-006) probably concern the same family, but no source says so, so they keep separate IDs. Policy-only matters with no individual child have no incident ID and appear in section 3 as POL-### or LEG-###.

## 2. Detailed case notes: what occurred, what the law required, and what a court found are three separate questions

Each note keeps three questions apart: what happened, whether a parent's consent was legally required, and whether any court found unlawful conduct. Each ends with an element-by-element statement on the four-element test. Minors are identified only by the pseudonyms or initials the sources use.

### MED-001. *Lavigne v. Great Salt Bay Community School Board* (Maine): the closest school-side allegation is a device, and it was never tested

**The child, "A.B.," was 13** in December 2022. The actor is a school social worker, Samuel Roy, who per the complaint "provided the chest binder and instructed A.B. on how to use it," gave a second binder the same day, and said he "was not going to tell A.B.'[s] parents"; some staff also used a different name and pronouns at A.B.'s request ([cert petition appendix, App. 3a–5a](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/25/25-759/390158/20251222174317126_Cert%20Petition.pdf)). The mother found a binder while cleaning A.B.'s room, met the principal and superintendent on December 5, 2022, withdrew A.B. on December 12 and addressed the board on December 14 ([Brief in Opposition, pp. 5–6](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/25/25-759/397047/20260223142651745_25-759BriefInOpposition.pdf)). The mother held ordinary parental authority; no court order or agency custody was involved.

On the rules, the board's January 14, 2023 statement cited Maine statutes giving minors access to mental-health services without parental consent (22 MRSA §1502) and a confidential counseling relationship with school-based providers (20-A MRSA §4008), and said it was not aware of "any violation of policy or law." Those statutes concern counseling, not the provision of a garment. The district's written Transgender Student Guidelines require a parent or guardian at the planning meeting, and the district denies any unwritten withholding policy; it also notes that the mother does not allege she ever asked the school for the information ([Brief in Opposition, pp. 3–7](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/25/25-759/397047/20260223142651745_25-759BriefInOpposition.pdf)).

The District of Maine dismissed the case on May 3, 2024, and the First Circuit affirmed on July 28, 2025, solely because the complaint did not plausibly allege a board policy or custom of withholding; it noted the guidelines "do not explicitly address 'the giving of chest binders or any other devices to students.'" Neither court decided whether a constitutional right was violated, and neither found whether a binder was given ([1st Cir. opinion](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca1-24-01509/pdf/USCOURTS-ca1-24-01509-0.pdf)). The Supreme Court requested a response and then denied certiorari on March 30, 2026; there was no settlement ([docket No. 25-759](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-759.html)). The evidence label is **reported allegation**: the complaint is unsworn, the "not going to tell" statement is pleaded on information and belief, and the district has neither confirmed nor specifically denied the binder allegation in the documents reviewed. Sworn testimony from the social worker or the child, or the school's personnel records, would settle it. **Four elements:** (1) alleged and direct, unproven; (2) fails, because a binder is a device; (3) alleged, unproven; (4) met. Even if every allegation were proven, this would remain a device case.

### MED-002. Yaeli Galdamez / Abigail Martinez (Arcadia USD, L.A. County DCFS): hormones as a minor, authorized by a judge on the mother's own account

**Yaeli was about 16 at removal (about April 2016) and died by suicide on September 4, 2019 at 19**, an adult who had left foster care. The 2020 complaint gives a birth date of April 23, 2000 and inconsistently says age 14; the federal court noted about 16 ([order of 2022-01-14, pp. 2–3](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cacd.826608/gov.uscourts.cacd.826608.52.0_2.pdf)). The account that involves a school comes almost entirely from the mother, through interviews and amicus briefs filed for her by First Liberty and Alliance Defending Freedom. Those briefs say school staff told Yaeli "to clandestinely join the LGBTQ club," that a school psychologist "told her to accuse her mother of abuse ... so that she would lose custody and the state would pay for gender-transition treatments without parental consent," that DCFS placed her in a group home, and that "a judge ruled that Yaeli could receive cross-sex hormones," signing "the order in her place" over the mother's objection ([amicus brief, No. 23-450, pp. 3–7](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/23/23-450/290771/20231129115825129_23-450%20Amicus%20Abigail%20Martinez%20Final.pdf); [amicus brief, No. 25-840, pp. 2–4](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/25/25-840/396397/20260217122626997_25-840%20Brief.pdf)). No source alleges that school staff scheduled, prescribed, dispensed or transported for hormones, and the prescriber is not identified.

The notes conflict on the litigation, and the conflict resolves in favor of the researcher who read the docket. Two notes files reported that no complaint or disposition could be located. A third read the filings directly: *Estate of Yaeli Mozzelle Galdamez v. County of Los Angeles* was filed September 4, 2020 (L.A. Superior Court No. 20STCV33881) and removed to federal court (C.D. Cal. No. 2:21-cv-05971). **No school district is a defendant and the complaint does not mention a school**, although the mother's brief says she "filed a civil lawsuit against the school district and DCFS." The complaint also has the opposite emphasis from the later account: it faults DCFS workers because they "failed to properly address and provide services" for the transition and says the mother "did everything within her power ... to accept the transition"; it pleads that defendants knew the decedent "was on hormone medication" ([complaint ¶¶2–9, 89](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cacd.826608/gov.uscourts.cacd.826608.1.2.pdf); [RECAP docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/60087339/the-estate-of-yaeli-mozzelle-galdamez-v-county-of-los-angeles/)). On January 14, 2022 the federal court dismissed the federal claims without leave to amend and remanded the state claims, deciding nothing about hormones, consent or a school ([order, pp. 5–10](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cacd.826608/gov.uscourts.cacd.826608.52.0_2.pdf)). State docket summaries, seen only at search-result level, show a demurrer sustained with leave, an order to show cause "re dismissal (settlement)" set and then vacated, and a notice of entry of dismissal; whether money changed hands is not established ([UniCourt listing](https://unicourt.com/case/ca-la23-the-estate-of-yaeli-mozzelle-galdamez-et-al-vs-county-of-los-angeles-by-and-through-department-of-children-and-family-services-et-al-586613)).

The applicable rule is the dependency statute: for a dependent child with no parent "capable of authorizing or willing to authorize" care, the juvenile court "after due notice to the parent ... may make an order authorizing" it ([WIC §369](https://california.public.law/codes/welfare_and_institutions_code_section_369)). The district's response, quoted in the mother's brief, is that "a claim suggesting our school or a staff member did not properly treat a student's severe depression is both completely inaccurate and troubling as our schools and staff would not be authorized or medically qualified to treat clinical depression"; DCFS is quoted as saying it "aggressively pursued the implementation of inclusive, gender-affirming laws, policies, and supportive services for LGBTQ+ youth," which is the mother's characterization of a filing the researchers did not see. The mother says she was "absolved of all claims of abuse and removed from the child abuse registry"; other coverage, seen only as a search summary, says DCFS changed its finding from "substantiated" to "inconclusive," which is weaker ([Daily Signal, 2024-09-08](https://www.dailysignal.com/2024/09/08/taken-california-stole-widows-daughter-transition/)). The Supreme Court denied her motion for leave to file the 2023 amicus brief ([docket 23-450](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/23-450.html)).

The label is **reported allegation** for the school role and the court order, since the amicus narrative is unsworn and repeated coverage traces back to the mother; the civil filing and its federal dismissal are **established by records**. Missing: the juvenile court's minute order, any school-psychologist report in that file, DCFS case notes, the prescriber's records, and the date testosterone began. **Four elements:** (1) alleged advocacy, not arrangement of treatment, denied by the district, with no documentary support in anything reviewed; (2) asserted in the complaint and by the mother, undocumented as to prescriber, date and age; (3) the mother was aware and objecting on her account, and the legally relevant consent was the dependency court's; (4) fails, because her own account describes a prior court order.

### MED-003. Kutzko family (Algonquin Regional H.S., Massachusetts): the court blocked the medication

The teen **turns 18 in April 2027** and is in DCF custody; the parents' legal rights have not been terminated. The parents and their attorney (Vernadette Broyles, Child & Parental Rights Campaign) allege "secret counseling sessions" in late 2024 that endorsed the child's wish "to be treated as something other than her sex," and that a counselor reported them to DCF. Court documents, as described by several outlets, show the teen went to a school counselor the day after an alleged January 2025 assault and the school contacted police. Why DCF has custody is contested. Governor Healey said "DCF did not remove the child because of a health issue or a gender-affirming care issue... DCF removed the child after there were criminal charges filed against the parents." The police statement alleged the father "punched the victim with a closed fist in the shoulder area"; the father received pretrial probation without a plea or finding, DCF's February 2025 letter reportedly found abuse "not supported" but supported neglect, and a restraining order the teen sought became permanent. The parents deny the allegations and say no court has found them unfit ([Boston.com](https://www.boston.com/news/local-news/2026/09/18/feds-investigating-mass-custody-case-involving-transgender-student-dcf/); [CBS Boston](https://www.cbsnews.com/boston/news/trump-administration-southboro-northboro-school-student-gender-transition/)).

PinkNews, citing WBUR, states: "That does not establish that the teenager is receiving testosterone or other gender-affirming medication." One outlet paraphrased the attorney more loosely, that the state "has administered gender-affirming care against the parents' wishes since the daughter was 15," without specifying the type of care ([WCVB](https://www.wcvb.com/article/massachusetts-dcf-gender-care-custody-case/73747124); [PinkNews](https://www.thepinknews.com/2026/09/18/maura-healey-trans-custody-2026/); [Deseret News](https://www.deseret.com/politics/2026/09/16/parents-fight-for-custody-of-children-who-want-gender-transition-with-states/)). Labels: the school counseling claim is a **reported allegation**; the mandated report and criminal charges are **established by records** as described by multiple outlets; DCF's request and the court's block rest on a single origin (the family's attorney) repeated by several outlets, with the juvenile file sealed; whether testosterone was ever given is **unresolved**. **Four elements:** (1) not alleged as to medication; (2) unproven and reportedly blocked; (3) fails, since the parents know and object; (4) fails, since a court is supervising.

### MED-006. Ashly Wallace / "Wynter" (Oregon): a parent's belief, no school link to medication, and a statute that makes parental consent unnecessary at 15

That statement is in tension with the district's own September 2023 process document, described in section 3 (POL-002); the tension is at the policy level and proves no specific concealed case. Label: **reported allegation**, disputed. **Four elements:** (1) alleged and disputed; (2) not alleged; (3) alleged and disputed; (4) not applicable.

### SOC-CA-004. Pasadena USD, the Doe family in *Mirabelli*: sworn, untested against the district, and without any medical element

Child Doe was **14 in August 2024**; the events run from age 10 to 14. In April 2021 a friend's mother told Mrs. Doe the child had announced a male name. In September 2021 the child gave her parents a note, "I am trans," asking for a haircut and a chest binder; the parents told her they did not agree to social transition "at school—or anywhere." In 2022–23 the mother emailed the principal, whose reply was "We support our LGBTQ students and follow state Education Code" with a link to the state FAQ; three teachers, asked whether there was anything she should know, each said "No"; in February 2023 the principal "denied any knowledge of the social transition" but said that if a child asked for secrecy, "We have to do that, it's the law." The parents found a therapist in February 2023; the child said in May 2023 "I can stop being transgender"; in February 2024 the father heard friends and a chaperone use the male name, and the parents "do not know" whether teachers were then doing so ([SAC ¶¶128–153](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.casd.757886/gov.uscourts.casd.757886.133.0.pdf)).

The complaint does not name the district. The identification of Pasadena Unified comes from the plaintiffs' August 26, 2026 appellate brief, which says the school was "operated by the Pasadena Unified School District" and that "PUSD's Rule 30(b)(6) designee confirmed these policies" ([Answering Brief, pp. 8–11](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/63d954d4e4ad424df7819d46/6a905f0d4fa78dfe6c216b9b_260826-Mirabelli.pdf)). Three inconsistencies in the retellings matter. The brief says that in fifth grade "the school had been facilitating this behind their backs," while the complaint's fifth-grade paragraphs allege only that the child told a friend. The brief adds a seating chart showing the male name, which is not in the complaint. And one outlet reported that the daughter changed names "without her parents' knowledge," although the parents knew of the identification from 2021 and what they allege they lacked was school confirmation ([WORLD, 2026-09-08](https://wng.org/roundups/9th-circuit-to-weigh-parental-rights-and-gender-ideology-1788898562)). The summary-judgment order also misattributes the Doe principal-meeting passage to a Poe declaration.

The rule the principal invoked was the state's 2016 guidance, under which schools were to respect a student's limits on disclosure "including not sharing that information with the student's parents" (POL-011). Pasadena Unified is not a defendant, nothing has been adjudicated against it, and no statement by the district was found. The district court accepted the parents' showing in granting summary judgment against state defendants; the Supreme Court's recital matches the complaint but names no district ([opinion, pp. 2–3](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25a810_b97d.pdf)). The binder request was made to the parents and refused; the only treatment is parent-arranged therapy; no staff referral is alleged. A separate lead is policy-level only: the Center for American Liberty petitioned for a writ over a December 2023 records request for Pasadena's gender-support-plan forms (POL-021). Label: **sworn but contested allegation**. **Four elements:** (1) social transition alleged; (2) no; (3) yes as alleged, in the sense that the school would not confirm and the parents had refused consent; (4) not applicable.

### SOC-CA-005. Santa Clarita "Lydia" (William S. Hart UHSD): partly corroborated by the district's own lawyers, with no medical element and no lawsuit

The student was **14 to 16**. In fall 2021, her first year in public school after homeschooling, the mother found journals with self-harm imagery and notes about a binder, top surgery and a male name; she and her husband told the child they would not affirm. Near the end of ninth grade she saw a male first name on a biology assignment, which the teacher called a nickname. She first spoke publicly at a Hart board meeting in January 2023, after which a trustee connected her with Pacific Justice Institute ([The 74, 2025-06-09](https://www.the74million.org/article/the-california-mom-at-the-center-of-trumps-crackdown-on-school-gender-policies/)).

What records establish is narrow and specific. A June 5, 2023 memo to the district from its law firm states that the mother of an Academy of the Canyons student "claims her daughter was being 'secretly socially transitioned' with the 'possible help' of a biology teacher," that "It appears that the student (born a female) is now identifying as a male and is using a new name at school," and that "The general rule, therefore, is that the District cannot inform a parent about a student's social transitioning without the student's consent, unless an exception applies" ([Hamill letter, exhibit memo pp. 1, 6](https://www.the74million.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/CaliforniaFERPAandTitleIXUSDOELTR1.31.25-1-1.pdf)). The memo records the mother's claims and the district's legal position; it does not admit that any employee initiated or encouraged the transition.

The rest rests on the mother: that the principal refused to meet, citing AB 1266; that an English essay was withheld for months; that the principal told the child she was "under a district mandate not to tell" (hearsay from the child); and that she holds "nearly a thousand" internal emails, which are unpublished. On the binder she says "An older transgender-identified girl gave my daughter a breast binder on school grounds," which is a peer, not staff ([Substack, 2026-11-15](https://lydia833.substack.com/p/parent-panel-for-segm)). The child's contrary perspective, from the withheld essay, is that the mother's response to her coming out at about 11 was "dismissive and critical"; the child has not been interviewed. The district spokeswoman declined to address the case and said officials would "continue to follow the laws and procedures applicable to the district"; the board formed a committee to review its policies in December 2023, with outcome not found ([The Signal, 2023-12](https://signalscv.com/2023/12/moore-messina-to-head-review-of-hart-districts-notification-policies)). In fall 2023 the school agreed to stop; the child graduated in June 2025 and identifies as a girl. There was no lawsuit and no formal FERPA complaint by the mother, but her account was the lead example in the letter that prompted the federal FERPA finding against California (POL-012) ([The 74, 2026-01-29](https://www.the74million.org/article/ed-dept-says-california-violated-law-by-concealing-students-gender-identity/)). That letter is dated "January 31, 2024" on its face but is addressed to January 2025 officials and reported as January 31, 2025. Label: **corroborated account**. **Four elements:** (1) social transition, supported by the memo; (2) no; (3) yes initially; (4) not applicable.

### SOC-CA-006. The Poe family (Clovis USD, then Yosemite Valley Charter School): the strongest California record of conduct continuing after a written parental objection

Child Poe was in **seventh grade in 2022–23**. The 2024 complaint says only "Fresno County"; the Supreme Court application and the 2026 brief name **Clovis Unified** and **Yosemite Valley Charter School**. These are one family at successive schools, and neither school is a defendant ([MSJ order, pp. 33–34](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/63d954d4e4ad424df7819d46/6949e11ea6bae817c8eaf637_Dkt%20%20307_MSJ%20Order.pdf)). The family is not from Escondido; the Escondido connection in *Mirabelli* is the two teacher plaintiffs.

In seventh grade teachers used a male name and pronouns, which plaintiffs say was done under Clovis's "Gender Acknowledgment Plan"; the parents "had no knowledge." In March 2023 the child asked to see a therapist and the parents agreed; the non-school therapist diagnosed gender dysphoria "but did not advise John and Jane Poe of the diagnosis" and referred her to a psychiatrist who prescribed antidepressants. At back-to-school night on August 29, 2023 teachers used the legal name with the parents. On September 6, 2023 the child attempted suicide, was held under Welfare and Institutions Code §5150 and transferred to a hospital, where a doctor told the parents she was identifying as a boy at school, "the first time anyone had told them." The parents then asked the principal and school psychologist whether staff used a different name: "They said, 'no,'… However, we learned that this was a lie" ([Answering Brief, pp. 11–12](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/63d954d4e4ad424df7819d46/6a905f0d4fa78dfe6c216b9b_260826-Mirabelli.pdf); [MSJ order, p. 33](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/63d954d4e4ad424df7819d46/6949e11ea6bae817c8eaf637_Dkt%20%20307_MSJ%20Order.pdf)). After a move to a charter school, an administrator answered the parents by quoting the state FAQ: "We cannot share the gender identity of the student with the parent even if that gender identity is expressed openly in class." In ninth and tenth grade the father emailed teachers instructing a gender-neutral approach; "Only one teacher responded, stating that his school had to follow California law. The teachers then proceeded to use a male name and pronouns." The district court wrote that "California public schools still refuse to use the child's given female name in spite of the Poe parents' instructions" ([MSJ order, pp. 19, 34](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/63d954d4e4ad424df7819d46/6949e11ea6bae817c8eaf637_Dkt%20%20307_MSJ%20Order.pdf)).

The medical events are psychiatric (a suicide attempt, two involuntary holds, antidepressants) and arose in parent-arranged or emergency care. No filing alleges blockers, hormones, surgery, a school-provided binder or a school referral to a gender clinic. The plaintiffs' expert testified that had the school told the parents "there would be no September hospitalization"; that is retained-expert opinion, not a judicial finding of causation, and the State's rebuttal was not reviewed. Clovis's policy background is independently reported: the 2022 plan "did not require parental consent" and was replaced in 2023 by a plan requiring it; the district's spokesperson said "We look at every child individually to make sure they are supported and safe at school" ([Fresno Bee via Yahoo, 2023-10-04](https://www.yahoo.com/news/clovis-wants-parental-consent-lgbtq-005807424.html)). No statement by either school about this family was found. Minor inconsistencies in plaintiffs' papers: the father is "an engineer" in the complaint and "a train mechanic" in the 2026 brief, and the charter is a campus school in one and "an online public charter school" in the other. Label: **sworn but contested allegation**; the facts were recited by the district court on summary judgment and by the Supreme Court, but the schools involved never had an adversarial opportunity to contest them. **Four elements:** (1) yes as to social transition only; (2) no; (3) yes, unaware and then objecting; (4) no court order involved.

### SOC-CA-008. *Regino v. Staley* (Chico USD): the strongest California "information" allegation rests on a pleading alone

"A.S." was **11**, a fifth-grader at Sierra View Elementary in 2021–22. All facts "are derived from the allegations in Regino's complaint." The counselor asked whether A.S. would like a different name and male pronouns and whether she wanted her mother notified; A.S. said no. The counselor then told the teacher and arranged for other staff to use the new name. In spring 2022 "the counselor provided A.S. with information about a local community group that advocated for LGBTQ+ causes and also discussed 'top surgery' and 'breast binding.'" In April 2022 A.S. told her grandmother, who told the mother. The mother "let A.S. know that she supported her and would assist in her transition, if that was what A.S. wanted," arranged counseling, and complained to the school; she alleges she "would not have allowed Sierra View to socially transition her daughter without first seeking guidance from a mental health professional." The district's statement, seen only as a search excerpt, is that "Chico Unified does not have a 'Parental Secrecy Policy,' nor do we ever try to change a student's individual identity" ([Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/chico-school-policy-protecting-trans-student-privacy-survives-lawsuit-by-mother/)). The January 2026 order was not retrieved and is described from secondary sources. The counselor's account appears in no record reviewed. Label: **reported allegation**. **Four elements:** (1) alleged; (2) no, information only; (3) alleged for about January to April 2022; (4) not applicable.

### SOC-CA-009. *Konen v. Caldeira* (Spreckels Union SD): a $100,000 settlement that denies liability, and an investigation that cuts both ways

"A.G." was in **sixth and seventh grade (2018–20)**. The complaint alleges that two teachers who ran an LGBTQ club told her she was bisexual, then transgender, "encouraged A.G. to change her name to a boy's name," and "instructed A.G. not to tell her mother"; that a teacher completed a Gender Support Plan, writing "Not ever. ### SOC-US-003. Kettle Moraine (Wisconsin): the one final judgment for parents is not a concealment case

"A.F." was described by the parents' counsel as **12**. The court's findings, made on affidavits and the district's interrogatory answers, are that the parents first placed her in a mental-health center that "focused on 'affirming'" the identity, that she returned to school "with some parental support," that the parents "ultimately changed course" and asked for her legal name and female pronouns, and that "The District replied by saying they will follow the guidance of A.F. in what pronouns to use, even over parental objections." The superintendent "was the individual ultimately responsible for deciding to affirm A.F.'s preferred gender identity over the objection of her parents," and the district "acknowledges that they affirmed A.F.'s social transition because of advice from A.F.'s therapist and worry over a potential Title IX violation." The parents withdrew A.F., who soon "concluded that she did not want to transition genders" ([Decision and Order, Findings 1–5, 21–23](https://assets.adflegal.org/archive/adfm/TF-Decision.pdf)).

On October 3, 2023 Judge Michael Maxwell granted the parents summary judgment, declared that the district's practice "without parental consent violates parents' constitutional right to determine the appropriate medical and healthcare for their children," and enjoined staff from using a name or pronouns at odds with a student's sex "without express parental consent." The court treated social transition as a healthcare decision on the parents' unrebutted expert affidavits; the First Circuit rejected that characterization in *Foote*. The district argued there was no live controversy because the family had left and said it handled such matters case by case ([WPR](https://www.wpr.org/wisconsin-judge-blocks-school-districts-policy-allowing-students-choose-pronouns)). The parents were aware throughout, and because they withdrew the child in response to the district's stated position, the period of actual use over objection appears short or nil. It is a trial-court judgment, not appellate precedent; whether the district appealed was not verified. Label: **established by records**. **Four elements:** (1) yes, social only; (2) no; (3) parents aware and had withdrawn consent; (4) no order.

### SOC-US-007. Delaware Valley Regional H.S. (New Jersey): the board's own letter establishes continuation over a father's refusal

Sworn declarations from both sides are in the record. Jane told counselor Ashley Miranda she wanted to socially transition at school and "did not want school staff to report her social transition to her father"; Miranda emailed the entire staff and attests she "did not affirmatively disclose" to the father. Label: **established by records** for non-notification and continuation over objection; who initiated the change is a **sworn but contested** point. **Four elements:** (1) yes, social only; (2) no; (3) yes; (4) no order.

### SOC-US-002. *Littlejohn v. Leon County* (Florida): the support-plan meeting is corroborated, the "secret" framing is contradicted

The child was **13** in 2020–21. The Eleventh Circuit, reciting the complaint, says the parents "did not allow their child to use a different name or pronouns, though they permitted the child to use 'J.' as a 'nickname' at school." Counselor Rachel Thomas and other staff met the child and developed a "Transgender/Gender Nonconforming Student Support Plan" without notifying the parents, because the child did not request their presence; the plan recorded the parents as "aware, but not supportive." Staff told the mother that "by law" the child had to request parental attendance, and an assistant superintendent wrote, "We currently do not have any Florida specific law that obligates us to inform the parents" ([11th Cir. opinion, pp. 3–6](https://media.ca11.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/files/202310385.pdf)).

Emails obtained by CNN and the Tallahassee Democrat, quoted through PolitiFact, show that on August 27, 2020 the mother told a teacher her child "would like to go by the new name [redacted] and prefers the pronouns they/them" and "If she wants to go by the name [redacted] with her teachers, I won't stop her," adding "I'm going to let her take the lead on this" ([PolitiFact, 2025-03-05](https://politifact.com/article/2025/mar/05/january-littlejohn-story-about-secret-gender-trans/)). The emails do not contradict the separate allegation that the plan meeting was held without the parents. The original articles were not fetched, and no response by the family to the email reporting was captured.

The district's 2018 guide told staff not to notify parents; Florida's Parents' Bill of Rights was enacted in 2021, after the events, and the board revised its guide in June 2022 to say staff "must not intentionally withhold information from parents" absent a reasonable belief of abuse. The district court dismissed in December 2022; the Eleventh Circuit affirmed 2–1 on March 12, 2025, holding the conduct was executive and did not "shock the conscience" as a matter of law; rehearing en banc was denied; certiorari was denied on April 27, 2026 after an express request to vacate in light of *Mirabelli* ([docket No. 25-259](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-259.html)). No court made findings of fact. Label: **corroborated account** for the meeting; the claim that the parents were unaware or had forbidden the name is **disputed**. **Four elements:** (1) yes, social only; (2) no; (3) disputed; (4) no order.

### SOC-US-008. *Willey v. Sweetwater County School District No. 1* (Wyoming): a discovery record that goes against the concealment account

Student "A.S." attended Black Butte High School in 2021–22; the mother is also a district special-education teacher. On the district's account of the summary-judgment record, there was no district policy on chosen names that year; two teachers testified they used an alternate nickname because the student asked, a third because he "heard and saw other students doing it." On March 29, 2022, at a training, the mother showed teachers a photo and they volunteered that they knew the student by another name; that evening she emailed staff demanding they stop. The Tenth Circuit heard argument January 21, 2026 and on March 10, 2026 ordered supplemental briefs on *Mirabelli*; no decision was found ([Cowboy State Daily, 2026-03-17](https://cowboystatedaily.com/2026/03/17/supreme-court-ruling-could-impact-wyoming-parents-suing-over-school-trans-policy)). ### CRT-001. *In re D.H.* (San Diego): a court order came first, with notice to the objecting parent

D.H., born January 2004, was taken into protective custody at 13 after the mother's arrest, declared a dependent in September 2017, and placed in long-term foster care; a no-contact order against the mother issued in July 2020 on D.H.'s own petition. On February 23, 2021 D.H. applied for an order for medical treatment with a Rady Children's Hospital physician's statement, a letter describing psychosocial assessments in 2019, 2020 and 2021, and a six-page informed-consent form. The application recorded that the mother and her counsel were notified on February 18 and opposed, and that the father, his counsel and D.H.'s counsel agreed. The court signed an ex parte order on February 25, 2021 "authorizing gender affirming therapy (i.e., testosterone treatment)." The first hormone appointment was April 30, 2021; **D.H. was 17**, and the order preceded treatment by about two months ([opinion, pp. 2–10](https://www4.courts.ca.gov/opinions/nonpub/D078915.PDF)).

The Court of Appeal held that the mother had actual notice and forfeited her due-process claim by not promptly seeking a hearing, and that the juvenile court had jurisdiction under Welfare and Institutions Code §362(a) and §369(c); the mother's objection "did not divest the court of its jurisdiction." It expressly did not decide whether the father's consent alone sufficed, and it said it did not imply that a juvenile "generally has the legal capacity or authority to consent to such medical treatment" ([opinion, pp. 16–18, 29–31](https://www4.courts.ca.gov/opinions/nonpub/D078915.PDF)). The opinion is unpublished and cannot be cited as precedent. The school in the record is San Pasqual Academy, D.H.'s foster placement, whose staff were part of the support system; it had no role in arranging treatment. The order and application themselves are confidential. Label: **established by records**. **Four elements:** (1) no; (2) yes; (3) no, the mother was notified and objected and the father agreed; (4) no, a prior order existed.

### CRT-002. *Hudacko v. Regents* (Contra Costa / UCSF): a drafting ambiguity in a custody order, not an absence of consent

S.H. was born in 2004 and was **about 17** at treatment. The August 26, 2020 custody order made the mother "the sole temporary legal custodial parent" of S.H., able to decide health matters "without Father's consent." Section 7a says S.H. "shall be permitted to pursue the services provided by UCSF as to [S.H.]'s gender identity, and shall be permitted to commence hormone therapy, if recommended by UCSF." Section 7b says S.H. "will not be permitted to undergo any gender identity related surgery until they are 18 years of age, absent a written agreement by both parties … or an order of the court" ([petition appendix, App. 146a–153a](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26-180/418433/20260807153429146_Petition%20for%20Certiorari-Hudacko%20v%20Regents%20of%20Univ%20of%20Cal.pdf)). On August 4, 2021, with the mother's consent, S.H. received a subcutaneous histrelin implant and began estradiol; the mother told the father by email on October 18, 2021.

The father alleges the implant was "gender identity related surgery" under §7b, citing the manufacturer's label that insertion "is a surgical procedure" and UCSF's billing of it to his insurer as "surgery," and that UCSF had the order and his October 2020 email refusing consent. The providers describe histrelin as medication that helps "temporarily suspend or block the physical changes of puberty," inserted "during an outpatient visit," and say the order "expressly authorized 'hormone therapy'" ([Brief in Opposition, pp. 2–5](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26-180/433112/20261118114719391_26-180%20Brief%20in%20Opposition.pdf)). The district court dismissed the Regents on immunity grounds and the mother and family lawyers as non-state actors, then gave the UCSF individuals qualified immunity because "[r]easonable doctors or lawyers could have believed the Supprelin implant was part of or an adjunct to the type of hormone therapy specifically permitted by Section 7a." The Ninth Circuit affirmed on October 21, 2025: "it was not clear the implant procedure fell within Provision 7b's 'surgery' exception," and the father "cites no clearly established law creating a parental liberty interest in precluding the procedure at issue here when the other parent and minor child consent to it." **No court has found that the order was violated or that it was not.** A state contempt application was withdrawn after the superior court indicated it lacked jurisdiction.

The cert petition (No. 26-180), limited to qualified-immunity doctrine, was filed August 7, 2026; the Court requested a response on September 17, and the opposition was filed November 18, 2026, the date of this status check ([docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/26-180.html)). There is no school role in the record. Label: **established by records** for the order and the treatment; the breach claim is a contested allegation. **Four elements:** (1) no; (2) yes; (3) the custodial parent consented, while the father objected and says he was not told; (4) disputed, since an order authorized hormone therapy and its reach to the implant is undecided.

### CRT-003. *Calgaro v. St. Louis County* (Minnesota): three elements as pleaded, and the missing one is the school

E.J.K. left her mother's home in May 2015. A legal-aid letter called her "legally emancipated," which the court said "had no legal effect." As the Eighth Circuit recites the complaint, "E.J.K. obtained gender transition care from Park Nicollet" and prescription medication from Fairview, and "Both providers thought E.J.K. could give effective consent … because she was living apart from her parents and managing her personal financial matters," citing Minn. Stat. §144.341; the providers refused the mother the records. The school district and the Cherry School principal "denied" the mother's requests for educational records and participation in educational decisions. Nothing says the school arranged treatment ([opinion, pp. 2–3](https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/19/03/172279P.pdf)).

The statute provides that "any minor who is living separate and apart from parents or legal guardian ... and who is managing personal financial affairs ... may give effective consent to personal medical, dental, mental and other health services" ([Minn. Stat. §144.341](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/144.341)). The court held that the providers "provided medical services to E.J.K. without parental consent" but were not state actors, that "a provider does not terminate parental rights by recognizing a minor's consent, even if the provider is mistaken," that no county or district policy was pleaded, that the principal had qualified immunity, and that injunctive claims were moot once E.J.K. turned 18. It did not rule on whether the minor's consent was valid. Dismissal was affirmed on March 25, 2019 and certiorari was denied ([docket 19-127](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/19-127.html)). Label: the ruling is **established by records**; the underlying facts are a complaint recited on dismissal, counted here as a **reported allegation**. **Four elements:** (1) no; (2) yes as pleaded; (3) yes as pleaded; (4) yes, no order. Three of four are met as pleaded, and the school element is the one missing.

### Corrections to statutory leads received with the assignment

**Vermont "H.657 / Act 136" is real but was mis-described.** Act 136 of 2026 (H.657) is "An act relating to various programming and requirements within the Department for Children and Families." Its relevant section lets certified homeless 16- and 17-year-olds consent to health care generally; the act does not mention gender-affirming care. The same bill number belongs to unrelated bills in earlier sessions (a fuel-tax bill, a lake designation, a communications-tax act), and Act 136 of the 2021–2022 biennium is a different law on leave for crime victims. The Vermont bill that actually proposed minor consent to gender-affirming care is H.659 (2022), which never left committee ([H.657 status](https://legislature.vermont.gov/bill/status/2026/H.657); [Act 136 summary](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2026/Docs/ACTS/ACT136/ACT136%20Act%20Summary.pdf); [H.659 status](https://legislature.vermont.gov/bill/status/2022/H.659)). Citing H.657 as a gender-care law overstates it; saying no such law exists is now also wrong for certified homeless 16- and 17-year-olds. Vermont's minor-consent counseling statute is 18 V.S.A. §8350, "Added 2017, No. 35," not a 2021 act ([§8350](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/18/196/08350)).

**Maine LD 535 / 22 M.R.S. §1508 is confirmed**, with details tightened: ages 16 and 17 only, hormones and puberty suppression, nonsurgical, five conditions, effective October 25, 2023. Testimony on the failed repeal bill cited "§1597-A" in places; the correct section is §1508. **Oregon's age-15 rule is ORS 109.640(4)**, enacted in 1971; HB 2002 did not create it, and the Oregon Health Authority's page still cites it as subsection (3), which is citation drift, not a substantive conflict ([OHA](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/ph/healthypeoplefamilies/abortionaccess/pages/legal-rights-privacy.aspx)). **California SB 107, AB 2119, AB 665 and AB 1955 do not authorize minors to consent to hormones or blockers**; any incident record that relies on them as the legal basis for such treatment rests on the wrong statute, and the only California route found is Family Code §6922 with its age, living-apart and financial-independence conditions. "Expressly authorized" and "reachable through a general statute" are different: only Maine's statute names gender-affirming hormone therapy, while Oregon's wording is broad enough to cover it and state and press sources describe it that way.

### POL-001. Elk Grove USD BP/AR 5145.18: a policy item with no incident behind it

**No Elk Grove incident is established.** No record was found of an Elk Grove student suspended, expelled or disciplined over pronoun use, of a student socially transitioned without parental notice, or of a lawsuit or civil-rights complaint on either point. The most motivated source, a California Family Council article dated November 13, 2026, cites no Elk Grove complainant, discipline record or proceeding. Absence of reports is not proof of absence, because student discipline records are confidential. The only Elk Grove episode with named facts is a 2024 dispute over an elementary-school LGBTQ club, a different subject, in which a trustee said "There was an investigation and they found no evidence" that staff told students to hide the club ([CBS Sacramento, 2024-09-11](https://www.cbsnews.com/sacramento/news/elk-grove-unified-opt-in-school-club-option-lgbtq-controversy/)).

**What was re-verified from district records.** Both documents are titled "Nondiscrimination/Harassment/Intimidation/Bullying"; this is not a stand-alone gender-identity policy ([EGUSD policies page](https://www.egusd.net/District/About-EGUSD/PoliciesProceduresNotices/index.html)). The district's own summary states that students who harass on a protected basis "shall be subject to counseling and appropriate discipline, up to and including expulsion," and employees "up to and including dismissal" ([poster, rev. 9/2023](https://www.egusd.net/documents/District/About-EGUSD/PoliciesProceduresNotices/Nondiscrimination-Poster-Revised-9-2023.pdf)). The 2026–27 handbook lists the regulation as "Revised: November 10, 2025," lists a student right "To be addressed by the name and pronouns that correspond with your gender identity," says of gender support plans that "School staff, caregivers and the student work together to complete the plan," and reproduces the statutory threshold for harassment-based suspension ([handbook](https://d16k74nzx9emoe.cloudfront.net/745531a8-8bcf-41a7-8558-74066af5b047/2026-27-District-Handbook.pdf)). The last board action confirmed from minutes is the unanimous consent-agenda approval of updates to BP 5145.18 on October 15, 2024 ([minutes](https://d16k74nzx9emoe.cloudfront.net/03eb82a2-6298-4aae-a9e8-df8d0522482a.pdf)). The November 10, 2025 date matches no board meeting, which fits the description of the regulation as a staff-issued document; a search summary's statement that the district "re-approved the language on November 10, 2025" appears to misread that revision date and is not repeated here as a board vote.

**What rests only on the California Family Council article's quotations.** The text of AR 5145.18 was not obtained from the district's system, which loads policy text by script behind bot protection. The article itself returned an access error and was read through a verbatim repost, whose fidelity is not independently confirmed. On that basis only: the regulation lists, as conduct that "may constitute gender-based harassment," "Refusing to address a student by a name and the pronouns consistent with the student's gender identity"; it says "For students in grades 4-12, disciplinary action may include suspension and/or expulsion," "provided that, in imposing such discipline, the entire circumstances of the incident(s) shall be considered"; it requires staff to use the chosen name and pronouns, excusing "inadvertent slips or honest mistakes"; and it contains no parental-objection provision ([repost of the CFC article](https://freerepublic.com/focus/f-bloggers/4403274/posts)). The same pronoun sentence appears word for word in another district's regulation built on the school boards association's sample, which supports the quotation without confirming Elk Grove's text ([El Rancho BP/AR 5145.3](https://www.erusd.org/pdf/board_policies/5145_3.pdf)). The article's own qualifiers matter: "The policy itself, Board Policy 5145.18, never mentions pronouns," and "The district's policy and state law reserve suspension for harassment that is severe or pervasive." The headline compresses two separate provisions into one; on the quoted text the pronoun item is an example of conduct that "may constitute" harassment, not a stand-alone offense with its own penalty. On the statutes (LEG-013), an isolated or mistaken pronoun use cannot by itself support suspension or expulsion. The article's count of "at least 10 districts and one county office" using the identical example is its own review and is unverified, as are its descriptions of several court decisions.

**The November 17, 2026 vote is unverified.** The meeting took place and a recording exists; local coverage reports a 5–2 vote that night on an unrelated labor item and does not mention the policy ([Elk Grove News, 2026-11-18](https://www.elkgrovenews.net/elk-grove-unified-board-project-labor-agreement-5-2-vote/)). That BP 5145.18 was on the consent agenda for renewal rests on the advocacy article and one derivative item. Whether it was pulled, amended or approved is unresolved. The records that would settle it are the district's posted agenda for that meeting with attachments ([agenda](https://simbli.eboardsolutions.com/SB_Meetings/ViewMeeting.aspx?S=36030236&MID=95406)), the meeting recording ([recording](https://www.youtube.com/live/d3mnQQYS4w8)), the adopted minutes, normally approved at the next regular meeting (December 15, 2026 per the schedule page), and the "last revised" date on the policy page.

**Two different questions.** A dispute over how one student addresses another is a question about peer speech and student discipline. A school changing how it treats a parent's own child without informing that parent is a question about parental rights. *Mirabelli* concerns the second: the injunction, as the Supreme Court described it, "prevents the schools from 'misleading' parents about their children's gender presentation at school" and "requires the schools to follow parents' directions regarding their children's names and pronouns." It does not decide whether one student may be disciplined for how he or she addresses another. *Mirabelli* bears on Elk Grove in one way: if the regulation has no parental-objection provision, that is a gap between it and the injunction as to the school's own conduct toward an objecting parent's child. Whether the injunction binds a non-party district directly, or only through the state defendants, was not resolved.

### POL-010. *Mirabelli v. Bonta*: current status and scope

**Status as of 2026-11-18: an interim Supreme Court ruling for objecting parents, with the merits appeal fully briefed and undecided.** The district court certified a statewide class on October 15, 2025 and on December 22, 2025 granted summary judgment and a permanent injunction for the plaintiffs on the parents' due-process and free-exercise claims and the teachers' free-exercise and free-speech claims ([MSJ order](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/63d954d4e4ad424df7819d46/6949e11ea6bae817c8eaf637_Dkt%20%20307_MSJ%20Order.pdf); [injunction](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/63d954d4e4ad424df7819d46/6949e13f76555ca3d193bc7d_Dkt.%20308_Permanent%20Injunction.pdf)). The Ninth Circuit stayed the injunction on January 5, 2026. On March 2, 2026 the Supreme Court ruled: "The application to vacate the Ninth Circuit's stay ... is granted as to the parents but is otherwise denied." Justices Thomas and Alito would have granted in full; Justice Sotomayor would have denied in full; Justice Barrett concurred, joined by the Chief Justice and Justice Kavanaugh; Justice Kagan dissented, joined by Justice Jackson ([opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25a810_b97d.pdf)). The Court held that parents seeking religious exemptions "are likely to succeed on the merits of their Free Exercise Clause claim" and that the due-process subclass is likely to succeed because parental rights include "the right not to be shut out of participation in decisions regarding their children's mental health." Justice Barrett wrote that "The word 'likely' is important, because it reflects that our assessment is preliminary." It is a ruling on likelihood of success, not a final merits judgment.

Afterwards, the Ninth Circuit vacated its stay "as to the parents' claims" on March 3 and on March 17 denied without prejudice the State's motion to confirm that the injunction does not compel disclosure "to parents who would engage in abuse," sending that question to the district court ([order of 2026-03-17](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/63d954d4e4ad424df7819d46/69b9adfbaccfa74302a5c30d_ee685501-3022-f111-8341-001dd80c4b81.pdf)). The district court denied the State's ex parte motion to modify the injunction on March 30, 2026 and awarded $4,526,888 in attorneys' fees the same day; the case was reassigned on April 6, 2026; the fee orders were appealed and stayed on August 13, 2026 ([district docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/67279752/mirabelli-v-olson/); [fee order](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/63d954d4e4ad424df7819d46/69caedab82c9665686e9c4e2_260330-Mirabelli-Order%20on%20Attorneys%27%20Fees-dkt%20364.pdf)). One notes file could not reach the docket and recorded the modification question as unknown; the file that read the docket controls. The plaintiffs' answering brief was filed August 26, 2026, the State's reply on October 16, 2026, and a supplemental-authority letter on November 6, 2026; consolidated appeals Nos. 25-8056 and 26-2337 are fully briefed, and no merits decision appears on the docket ([9th Cir. docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72227486/mirabelli-et-al-v-bonta-et-al/)).

**Scope.** The injunction runs against the Attorney General, the State Superintendent and State Board members and those in concert with them. It bars them from applying state privacy and nondiscrimination provisions and guidance so as to permit or require an employee to mislead a parent about a child's gender presentation at school, "whether by: (i) directly lying to the parent; (ii) preventing the parent from accessing educational records of the child; or (iii) using a different set of preferred pronouns/names when speaking with the parents than is being used at school," or to use a name or pronoun at odds with the legal name "where a child's parent or legal guardian has communicated their objection." By its text it prohibits misleading parents and overriding communicated objections; it does not in terms impose an affirmative duty on every school to notify. The Supreme Court noted that the injunction "does not provide relief for all the parents of California public school students, but only for those parents who object to the challenged policies or seek religious exemptions." The teacher-protective paragraphs remain stayed, so a statement that the injunction was "restored in full" overstates the order. The school boards association advised that the ruling "likely affects all LEAs"; the State's merits position, as reported, is that the opinion does not bar withholding from parents who have not asked ([CSBA, 2026-03-06](https://blog.csba.org/mirabelli-march26/); [WORLD](https://wng.org/roundups/9th-circuit-to-weigh-parental-rights-and-gender-ideology-1788898562)). The abuse carve-out is the main live compliance dispute. The family facts behind the case (SOC-CA-004, SOC-CA-006) were litigated against state defendants, not against the schools involved, and no court has made contested findings about any individual child.

Outside California, *Mirabelli* has moved lower courts without producing a final judgment for any parent: a Second Circuit remand (SOC-US-006), a reinstated claim in Michigan before a voluntary dismissal (SOC-US-005), and supplemental briefing in the Tenth Circuit (SOC-US-008). ## Conclusion

The phrase "the school hid it" covers four different things in these records, and they have very different evidentiary support: not telling parents, excluding them from a planning meeting, telling them something untrue when asked, and continuing after they objected. The best-documented incidents are at the two ends. Non-notification is common in the allegations and is written into several policies, while continuation over an explicit objection is established by a board's own letter in New Jersey, by judicial findings in Wisconsin, and by emails described as exhibits in the Poe family's case. Affirmative deception, the claim that carries the most weight in public argument, is the one a discovery record has actually negated (*Willey*) and a parent's own emails have undercut (*Littlejohn*). A catalog that keeps those four apart will age better than one that sorts cases by which side brought them.

The medical question will not be settled by more lawsuits about names and pronouns. It turns on a small set of non-public records: two sealed juvenile files, claims data that can distinguish a school site and a consent basis, and consent forms at on-campus clinics in the two states where a minor's own consent is lawful. The next dated events that could change entries here are the Ninth Circuit's merits ruling in *Mirabelli*, the Supreme Court argument in *International Partners for Ethical Care v. Ferguson* on December 7, 2026, the Court's action on the *Hudacko* petition, the Elk Grove minutes expected after December 15, 2026, and the *Blair* and Kutzko trials set for March 2027.
