# Loudest gender claims outrun the record

The strong version of the claim (a school sends a child for an "operation" or hormones without the parents, or a state seizes children to transition them) does not arise from any documented case. It arises at the point where national politicians, cable hosts, campaign ads and high-reach sites retell local material that is about names, pronouns, support plans, clubs, records and shelter-notification rules. The companion catalog tested 51 incidents against four elements (school arranged it; medication or surgery actually given; parent unaware or not consenting; no prior court order) and **none met all four**; the nearest are a chest binder that was never proven (MED-001), hormones a juvenile judge authorized on the mother's own account (MED-002), and a Minnesota case where the school's only role was refusing a mother records (CRT-003). Of the 33 claims the researchers inventoried on that side (40 rows here, with viral numbers, two campaign ads and a comparator added), every one asserting school- or state-arranged medication, surgery or seizure has no underlying case as worded, the "state took my child" stories rest on real cases whose records show a different trigger, and the substantially accurate claims are about social transition, written policy or statute text. The same test was applied to the opposing side: of 18 claims traced (21 rows here), none is pure invention, but the absolute denials ("no school hides anything", "no minors get surgery") are contradicted by records and the harm forecasts and medical certainties ("will result in" homelessness and violence, "life-saving", "fully reversible", regret "as low as 1% or 2%") outrun their evidence, while the narrow statements (schools do not medicate or operate; surgery on minors is rare; Texas did investigate consenting parents; threats followed viral posts) hold up. The best-documented driver is electoral: one advocacy group has said on the record that it tested messages to find one candidates would use, and tracked Republican spending on transgender-themed TV ads reached about $183 million by October 25, 2026, though those ads were mostly about sports and minors' medical care, not schools. Since January 2025 the federal executive has been the largest single converter of the narrative into orders, investigations and suits, and its own documents keep social transition and medical treatment apart in their operative text while fusing them in their rhetoric. No social-media post was viewed directly in this research, reach figures are mostly missing, the shared platform cap of 200 searches was hit, and no poll was found that measures how many people believe the strong claim.

## 0. Summary: the strong claim is made downstream of the documents, and both sides fail the same test in mirror image

### What was tested and what the record side shows

This report works from the outside in. It starts with the most sensational wording in circulation, traces each claim to its earliest findable origin, and then sets it against the record assembled in the companion evidence catalog. The catalog's core finding is restated here so this report stands alone: **zero of 51 catalogued incidents show a U.S. school or school employee arranging gender-related medication or surgery for a minor without the relevant parent's knowledge or consent and without prior court authorization**. That describes the searches performed; it does not prove no such case exists, and the decisive records in the closest cases (juvenile files, medical charts, claims data) are sealed or private. The finding held when CNN asked the White House in October 2026 for cases behind the claim: it supplied none, sent a list of social-transition cases, and the one cited case that mentioned surgery ended without it ([CNN via KEYT, 2026-10-20](https://keyt.com/politics/cnn-us-politics/2026/10/20/fact-check-trump-brings-back-his-2024-lie-that-schools-are-secretly-sending-kids-to-hospitals-for-gender-surgery/)). In 2024 the campaign answered the same request with school notification policies ([PolitiFact, 2024-09-05](https://www.politifact.com/factchecks/2024/sep/05/donald-trump/trumps-pants-on-fire-claim-that-schools-are-perfor)).

What the records do show is a two-step pattern. Schools are documented or alleged to have used a new name and pronouns, counseled a student, or withheld information; any medication was then prescribed by clinicians under a parent's consent, a minor-consent statute, or a court order. Within the catalog, 6 entries are established by records, 7 are corroborated accounts, 5 are sworn but contested and 33 are reported allegations. The well-supported complaints are at two ends: non-notification written into policy, and continuation over a parent's explicit objection, which is established by a board's own letter in New Jersey (SOC-US-007) and a final judgment in Wisconsin (SOC-US-003). Affirmative deception, the element that carries most weight in public argument, is the one a discovery record negated (SOC-US-008) and a mother's own emails undercut (SOC-US-002).

### The four findings of this report

First, **category decides accuracy**. Every inventoried claim that says medication or surgery was given through a school, or that a state took a child in order to transition the child, has no case behind it as worded. The claims that are substantially accurate describe names, pronouns, clothing, meetings, policy wording or statute text. A speaker can be accurate about social transition and unsupported one sentence later about medicine; the 2026 State of the Union passage does both.

Second, **the distortion has an identifiable location**. Originators and litigators overwhelmingly describe names, pronouns, support plans and records. The "operation", "three or four days later", "kidnap" and "castrate" versions appear in rally speech, cable commentary, campaign ads and aggregator headlines. Several conservative-movement figures, asked directly, stated the limit: Moms for Liberty co-founder Tiffany Justice said "Are kids getting surgery in school? No they're not", and Heritage's Thomas Jipping said he was "not aware of a specific incident yet where it has gone that far" ([CNN via ABC17, 2024-09-04](https://abc17news.com/politics/national-politics/cnn-us-politics/2024/09/04/fact-check-trump-falsely-claims-schools-are-secretly-sending-children-for-gender-affirming-surgeries/)).

Third, **debunks overreached in specific, checkable ways**. Fact-checks of California's AB 665 omitted its residential-shelter clause; fact-checks of Washington's SB 5599 conceded only in passing that a shelter may withhold a runaway's location from parents; experts quoted against the surgery claim said parental consent is always legally required, which is too broad for Oregon and Maine; and a headline that a Florida school "Did NOT Conceal" covers the pronouns but not the support-plan meeting held without the parents.

Fourth, **the opposing narrative fails the same test in mirror image**. Each side takes the other's documented kernel (nondisclosure policies exist; surgery on minors is rare) and converts it into its own absolute. Each side's lawyers and compliance writers are more accurate than its headline writers. Each side has careful voices who corrected their own camp, and neither side's originators retracted a strong claim.

The neutral baselines are these. A Reuters/Komodo claims analysis found that "at least 121,882 children ages 6 to 17 were diagnosed with gender dysphoria in the five years to the end of 2021" and that those starting puberty blockers or hormones "totaled 17,683 over the five-year period, rising from 2,394 in 2017 to 5,063 in 2021", "probably a significant undercount" ([Reuters, copy on supremecourt.gov](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/URLs_Cited/OT2024/23-477/23-477-4.pdf)); secondary sources attribute to it 776 mastectomies and 56 genital surgeries among 13- to 17-year-olds in 2019–2021, though the data sidebar was not read directly. A JAMA Network Open study of 2019 claims found 85 gender-affirming surgeries among 22,827,194 insured minors, 82 of them chest procedures ([PolitiFact](https://www.politifact.com/factchecks/2024/sep/05/donald-trump/trumps-pants-on-fire-claim-that-schools-are-perfor)). So "thousands of minors received hormones" is supported by claims analyses, while "thousands had surgery" depends on the database and definition: 85 in one year in one study, 776 chest surgeries over three years in another, 5,747 "sex change surgeries" over five years in an advocacy database. None of these sources says anything about schools or about parental consent. Joining the numbers to "schools" or "without parents" is the added step.

### Debunks that overreached

Several claims rated false contain a narrower true statement that the debunk did not state, and a report that treated the verdict as the whole record would repeat the gap. On AB 665 (NAR-014), Lead Stories quoted a sponsor saying "The bill is specific to outpatient mental health care" and Third Way, a center-left think tank, described it as covering "mental health services, like counseling"; neither mentions the residential-shelter clause, and the Federalist rebuttal by Nicole Pearson and Erin Friday was built on exactly that omission while conceding the bill does not "expressly" change child-welfare procedures ([The Federalist](https://thefederalist.com/2023/06/21/fake-fact-checker-hides-truth-about-california-bill-that-will-strip-kids-from-their-parents/)). On SB 5599 (NAR-015), the fact-checks concede that shelters "no longer have to report the location of a runaway child to the child's parents if the child is seeking gender-affirming or reproductive care", and PolitiFact's expert said the timeframe for the agency to contact parents is unclear ([FactCheck.org](https://www.factcheck.org/2023/05/online-claims-misrepresent-washington-bill-aimed-at-runaway-transgender-youth)). On Oregon (NAR-005), Third Way lists "this law allows minors 15 and older to receive sex reassignment surgeries without parental consent" under "Opponents' lies" while its own "truth" paragraph says "The current medical age of consent in Oregon is 15" ([Third Way](https://www.thirdway.org/memo/correcting-gop-lies-about-blue-state-bills-on-transgender-youth)); the misattribution to the 2023 bill is wrong and the underlying permission is real. On Littlejohn (NAR-026), the Lead Stories headline that the school "Did NOT Conceal" covers the pronouns, not the plan meeting. On Indiana (NAR-022), the fact-check omits the restriction on what the parents could discuss at visits. A search summary says a Seattle Times op-ed supporting SB 5599 was corrected for misdescribing the bill; that was not verified directly.

### Retractions, and their absence

**No originator of a surgery, hormone or custody claim in this inventory was found to have retracted it.** The surgery claim was fact-checked on September 4–5, 2024 and repeated on September 7 and 12 and in October 2024, then revived at four rallies in October 2026. What was found instead falls into six kinds. An ally conceded without the speaker doing so (Justice: "Are kids getting surgery in school? No they're not", while "thankful to President Trump" because "It grabbed your attention, and we're talking about it now"). The claim was reframed (the "direct nexus" email). A platform acted where the originator did not (Facebook flagged the SB 107 post). A speaker walked back her own words (Guzman in Virginia). Events ended the claim (AB 957 vetoed, LD 1735 failed, the Illinois bill died, the Colorado custody language was removed, the Kiel investigation closed). And the debunking side corrected itself: Snopes updated its September 5, 2024 item on September 13, which a search summary says acknowledged ambiguity about where the surgeries supposedly took place.

The litter-box comparator behaved differently. A Nebraska senator apologized within hours, Joe Rogan said "it doesn't seem that there's any proof that they put a litter box in there", Tony Dungy deleted a post and apologized, and a Maine candidate said he no longer believed it ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litter_boxes_in_schools_hoax)). Retraction tracks checkability: a litter box in a named school can be denied by that school in a day, while "it happens in some states" cannot be falsified by any one institution. Deletions cannot be assessed here, because the posts were not seen.

Later records complicated two fact-checks without vindicating the strong claim. The Supreme Court granted review of a challenge to SB 5599, which shows a live legal dispute, not that the "take children" claim was right (POL-020). The March 2, 2026 *Mirabelli* order found objecting California parents likely to succeed against non-disclosure rules, which supports the social-transition complaints and says nothing about medication (POL-010) ([Supreme Court opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25a810_b97d.pdf)).

### When each family spiked

Each family spikes on an identifiable trigger. Social-transition clips and leaked documents came first (the Oakland closet, the Eau Claire slide and the Hartford post, all February to April 2022). Custody-statute claims followed the blue-state legislative calendar (SB 107 in September 2022; AB 665, AB 957 and SB 5599 in March to June 2023; Maine in January 2024; Colorado in April 2025). On-campus clinic and hormone stories followed a records release in July 2023 and pieces in March and May 2024. The surgery claim arrived on a campaign stage in August 2024 and returned at the February 2026 State of the Union and the October 2026 rallies. Court events such as the cert petitions in *Cox* and the Washington case and the *Mirabelli* order re-energized stories without adding medical facts. "Spike" here means a clustering of dated publications found; no engagement series, search-trend data or broadcast counts were collected.

## 3. The channels and mechanics: a four-step relay with lags of hours to days, and corrections that arrive later on different sites

### The documented relay path

The documented path has four recurring steps. A local artifact appears: a teacher's own video, a school-board comment clip, a records-request document, a bill's passage, or an advocacy article. An aggregator or advocacy outlet repackages it under a compressed headline. National partisan outlets and an elected official or candidate pick it up. Then an institution responds with threats received, discipline, a policy change, a lawsuit or legislation, and that response becomes the next story. Each step shortens the text. The strong version (surgery, removal of children) appears at the national-politician and national-site stage; local artifacts in every case traced concern names, pronouns, clubs, records or shelter-notification rules.

### End-to-end case studies

**From a teacher's video to threats.** NBC's count of 33 instances rests on a review of "local news sources, social media posts and interviews with experts and victims", and NBC states its limits: "the direct inspirations for the threats are not known", prosecutors charged in only three of the 33, and "The charging documents associated with those prosecutions did not mention either Libs of TikTok or Raichik". Raichik called the coverage a "hit piece" and has said she "100 % condemn[s] any acts/threats of violence". Wikipedia's summary of the same article gives 20 bomb threats, a one-count discrepancy that was not resolved. Media Matters, a progressive watchdog using its own coding, counted at least 48 instances of threats or harassment after posts, and identified eight FoxNews.com articles that each used a single teacher TikTok video as the primary source ([Media Matters timeline](https://www.mediamatters.org/libs-tiktok/timeline-impact-libs-tiktok-told-through-educators-health-care-providers-librarians); [Media Matters, 2022-12-09](https://www.mediamatters.org/libs-tiktok/foxnewscom-mimicking-libs-tiktoks-anti-lgbtq-strategy)). In the Union case, reports differ on whether the threats ran two or six consecutive days. No peer-reviewed study of the account and harassment was found.

**From legislative text to district policy to copies.** After AB 1314 died without a hearing, its language went to the Chino Valley board, which adopted it on July 20, 2023 with public comment cut from three minutes to one ([Daily Beast via Yahoo](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/california-megachurch-pushing-public-schools-083343835.html)). Murrieta Valley followed on August 10, Temecula Valley and Anderson Union on August 22, Rocklin on September 6 and Orange Unified on September 7: six districts in seven weeks ([Daily Citizen](https://dailycitizen.focusonthefamily.com/six-california-school-districts-pass-parental-notification-policies-for-children-confused-about-their-sexual-identity/)). The Attorney General sued Chino Valley on August 28, 2023 ([OAG release](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-announces-lawsuit-challenging-chino-valley-unified-school)).

**From leaked audio to a settlement.** In Spreckels the district emailed new club rules within days of the Substack post, saying it "has taken immediate steps to address several of the concerns raised in the article". The teachers were placed on leave and resigned; the district's outside investigation answered "No" on whether they "coached" students; the case settled for about $100,000 with no admission of liability ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/nbc-out/out-news/california-district-pays-100k-settle-suit-support-students-gender-tran-rcna102559)).

**From a board comment to a federal finding.** "Lydia" first spoke at a Hart district board meeting in January 2023; a trustee connected her with Pacific Justice Institute; attorney Julie Hamill's January 2025 letter to the Education Department led to an investigation opened in March 2025 and the January 28, 2026 FERPA finding against California; Hamill is described as "Now an assistant U.S. attorney" ([The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/ed-dept-says-california-violated-law-by-concealing-students-gender-identity/)). This is the one path documented end to end from a parent's account to federal action.

**From a list to a bill.** Defending Education's count appears in the sponsor's release for the PROTECT Kids Act ("21,000 schools covering over 12 million students have policies allowing or requiring district personnel to keep a student's transgender status hidden from parents") and under a Moms for Liberty headline that turns policies into ongoing transitions ([Walberg release](https://walberg.house.gov/media/press-releases/chair-walberg-introduces-bill-safeguard-parental-rights)).

**From bill passage to television.** The "state will take your kids" framing reached paid television in 2026 in the Texas ad (NAR-038), with about 17,000 airings and more than $23 million through October 25.

**The control case.** The litter-box claim began as a public comment on December 21, 2021, was shared in January 2022 by a party official and by Libs of TikTok, was rated false by Snopes on January 24, 2022, and still peaked in September and October 2022 on campaign stages and a podcast; bills followed in North Dakota, Oklahoma and Texas. Elections act as a second amplifier: that claim peaked before the 2022 midterms and the Trump line recurred in October 2024 and October 2026.

### Platforms: where material originates and where it is amplified

TikTok and personal accounts are documented as the origin of raw material, in the form of teachers' own videos. X is documented as the main amplifier and the place where officials attach themselves to a post. School-board meetings and their video feeds are origin points; ProPublica found "nearly 90 incidents in 30 states" and at least 59 people arrested or charged at board meetings between May 2021 and November 2022, many over masks and others over LGBTQ books, with no share given ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/school-board-meetings-flashpoints-for-anger-chaos)). Substack is an origin point for scoops and for a parent's own account. Facebook is an amplifier and organizing layer: Media Matters counted "at least 860 right-wing parents and school-related groups" with at least 717,000 members in November 2021, but its topic counts were masks, critical race theory and vaccines and showed no gender-identity example, so this is evidence of infrastructure, not of gender content ([Media Matters](https://www.mediamatters.org/facebook/hundreds-facebook-groups-are-stoking-outrage-over-school-policies-masking-and-school)). Podcasts carried three of the steps traced here: Rogan promoted Libs of TikTok in August 2021, repeated the litter-box rumor in October 2022 and hosted Trump's repetition in October 2024. Rumble, Telegram, Truth Social, Gab and Gettr are documented as distribution endpoints, not origins. Christian broadcasting and church networks carry the family documentary "Art Club", which CBN said "is spreading to churches, voter forums, media, and online" ([CBN](https://cbn.com/news/us/art-club-parents-share-horror-story-after-school-staff-tries-turn-daughter-be-transgender)). No documentation specific to this narrative was found for Nextdoor or Instagram.

Three platform changes are documented. X removed the sentence "This includes targeted misgendering or deadnaming of transgender individuals" from its hateful-conduct policy on April 8, 2023 ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/twitter-quietly-reversed-its-policies-to-allow-for-intentional-deadnaming-and-misgendering-181754382.html)). A randomized field experiment with 4,965 active U.S. X users over seven weeks in summer 2023 found the algorithmic feed showed more conservative posts and fewer news-organization posts than the chronological feed and shifted attitudes by about 0.08–0.12 standard deviations; it covers one platform and one summer, the effects are small, and it was read through a secondary report ([PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/xs-feed-algorithm-shifts-users-political-opinions-to-the-right-new-study-finds/)). Meta announced on January 7, 2025 that it was ending third-party fact-checking, the program through which PolitiFact's SB 5599 check originated ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/07/meta-drops-fact-checking-and-loosens-its-content-moderation-rules)). Evidence that monetization drives this content specifically is thin: the only dollar figures concern total creator payouts and a watchdog's projection of platform ad revenue from reinstated accounts.

### Institutional channels, and syndication

Beyond social media the narrative moves through conferences (the claim's national debut was at a summit of about 600 attendees where transgender students were "the main focus of the messaging"), school-board meetings, congressional hearings and floor votes, executive orders, official press releases, amicus briefs (about 55 in the Washington case, through which family accounts such as Abigail Martinez's travel), television ads and documentaries ([Hechinger Report](https://hechingerreport.org/moms-for-liberty-national-summit-anti-trans-issues/)). Press-release journalism multiplies single-source items. Of "over 700" right-wing website articles mentioning Libs of TikTok in the first nine months of 2023, Sinclair's The National Desk and its local station sites accounted for 35%, by a progressive watchdog's count; and one story on a poll sponsored by Parents Defending Education appeared under identical headlines on at least ten local station sites, reporting the sponsor's numbers without field dates or margin of error ([KUTV](https://kutv.com/news/nation-world/most-us-voters-support-parental-consent-in-school-gender-identity-policies-study-shows-parents-defending-education-pde-crc-research-parent-rights)). Local-news supply is thin: 213 counties have no local news source and 1,524 have one ([Medill](https://www.medill.northwestern.edu/news/2025/news-deserts-hit-new-high-and-50-million-have-limited-access-to-local-news-study-finds.html)).

### What research shows about corrections

Corrections travel through fact-checkers, wire services, mainstream and nonprofit education outlets, district statements and advocacy organizations, typically days after the claim. The available research suggests they largely reach different audiences, though direct evidence for this narrative is absent and the conclusion rests on general studies. In web-traffic data from the 2016 campaign, visits to untrustworthy sites were concentrated among "a subset of Americans with strong preferences for pro-attitudinal information" ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32123342/)); the statement that fact-checks "almost never" reached those readers comes from a press summary, not the paper. A study of 237,180 noted cascades on X found a displayed Community Note reduces subsequent spread by 61.2%, but because notes "often appear too late" the system-wide reduction is 14.9%, and the effect is "significantly weaker for posts from influential accounts and political content" ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42086582/)). False news was about 70% more likely to be retweeted than true news in a 2018 study of 126,000 cascades ([MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2018/study-twitter-false-news-travels-faster-true-stories-0308)). Corrections do reach some speakers, since the litter-box retractions occurred, but they do not stop reuse by others. Two responses on the correcting side may reduce its reach further (union advice to lock accounts and avoid media; migration to Bluesky and Threads); that is an inference, and no study of either effect was found.

### Feedback loops

Attention changed policy and behavior in documented ways. Spreckels changed its club rules; Chino Valley's policy was copied by five districts; Orange Unified adopted a policy, saw two sponsoring trustees recalled and rescinded it. Oakland cancelled a day of school for 570 students after a bomb threat. Florida's 2022 law was signed with the governor repeatedly invoking one family's story. Litter-box bills were filed after a hoax. Federal investigations were announced with no findings or resolutions reported at the catalog's cut-off and then became news themselves; complaints against three districts were filed two days before their superintendents testified at a House hearing where Chicago's schools chief said "We do not hide information from parents." ([The 74/Chalkbeat](https://www.the74million.org/article/superintendents-defend-transgender-student-parental-rights-policies-on-capitol-hill/)). One attempted route stalled: the California initiative did not qualify.

### What is not measured

No impressions, view counts or share counts were found for the Trump clip, the SB 5599 posts, the Musk AB 957 post or the Elk Grove items. No platform transparency data on specific posts was retrieved. No evidence specific to this narrative was found on Nextdoor, Instagram, YouTube recommendation, Telegram or Truth Social. No analysis exists of Community Notes on posts about schools and gender, and none of whether fact-checks of these claims reached the audiences that saw them. Advocacy-funded figures on both sides lack full published methods.

### Polling incentives

Polling (class (b), tabulated in section 3) gives politicians a real incentive on two narrow propositions: that parents should be told, and that minors' medical transition should be restricted. Support for "telling parents" ranges from about 40% with a neutral option, to 36% when the student says they feel unsafe, to 66–90% in advocacy-sponsored or forced-choice wordings with no safety condition (one poll's party breakdown reaches 94% among Republicans). Approval of Trump's handling of transgender issues (52%, May 2025) ran above his overall approval (41%). There is no measured incentive on the strong claim, because no poll found tests it (e). Ballot tests cut the other way: Washington's parental-notice initiative failed on November 3, 2026 with about 52.5% voting no per press reports, and the California initiatives did not qualify (LEG-031, LEG-033).

Effectiveness evidence is mixed and mostly partisan-sourced. A Democratic polling project found the statement that Harris "is focused more on cultural issues like transgender issues rather than helping the middle class" was the top reason swing voters who chose Trump gave for not choosing her ((b); [Blueprint](https://blueprint2024.com/polling/why-trump-reasons-11-8/)). A pro-Harris super PAC reportedly found one ad shifted the race 2.7 points, with no published method; an advocacy-released randomized test found no significant vote effect (both from search summaries). Third Way noted that the three 2022 gubernatorial candidates who made such ads central "lost by healthy margins" ((d)). After the November 3, 2026 midterms, in which Democrats are reported to have won both chambers of Congress and the two most heavily targeted Senate races, Gov. DeSantis called the result an "extinction-level event" ((c); [Just the News](https://justthenews.com/nation/states/desantis-claims-midterms-were-close-extinction-level-event-republican-party)). That election outcome rests on two post-election articles and search summaries; certified results and seat counts were not retrieved, and the claim that the ads cost Republicans the election is inference.

Counsel are concentrated ((b)): of 17 litigated non-California school cases in the catalog, the Child & Parental Rights Campaign appears in six, ADF in five and America First Legal in two, so the collection "reflects where those organizations chose to sue". Free and confidential," beside "Donate Now" ((a); [CPRC](https://childparentrights.org/contact/)). ### Do legal documents and political speech distinguish social transition from medical treatment?

**They do, and the difference tracks the kind of document.** The legal organizations and the enforcement documents keep the claims apart. ADF's 2025 piece alleges secret social transition only. The Education Department's March 2025 letter concedes FERPA imposes no affirmative duty to inform, and Education Week's survey of the federal investigations "does not describe medical treatment in any of the cases". EO 14190 defines "social transition" to exclude "chemical or surgical mutilation". The two claims are fused in political communication: the same order's purpose clause, Project 2025's "social or medical gender transition without parental consent", campaign ads, and the President's rally statements, for which his campaign and White House twice offered notification policies and social-transition cases as the support. Polls keep them apart as well; only one offered "without parental consent" as an option for minors' treatment, and 16% of Democrats chose it.

Advocates do state a bridge between the two. Waggoner argues social transition "is a form of medical treatment" and that "desistance rates collapse in the face of social transition" (no study named), and the Cass Review is cited for an association between early social transition and proceeding to a medical pathway (from a search summary; the report was not opened). The Kettle Moraine court adopted the "medical treatment" characterization and the First Circuit rejected it in *Foote*. What the record shows about the bridge is narrower: in the catalog's cases any medication was prescribed by clinicians under parental consent, a minor-consent statute or a court order, and a state expert testified that "in order to get to gender clinic, you have to be out to at least one caregiver". One group moved directly from a notification ruling to a medical demand: responding to *Mirabelli*, Schilling called for "nationwide bans on the poison of child sex changes".

Strategists' own tests point the same way. APP's 2019 polling compared bathrooms with sports, and its 2026 memo lists sports and spaces, tax-funded "sex changes", minors' transitions and a violence message; school secrecy is not among the tested messages in either. That suggests the persuasive weight sits with sports and minors' medical care, and that school secrecy functions more as the litigation and governing agenda than as the tested ad message. This is an inference from what the documents list, not a statement by APP (e).

### Hypotheses that remain undocumented

Four hypotheses are plausible and should be labelled as hypotheses (e). That donors select for this messaging: no document found shows a donor directing it. That fundraising rewards escalation from "not told" to "medicated": no fundraising email or donation page built on the school-medication claim was retrieved, and no organization reports revenue by issue. That impressions-based payouts rewarded the most extreme version: X paid posters by impressions from July 2023 until it ended the program on September 7, 2026 ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/09/13/x-creator-payouts-murray-hill-guy-ragebait/)), but no account-level earnings tied to this content were found, and Libs of TikTok's operator declined to say whether she had received a payout (search summary). That foreign operations amplified the school narrative: one reported Russian operation used transgender topics including "gender transition and reassignment surgeries", but the reporting does not mention schools, children or parents, and no finding by Microsoft, Meta, the intelligence community or academic observatories specific to this narrative was located in one extended search ((c); [VOA](https://www.voanews.com/a/new-evidence-china-russia-and-iran-targeting-us-elections/7836957.html)). A fifth, that the strong claim persists because the weak claim is true often enough to be cited when challenged, is consistent with the two documented substitutions, but no strategist has said so (e).

### What holds up

The best-supported statements on this side are the narrow ones. Schools do not arrange medication or surgery, on everything the catalog found. Surgery on minors is rare. Texas did open child-abuse investigations of parents and ran them under special rules, though no child was removed. Threats did follow viral posts about schools, libraries and hospitals. The weakest statements are universal negatives and forecasts. That pattern matches the other side, where the best-supported incidents are nondisclosure and continuation over objection and the weakest claim is school-arranged medical treatment. On suicide and self-harm specifically, the people who run the underlying surveys state limits that political users of the numbers drop: the surveys are cross-sectional, recruited through social-media ads, and measure self-reported ideation and attempts; "not affirming" is a respondent's rating of a space, not a measure of abuse; and the "one accepting adult" statistic (40% lower odds of a reported attempt) counts parents among the accepting adults and does not compare disclosure with nondisclosure ([Trevor Project brief](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/research-briefs/accepting-adults-reduce-suicide-attempts-among-lgbtq-youth/)).

### The messaging guides in their own words

The published guides are candid about strategy. The Movement Advancement Project's 2025 guide, with seven national partners, tells advocates that "Decisions about this care ... should rest with families, doctors, and the patient—not politicians"; to "Leave in-depth medical conversations to physicians and providers" and, if pressed, to say "I'm not a doctor, but what I know is that the government should not be overruling parents"; and to "Avoid sweeping or categorical statements about complex aspects of transgender youth medical care", because "citing specific ages or including words like 'always' and 'never' in your messages makes them easy for anti-LGBTQ opponents to attack". Its stock answer remains "Decades of peer-reviewed research show that it is safe, effective, and essential" ([MAP guide](https://mapresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/talking-about-transgender-youth-health-care-1.pdf)).

A 2021–2022 guide by a communications firm, the Transgender Law Center and a polling firm tells advocates not to rebut: "advocates have an unfortunate habit of repeating (and retweeting) their offensive claims with the simple addition of a 'not' ... these refutations reinforce the opposition's narrative". It says to "Name the villains who violate our values, expose their motivation of getting back into or holding onto power", using "Certain politicians..." It advises against leading with harm, because doing so "decreases our base and even activists' trust in transgender young people to know what is best for their own health and well-being". It names "parental rights" as an attack to withstand and is frank about weakness: on sports, "Our base and persuadables want to support transgender student-athletes, but are extremely susceptible to our opposition's argument" ([guide, NEA-hosted copy](https://www.nea.org/sites/default/files/2025-03/advancing-lgbtq-inclusion-and-belonging-handout-2-rcgn-messaging-guide-trans-youth-the-freedom-to-be-ourselves-handout-1.pdf)). A separate family-acceptance guide treats parents as the persuadable audience and the family as the protective setting, which sits uneasily with "forced outing" messaging that presents parents as the risk ([MAP family guide](https://www.mapresearch.org/file/talking-about-family-acceptance-transgender-youth.pdf)). The ACLU's 2022 guide leads with harm and suicide, the approach the second guide advises against.

Guidance and practice diverge. The instruction to avoid "always" and "never" is the nearest thing to an internal correction of the absolute claims, yet a public advocacy page updated a year later still says "fully reversible". The instruction not to repeat opposition claims, even to deny them, is a plausible reason this side rarely engages documented nondisclosure cases on the facts and reframes them as privacy; no source states that link, and it is inference. The guides give persuasion effectiveness, not evidentiary weakness, as their reason for staying out of medical detail.

### Where corrections appear on this side, and where claims persist

## Conclusion

The two narratives are not symmetrical in content, but they are symmetrical in structure. Each has a documented core that its opponents tend to deny and an undocumented extension that its own amplifiers tend to add. For one side the core is written nondisclosure policy and a handful of record-backed cases of continuation over a parent's objection, and the extension is medicine and seizure. For the other the core is that schools do not medicate, surgery on minors is rare and families were investigated and threatened, and the extension is that nothing is withheld, the science is settled and notification will produce the worst outcomes. In both, accuracy tracks accountability: text written for a court, an administrative record or a school administrator is narrower than text written for a rally, a headline or a donor page. That is why the fact-check record has accumulated for four years without changing the strong claim. When evidence is requested, the claimant supplies the core and keeps the extension.

Three things would change this picture, and none of them is another lawsuit about names and pronouns. The first is measurement: a single poll on belief in the strong claim and ad-content coding would show whether the strong version is widely held or merely loud. The second is the small set of non-public records on which the medical question actually turns. The third is the calendar: the Supreme Court argument on Washington's runaway-youth law on December 7, 2026, the Ninth Circuit's merits ruling in *Mirabelli*, the Elk Grove minutes expected after December 15, 2026 and the trials set for March 2027 will each generate a new round of both narratives, and each can be checked against the category it actually concerns before the headline arrives.
